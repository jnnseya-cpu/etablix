**Twelve threads. Zero pounds of revenue.**

## The thing they now have in common

Read the blockers rather than the names and they collapse into one item.

RM6242 needs one completed contract example. CHIC was returned for insufficient
evidence of delivery capability and invited referees it will telephone. The
Catapult refused us because nothing has been piloted with a customer. Feltham
wants a trade record. Every live thread is asking the same question in a
different form: **has anyone paid you to do this, and will they say so?**

**It is not twelve problems. It is one problem, twelve times.** And it is not a
documentation problem — the documents are built, and the GCA assessment proves
they work: the management accounts, the statement of financial position and the
cash flow were accepted on their own merits under Question 128, with no
guarantor.

## What the GCA confirmed, and why it ends the argument

Asked directly whether a completed fixed-scope advisory appointment — a written
report on site establishment requirements and costs for one site, delivered end
to end and accepted by the client — would satisfy the contract example
requirement, the assessment team replied that it **"fully satisfies the
requirement."** No minimum value. No maximum value. One example for a single
service heading.

So the entry ticket to a four-year government framework is **one paid
diagnostic, of any value, with a signed Attachment 1a.**

That is the cheapest unlock available anywhere in this business, and it is the
same unit of work the Facebook ads pack independently concluded was the only
thing sellable cold. Two unrelated lines of analysis arrived at the same answer
in the same week.

## So the instinct that this is becoming a distraction is half right

**Right about the pattern.** Twelve threads and no revenue is not a pipeline, it
is an administrative habit. Each application feels like progress because it
produces an artefact and a reply. None of them can convert, because every one of
them terminates at the same locked door.

**Wrong about this email.** It is the opposite of a distraction: it is the one
reply that identified the key and described its shape precisely. Treating it as
noise because it arrived in the same inbox as eleven dead ends would be the
expensive mistake.

**The resolution is the same either way, which is how you know it is right:**

> **Stop applying. Sell one diagnostic.**

## What that means in practice

1. **Reply to the GCA once, briefly, and close the thread.** They have answered
   everything. Another round of questions is the distraction. Ask for the
   Attachment 1a template, say we will return with the completed example, stop.
2. **Do nothing further on any DPS** that is blocked on a contract example. The
   work is done; the door is locked from the other side.
3. **Finish only what is blocked on something buyable.** Cyber Essentials and
   insurance are purchases with lead times, not customers. They unlock RM6264
   and they are prerequisites for appointment on RM6242 anyway. Buy them.
4. **Put the whole remaining effort into one paying client**, and scope the
   first engagement so that the Attachment 1a signature is in the engagement
   letter rather than requested afterwards. A client asked to sign a
   confirmation of works at the point of scoping signs it as a formality. The
   same client asked six weeks after the final invoice has to be persuaded.
5. **Select one service heading on RM6242**, not several. Two headings require
   two examples, doubling a bottleneck that currently stands at zero.

## The one measure that matters now

Not applications submitted. Not registrations held. **One signed Attachment 1a.**

Everything in the procurement pipeline converts the day it exists, and nothing
in it converts before.
