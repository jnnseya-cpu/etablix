# The pre-construction site assessment — the front half of the diagnostic

## What this is, and what it is not

`backend/lib/pipelines/site-requirements.js` produces the **output**: twelve
sections running from package structure and Employer's Requirements through the
interface matrix, welfare, HSEQ and CDM, the evaluation model and the contract
strategy. That is the tender package a client can issue.

This document is the **input** — how you establish what the site actually
demands before any of that is written. Until now that step has been
undocumented, which means the quality of a requirements package has depended
entirely on what a client happened to mention.

The two are halves of one service and must not be treated as rival
methodologies. If they ever disagree, this one describes what to find out and
`SECTIONS` describes what to write.

## Why it matters commercially

`business/strategy/the-bottleneck.md` records that every live procurement
thread asks the same question in a different form: *has anyone paid you to do
this, and will they say so?* It also records the answer a government framework
assessment team gave in writing — that one completed fixed-scope advisory
appointment, **a written report on site establishment requirements and costs
for a single site, delivered end to end and accepted by the client**, fully
satisfies their contract-example requirement. No minimum value.

That report is precisely what this method produces. It is the cheapest unlock
available anywhere in this business, and it has not had a written method until
now.

## Before any of this is used with a client

**Check the regulatory references against current guidance.** The method cites
HSE planning guidance, HSE guidance on underground services, the Environment
Agency's Land Contamination Risk Management approach, the distinction between
an asbestos management survey and a refurbishment or demolition survey, HSE
expectations on temporary works, and the CDM 2015 requirement for a
construction phase plan before construction begins. Those are the right
authorities for the points made. Verify the current form of each before any of
it reaches a client document, because guidance is reissued and a confident
citation of a withdrawn document is worse than no citation.

**It is Great Britain, with England-specific environmental guidance.** The
engineering holds more widely; permissions and statutory duties need local
verification. Say so in any deliverable rather than letting a reader assume
otherwise.

**It mentions CDM without claiming a role, and it must stay that way.** The
construction phase plan is referenced as something that must exist before
construction begins. Nothing in this method appoints ETABLIX as Principal
Contractor or Principal Designer, and nothing drawn from it may imply either.
Most of the targets for this service hold both roles themselves.

**It is a method, not an offer.** A client buys an outcome, a date and a price.
Turning this into something sellable needs a scope of service on top: what is
delivered, over how many days, for a fixed fee, with what the client must
provide. That wrapper does not exist yet and is the next piece of work.

## 1 · Three site types, and the assumption each one invites

A live operational site is often also brownfield; the categories overlap. What
matters is the constraint each introduces.

| Site type | Principal challenge | The dangerous assumption | What the visit is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Greenfield** | Creating access, working ground, drainage and utilities where little exists | "It is an empty field, so mobilisation will be straightforward" | Whether the land can support construction, and what enabling infrastructure is needed |
| **Brownfield** | Discovering and managing previous development and land use | "The buildings are gone, therefore the site is clear" | Buried obstructions, contamination, redundant and live services, condition of retained assets |
| **Live operational** | Building while protecting an existing business, service or occupied facility | "Operations will accommodate construction when we arrive" | Separation, service continuity, access, shutdowns, interfaces, controlled handback |

Years of successful operation do not prove an existing structure or service can
accept new loads, modifications or interruptions. A greenfield appearance does
not establish clean ground or the absence of buried infrastructure.

## 2 · The desk study comes before the walkover

Walking a site without reviewing what is known produces observations without
context. Request, recording each document's revision, coverage and limitations:

project scope and concept layout · ownership, lease, boundary and access ·
planning permission and conditions · topographical and ground investigation
reports · utility records and previous detection surveys · historic maps,
previous uses and demolition records · existing drawings, calculations and the
health and safety file · asbestos information · drainage records and flood
information · operational procedures and maintenance records · ecology, trees,
archaeology and neighbouring assets.

Put anything unverified in an **information to confirm** category. An old
drawing must not silently become a construction assumption.

Then issue, before the visit, the list of decisions the visit exists to
support — for example: can the proposed access take the largest delivery; is
the compound location available throughout the programme; can the work proceed
without interrupting the existing supply; what investigations are needed before
foundation design is fixed; which permissions could control the start date.

## 3 · Who attends, and in what conditions

Construction lead, civil and structural designer, surveyor, client
representative — adding ground, environmental, logistics and temporary works
specialists by risk. For an operational site, add facilities and maintenance
staff, the operations manager, and whoever controls isolations and permits.
Those people know about undocumented modifications, recurrent flooding and
services that cannot be interrupted.

Four parts: opening briefing; external approach and perimeter; systematic
internal walkover; close-out with findings, uncertainties, owners and dates.

Visit in the conditions that reveal the problem — delivery peak, shift change,
production running. Revisit after rainfall or in darkness where drainage or
lighting matters.

**A walkover is not an intrusive investigation.** Opening chambers, entering
confined spaces, disturbing materials and exposing services each need their own
arrangements.

## 4 · Greenfield

**Access, from the public road to the workface — not to the gate.** Junction
geometry, visibility, waiting space; narrow lanes, bridges, culverts,
gradients, weak verges; overhead lines, trees, height restrictions, third-party
access; whether an artic can enter, turn, unload and leave; whether an
emergency vehicle reaches the workface in the wettest phase; conflicts with
farms, residents and public routes. *A transformer may fit through the gate and
still be impossible to manoeuvre into position.*

**Ground and earthworks.** Soft or waterlogged ground, rutting, standing water;
changes in vegetation or level; ditches, ponds, springs, embankments, historic
filling; slopes affecting excavation stability and plant movement; topsoil
storage. Investigate the ground under haul roads, compounds, crane pads and
piling platforms — *a ground investigation sufficient for foundations may say
nothing useful about a crane position eighty metres away.*

**Water.** Trace where it comes from, crosses and would go once vegetation is
stripped. Plan separately for clean upstream runoff, silty construction runoff,
excavation water, concrete washout and foul drainage. Do not assume a nearby
ditch is an authorised discharge point.

**Ecology and season.** Survey windows can determine programme feasibility, and
a missed window moves when a reliable assessment exists. Include the compound,
access road and temporary utility routes, not only the permanent works.

**Temporary services.** A pole or a main nearby is evidence of infrastructure
nearby. Confirm capacity, connection point, lead time, commercial terms, land
rights, water pressure and potability, foul options, communications resilience.

## 5 · Brownfield

The central question: what remains from previous uses, and how does it affect
the works?

| Inspect | Look for | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Old building footprints | Slab edges, plinths, patched surfaces, settlement | Compare historic layouts against new foundations and trenches |
| Below-ground structures | Pits, basements, tanks, ducts, redundant chambers | Locate, investigate, decide removal, filling or retention |
| Contamination indicators | Staining, odours, drums, unusual deposits, vegetation dieback | Competent assessment and targeted investigation |
| Existing hardstanding | Cracking, differential settlement, unknown construction | Assess against actual plant and imposed loads |
| Services | Unlabelled chambers, abandoned cables, undocumented connections | Records, detection, tracing, controlled verification |
| Retained structures | Corrosion, cracking, distortion, previous alteration | Structural appraisal and opening-up |
| Previous remediation | Caps, membranes, monitoring wells, treatment areas | Verification records and restrictions on disturbance |

**"Redundant" does not mean isolated.** A cable serving a demolished building
may still be live upstream. A drain under a cleared plot may serve another
property. Utility plans indicate location; they do not prove position.

For contamination the model is **source → pathway → receptor**. Appearance
alone cannot establish whether material is safe to reuse or dispose of.

For asbestos, check whether the information covers the actual intrusive work
area. A management survey is for normal occupation; refurbishment or demolition
may need destructive investigation. Inaccessible areas remain unresolved, and
must be recorded as such.

And check the sequence: removing a slab, wall or frame may remove support or
restraint that something retained still needs.

## 6 · Live operational

**Trace each critical system**: what it supplies, who owns and controls it,
normal and standby arrangements, isolation points and the verification needed,
consequence of interruption, maximum tolerable downtime agreed by the operator,
restoration and acceptance. Electricity, water, drainage, fire, communications,
controls, ventilation, process.

Do not stop at the work area. *Excavating outside a facility can interrupt a
fibre connection controlling equipment inside it.*

**Observe the operation.** Walk the routes used by staff, the public,
production deliveries, forklifts, maintenance and emergency responders.
Identify servicing space and emergency access that must stay available. An
apparently unused yard may be the only place for an emergency generator.

**Agree the interface plan before the affected work**: construction boundary
and crossings; shared roads, priority and delivery windows; live services and
isolation authority; fire protection impairment and reinstatement; dust, noise
and vibration limits with stop triggers; shutdown window, prerequisites and
recovery; security and escorts; testing, acceptance and formal handback.

A permit identifies task, location, validity and precautions. It supports the
risk assessment; it does not replace it.

**Plan the tie-in and the rollback together.** Verified existing
configuration; approved method and isolation plan; materials, tools and
competent people ready; defined tests before reconnection; the decision point
beyond which further work jeopardises restoration; a practicable rollback; a
named operational acceptance authority.

*A six-hour shutdown is not six hours of installation.* Isolation,
verification, testing and restoration consume part of the window.

## 7 · Recording it so it is usable

A folder of photographs is evidence, not an assessment. Each observation
carries: unique reference · exact location, marked on a drawing · the
observation · the evidence · **information status** · potential consequence ·
required action · owner · required-by date · closure evidence.

Information status is the part that does the work:

**Observed** (visible on the visit) · **Measured** (value obtained, method
recorded) · **Reported** (supplied by someone else) · **Verified** (checked
against suitable evidence) · **Unknown** (unresolved).

Photographs record location, direction, date and subject, linked to the
marked-up plan. Take a baseline condition record of roads, boundaries,
neighbouring assets and retained structures where damage could later be
disputed. Record inaccessible areas explicitly.

> "Ponding observed along the proposed compound's western edge. Cause and
> seasonal extent unknown. Civil designer to assess drainage and platform
> levels before compound design release."

against

> "Site wet — allow drainage."

The second cannot be designed, programmed or priced.

## 8 · Buy investigations to answer decisions

Commission against the decision, not as a standard package: topographical
survey → levels and layout; ground investigation → foundations, earthworks,
temporary platforms; utility detection → excavation routes and protection;
drainage CCTV → connectivity and retention; structural survey and opening-up →
capacity and alteration sequence; asbestos refurbishment or demolition survey →
safe scope of intrusive work; contamination investigation → remediation and
materials strategy; ecology and arboriculture → boundaries and timing;
swept-path assessment → access geometry; baseline monitoring → existing
movement, noise or vibration.

Every scope states coverage, accuracy, deliverables, exclusions and intended
use. **A survey excluding the proposed access or compound leaves the
mobilisation design unresolved** — and that is the exclusion most often missed.

## 9 · Design the site as an engineered system

Drawing cabins in a convenient corner is not site establishment design. Start
from the permanent works footprint and construction sequence, land available by
phase, peak workforce and shift overlap, largest delivery and maximum plant
loads, crane positions and operating envelopes, storage and delivery frequency,
temporary utility demand, protected areas and operational exclusions, emergency
access and escape.

Then: **logistics and circulation** — access, turning, unloading, pedestrian
separation, delivery booking, and how material travels *after* unloading;
**compounds and welfare** — sized for actual occupancy, with walking distance,
servicing, cleaning, sewage removal and seasonal conditions considered, and
available from the start; **temporary utilities** — connected load against
realistic coincident demand, starting currents, protection and earthing, water
flow and storage, foul capacity, lighting, communications, isolation;
**temporary works** — haul roads, crane and piling platforms, excavation
support, propping, hoardings, scaffolds, cabin foundations, each with design
responsibility, loading assumptions, checking, inspection, release and removal
sequence; **environmental and emergency** — drainage controls, fuel and
chemical storage, waste segregation, washout, spill response, fire access,
muster points, rescue access.

Near overhead lines, get the asset owner's input and clearances. Do not take a
clearance from memory.

## 10 · One layout per phase

| Phase | What the layout must achieve |
|---|---|
| Enabling | Safe access, initial welfare, segregation, environmental controls |
| Earthworks and substructure | Haul routes, excavations, spoil, piling areas |
| Main construction | Lifting, unloading, laydown, workforce accommodation |
| Fit-out and commissioning | Controlled access, protected completed areas, energised boundaries |
| Handover and demobilisation | Operational access, removal routes, reinstatement |

Check every temporary installation against future works. A compound over a
future drainage corridor creates a second mobilisation nobody priced. And
verify the crane that installs the cabins can return to remove them.

## 11 · Programme and estimate from the constraints

Investigations, approvals and enabling works are real activities with
dependencies:

> ground investigation → interpretation → platform design → procurement →
> construction → inspection and release → crane mobilisation

> existing-system verification → tie-in design → operational approval → outage
> booking → readiness review → isolation → installation → testing → handback

The estimate separates establishment, time-related running costs, production
constraints (restricted hours, escorts, small deliveries, limited workfronts),
investigations, defined risk allowances, and demobilisation.

Tie every uncertainty to a stated assumption and a named owner. "Ground risk
included" is not a commercial position. State the ground model assumed, what
remains unknown, and how the contract allocates it.

## 12 · Readiness gates

Separate releases for enabling works, main construction and high-risk
operational interfaces. Before releasing one: the area is available and legally
accessible; permissions and conditions are satisfied; information is adequate
for *that* activity; design and temporary works are sufficiently developed;
services and operational interfaces are controlled; access, welfare and
emergency arrangements are in place; competent people and equipment are
available; outstanding risks have named owners and acceptable controls.

## 13 · What the assessment delivers

1. Site constraints drawing — boundaries, services, exclusions, interfaces
2. Photographic and baseline condition record
3. Findings and action register, with owners and deadlines
4. Survey and investigation schedule against specific uncertainties
5. Buildability and construction sequence review
6. Phased site establishment and logistics drawings
7. Temporary works register and design briefs
8. Temporary utilities demand and connection plan
9. Operational interface and shutdown plan, where applicable
10. Updated programme, estimate and mobilisation readiness record

**That list is the product.** It converts a client's request for cabins, roads
and utilities into a defined, phased operating requirement with quantities,
capacities, responsibilities and removal obligations that can be priced and
delivered — and it is the evidence an assessment team said would satisfy a
contract-example requirement.
