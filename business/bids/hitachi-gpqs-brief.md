# GPQS — what Gary was pointing at

**GPQS is Grid & Power Quality Solutions**, a Hitachi Energy business unit
inside their Grid Integration business. In the UK it also appears written as
**GPQSS — Grid Power Quality Solutions & Services**, and internally the function
is paired with HVDC as **"HVDC & GPQS UK&IE"**.

Gary named two programmes. The second one is the more interesting of the pair,
and it is the one that fits this company better.

## What GPQS actually builds

Reactive power and grid stability equipment, and the substation compounds that
house it:

- **STATCOM** and **SVC Light** — static synchronous compensators
- **Static var compensators (SVC)** and synchronous condensers
- **Harmonic filters**, capacitor banks, metal-enclosed capacitor banks
- **Phase-shifting and special transformers**
- The **MACH** control and protection system that runs them

Named UK work in the public record includes SP Energy Networks' **Eccles**
substation — two sets of SVC Light STATCOM plus synchronous condenser under
central MACH control — and earlier relocatable STATCOM-based SVC projects on the
National Grid system.

## Why GPQS is the better fit than HVDC, not the lesser one

This is the part worth understanding before the next conversation.

**HVDC is a handful of enormous sites.** A converter station is a multi-year
build of very large scale — Eastern Green Link 2, the Scotland and Shetland
links. Few projects, each vast, each with a bespoke site strategy and a main
works contractor already appointed.

**GPQS is a portfolio of many smaller compounds, built in sequence.** STATCOMs,
SVCs and synchronous condensers go in across the network wherever stability and
reactive power are needed — often on constrained, rural or bolt-on sites next to
existing substations. Each one needs access, a compound, temporary power and
water, welfare, security and logistics before anything of value happens. Then
the next one does too.

**That is the repeatability problem, and it is the one on your record.** Thirty
or more new-build generation and storage sites at UK Power Reserve, built one
after another, each set up close to fresh. A programme of GPQS installations has
the same shape, and it is the case where one accountable site-services package
is easiest to justify commercially — because the saving is not on one site, it
is on the fifteenth site being the same as the first.

On an HVDC converter station you are one supplier among many on a site somebody
else already controls. On a GPQS programme you could own a repeatable package
across a portfolio. Lead with GPQS when the conversation gets to what you would
actually do.

## Two facts worth having in your head

**Hitachi Energy's UK main office is Alpha Tower, Birmingham** — they moved it
there in 2024, and it holds over 240 people including leadership and commercial
teams. The HVDC & GPQS UK&IE function is run from there. **You are in Birmingham
B44.** That is a real fact, not a sales line, and it is the only reason a
face-to-face meeting is a plausible ask rather than an imposition. Jaime is
probably there.

**Stone, in Staffordshire, is the engineering and delivery base** — around 400
staff on grid integration, engineering and project delivery. If registration
leads to a technical conversation rather than a commercial one, that is likely
where it goes.

## What to do with this — and what not to do

**Do not put any of it in the registration email.** That email's job is to get
a registration route, and a paragraph demonstrating research turns an
administrative request into a pitch. Keep it exactly as drafted.

**Use it in the conversation that follows.** When somebody asks what you would
do for them, the answer is: a repeatable site-establishment package across a
GPQS programme, priced once and reused, rather than assembled from scratch at
each installation. That sentence lands because it is true of their business and
because it is the thing you have actually done.

**Do not claim HVDC experience.** You have not delivered a converter station.
The site-establishment observation is true of large civils generally, which is
why it works — not because you have built one of these.

**Ask, do not assert.** "Is the site setup on a GPQS programme assembled per
installation, or is there a standard package that travels?" is a question a
practitioner asks and a stranger cannot. It is also the question whose answer
tells you whether there is a business here.
