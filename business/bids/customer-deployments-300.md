## Why it is written this way

**"What it showed" is the move that makes these usable.** An assessor cannot
score somebody else's project as your deployment. They can score an
observation that explains where your mechanism came from, and that is what each
entry ends on. It converts a CV into evidence for the solution.

**UK Power Reserve goes first deliberately.** It is the repeatability story —
thirty-plus near-identical sites, each set up afresh — and repeatability across
a portfolio is exactly what infrastructure productivity means. Skye is the
bigger project; UKPR is the more relevant one.

**Employers are named, nothing else is.** Naming a former employer is ordinary
CV practice. End clients, contract values, commercial terms and supplier names
are not, and none appear. The Scottish project is located by region rather than
by site, which is the line between describing your record and disclosing a
client's.

**The last paragraph refuses the percentages again.** Repeating that refusal
across both this field and the track record field is consistent rather than
repetitive, and consistency is what makes it read as a policy rather than an
excuse.

## Before you paste it

**Verify the UK Power Reserve figures.** 628MW and thirty-plus sites came from
my earlier notes rather than from you. Check them against your own record
before they go on a funded application, and cut them to what you can evidence
if they are not exact.

**Do not upgrade "the founder's" to "our".** One word turns an honest answer
into a false declaration, and it is the word an assessor cross-checks against
Companies House incorporation dates.

**Do not add a third example, a pilot, or a trial.** There is no pilot. The
specimen diagnostic is a fictional project and must not appear in a field
headed customer deployments.
