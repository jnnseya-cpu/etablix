# "What do you want from this programme" — 200 words

The weak answer to this field asks for growth support, mentoring and
introductions. Every applicant writes that, and none of it is specific to this
programme.

The strong answer names the one thing the programme can give that nothing else
can, shows the routes already tried so the ask is not lazy, and then states
success in terms somebody could check in March.

---

## The answer — 197 words

> **What we cannot get elsewhere: a buyer willing to release a pack.**
>
> We have used the routes open to us. Direct approaches to senior people have
> produced replies and useful correction, but no route to anyone who can
> actually engage a supplier. Public sector dynamic purchasing systems are in
> progress and slow by design. General business support is advice, not sector
> access.
>
> The barrier none of them clears is circular: we cannot demonstrate without a
> client's pre-construction information, and no client releases that to an
> unproven supplier without a relationship that itself requires a
> demonstration. A convenor the sector already trusts breaks that circle. That,
> and funding to have the findings independently verified rather than
> self-assessed, is the ask.
>
> **Success at end March 2027, stated so it can be checked.**
>
> Three retrospective runs completed with three separate infrastructure
> organisations, on their own packs.
>
> Findings independently verified, with the proportion confirmed published
> whatever it is.
>
> At least one paid engagement in ETABLIX's own name, or a documented reason
> there is none.
>
> Cyber Essentials in place.
>
> One reference the company can cite as its own.
>
> The last is the only one that matters. Everything else is how we get there.

---

## Why it works

**It names one ask, not a wish list.** A buyer willing to release a pack. That
is a thing the Catapult can actually do and almost nobody else can, and an
assessor reading it knows immediately whether they can help.

**The second paragraph earns the ask.** Listing what has already been tried —
and admitting the direct approaches produced correction rather than customers —
stops this reading as a company that wants introductions because it has not
bothered to look for its own. It also quietly demonstrates the market
engagement the earlier fields could not evidence.

**The circular barrier appears for the third time**, in the barriers answer, in
the standards answer and here. That is not repetition; it is one company giving
one consistent account of its position across a whole application, and it is
what separates a written application from an assembled one.

**Success is checkable in March.** Three runs, a verified proportion, a paid
engagement or a documented reason, a certification, a reference. Somebody could
hold you to every line, which is exactly why they are worth writing.

**"Or a documented reason there is none" is the line that earns trust.** It
admits the paid engagement might not happen and commits to explaining why
rather than quietly dropping it. Nobody else will write that, and it costs
nothing that a false promise would not cost more.

**The last two sentences say what the company actually lacks.** One reference
in its own name. An assessor who has read the track record and deployments
fields already knows it, and seeing the applicant know it too is worth more
than another paragraph of capability.

## Before you paste it

**Do not soften "no route to anyone who can actually engage a supplier".** It
is the sentence that makes the ask specific, and softening it makes the whole
answer generic.

**If you have a target date for Cyber Essentials, leave it out anyway.** The
line says "in place", which is the outcome. A date is a commitment somebody
checks, and this field is about March 2027 already.

**Do not name a target client.** The success measures say "three separate
infrastructure organisations" deliberately. Naming one you have not asked is
the single line here that could be checked and found wanting.
