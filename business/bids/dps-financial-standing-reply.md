## Notes before you send

**The one thing that decides this.** They have told you a guarantee is the
easiest route for them to evidence your financial capability. That makes
finding a guarantor the highest-value hour of work in this application — worth
more than any amount of polish on the forecast. A personal guarantee from
someone of sufficient standing counts, and so does a parent company: if
Groupe Nseya has the standing to give one, ask there first.

**Do not send a forecast without its assumptions.** A three-year forecast with
no stated basis reads as invention to an assessor, and assessors read a great
many of them. One line under each: what win rate, what average engagement
value, what payment terms, what cost base. A modest forecast you can defend
beats an ambitious one you cannot.

**Keep the contract examples specific.** Dates, your role, the scope inside
the contract, the interfaces you managed, and what the outcome was. An
assessor is testing whether the company can perform the works — the evidence
for that is the detail only somebody who was there could write.

**Say nothing you cannot evidence.** No turnover figures until they come from
the accounts, no accreditations that are pending, no guarantee until it is
signed. A single unsupportable claim in a financial-standing submission costs
more than every strong sentence around it earns.
