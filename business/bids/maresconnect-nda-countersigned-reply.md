## Check before you send

**Look at the date line on the countersigned copy.** The template arrived
showing 2025 and we corrected it to 2026 before signing. If their execution
reinstated 2025, the agreement is dated in a year that has passed, and that is
worth a separate one-line email offering a re-executed copy — not a line in this
one, which would read as checking their work.

**The signature block in the last email carried the company number nowhere and
named the products as an "AI Operating System".** Both are changed above. The
number belongs in any signature going to a procurement reader — it is the first
thing they check and the cheapest line in the letter. The product descriptors
are off this email entirely: a confidentiality-bound HVDC procurement is not the
place to advertise two unrelated platforms, and the standing rule is that the
technology is never raised in front of a customer. Use the block above for
anything that goes to MaresConnect from here.

**Clause 4.1.5 is live from today.** The agreement is executed, so we cannot
initiate contact with MaresConnect's contractors, suppliers, lenders or advisers
about this project without their prior written consent. The restriction is
limited to this transaction, so ordinary business development with the same
firms is unaffected — but if the signals queue surfaces a MaresConnect award,
the winner must not be written to about that scheme. Clause 4.2 permits it with
written consent, which is why the third question in the earlier email asked how
they would prefer us to engage; that question is now parked until we are inside
Aconex.

## What is not asked, and why

The supply-chain-register question and the engagement-route question were both
put on 21 October and neither has been answered. Asking all three again in a
reply to an administrator who has just done her part would read as a mailshot,
and it spends the goodwill of a cooperative exchange on nothing. They go into
the first clarification instead, where they are logged, answered on the record
and visible to whoever actually owns the packaging decision.

## What the PQQ will ask that we cannot yet answer

Worth knowing before access, because none of it improves by being discovered on
3 December:

- **No SSIP registration and no ISO certification are held.** Neither may be
  implied anywhere in the submission. Where a field is pass/fail on either, that
  is the whole bid/no-bid question and it is better found early.
- **No employers' liability**, correctly: the only employee is the director, who
  holds a majority of the issued share capital, so the company is exempt under
  the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969. Write the exemption
  out in full rather than leaving the field blank.
- **PI and PL are in force at £5,000,000 each** from 26 October 2026, policy
  CHDB5802551XB. Whether the limit is each-and-every-claim or aggregate is still
  open with Simply Business, and a 750MW interconnector PQQ is exactly the sort
  that asks.
- **No statutory accounts are filed.** The company has not reached its first
  accounting reference date. Say that, and offer the management information
  prepared from the company's own records.
- **The scope we would bid is the temporary site environment only.** The
  position in the last email was right and should be repeated in the PQQ
  verbatim: not the converter stations, not the Irish 400kV substation, not the
  cable systems. An EPC-shaped PQQ scored against an EPC-shaped respondent is a
  low score for the wrong reason.
