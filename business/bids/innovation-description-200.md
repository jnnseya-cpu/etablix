# "What is innovative about your solution?" — 200 words

For the application field asking about novel elements, intellectual property,
patents, proprietary methods, data assets and differentiation.

**It opens by saying there are no patents.** That is deliberate. A patent claim
is the single most checkable statement on any innovation application, and an
assessor who finds one overstated discards everything around it. Saying it in
the first line and then showing the differentiation is architectural reads as
confidence. Burying it reads as hoping nobody looks.

**Every claim in it is true of the code as built**, not of the roadmap. The
sixteen engines exist and the registry reports sixteen.

---

## The answer — 200 words

> No patents are held or applied for. The differentiation is architectural and
> built.
>
> Most construction platforms are systems of record. AI entrants wrap a language
> model over documents. Both produce findings nobody can audit, because the model
> generates them.
>
> CONSTRUX inverts it. Sixteen deterministic engines run first and produce the
> findings — obligations, notices, time bars, change validity and programme
> logic. The model
> writes the report from them and cannot recompute them. Each agent's brief is
> generated from that registry, so no agent can describe a check its engine does
> not perform.
>
> Three further elements we believe uncommon:
>
> Engines refuse rather than warn. A claimed change on a barring contract with no
> notice is rejected, not flagged.
>
> Bitemporal state records what was known and when, not only what is true — the
> question that decides disputes.
>
> A clause graph holds the contract as obligations and time bars rather than
> searchable text; every output passes an adversarial challenger; and VERYX rolls
> the same records to portfolio level.
>
> Protectable assets: the engine rule base and clause-graph schema, held as
> copyright and trade secret, plus the ETABLIX, CONSTRUX and VERYX marks. The
> rule base is the durable asset, deepening with every contract read.

## Why each part is there

**"No patents" first.** See above. It also makes the rest of the answer
credible: a company that will say the uncomfortable thing in line one is a
company whose other claims can be taken at face value.

**The competitor sentence names categories, not products.** "Systems of record"
and "language model wrapped over documents" describe the market accurately
without asserting anything about a named product that a reader might know
better than you do. That is the version that cannot be caught out, and it still
shows you know the field.

**"Cannot recompute them" is the technical claim.** It is an architectural
constraint rather than a promise about model behaviour, which is what makes it
worth stating. Every applicant says their AI is reliable. Very few describe the
thing that stops it being wrong.

**The generated-brief point is the strongest sentence in the answer** and it is
easily skimmed past. An agent whose brief is generated from the engine registry
cannot claim a check that does not exist, because the description and the
executable are the same artefact. That is a structural anti-hallucination
property, not a mitigation.

**"We believe are uncommon" rather than "unique".** Unique is a claim about
every competitor in the world and it is unprovable. Uncommon is honest and
costs nothing in persuasive force.

## Before you paste it

**Check the count in the form.** It counts hyphens, dashes and em rules
differently from a word processor. If you need to cut further, shorten the
engine list — it is illustrative, and four examples carry the point as well as
six.

**If the form has a separate IP field**, move the last paragraph there and use
the recovered words to expand the bitemporal point, which is the one an
assessor from the infrastructure side will recognise fastest.

**Do not upgrade "no patents" to "patent pending" unless an application has
actually been filed.** It is checkable at the Intellectual Property Office in
under a minute, and it is the kind of thing an assessor checks precisely
because so many applicants overstate it.
