# CHIC has returned the application. What to resubmit.

**This is not a rejection.** CHIC have reviewed the submission, named exactly
two gaps, and written *"upon receipt we can progress the application."* That is
an invitation with a specification attached, from a named buyer eight miles from
your registered office.

It is also exactly what their own supplier guide warned about, and what the
register recorded before submission: Section 5 provides for a start-up with
limited trading results, and the guide says twice that **insufficient detail
means the application is declined and returned.** The statement was the whole
submission and it was too thin. Now it gets written properly.

---

## What they actually asked for, read closely

### Technical and Professional Ability

> "the level of detail submitted is insufficient to enable a comprehensive
> assessment… please provide further details of your organisation's relevant
> experience, expertise, and delivery capability. You may also submit client
> testimonials and/or references, including contact details for referees, who
> may be contacted as part of our verification process."

Two things in that. **Detail** is compulsory. **Referees are invited** — and
they will be contacted. That second part is the one that moves the application
from an argument into a verified record, which is precisely what this company
lacks.

### Economic and Financial standing

> "as you are a start-up company and a formal financial report is not yet
> available, could you provide **one or more** of the following… statement of
> turnover, profit and loss account, balance sheet, cash flow statement,
> guarantor statement (i.e. bank)."

**Read "one or more" twice.** The bar is one of five. A guarantor is the fifth
option and not a requirement — which retires the guarantee pressure that has
been hanging over the financial side of this application since the summer. You
do not need a bank guarantee to clear this section.

## Section 1 — Technical and Professional Ability

Give them four things, in this order, in the narrative and with the detail
attached under Supplementary Information.

### 1. The delivery record, in project detail rather than in summary

This is where the last submission was thin. Do not describe a career. Describe
projects, one at a time, with the facts only somebody who was there can supply.

**Project one — the turnkey programme.** 36 months. A 309-bed workforce village
inside the turnkey scope. Name the dates, your role, the scope that sat inside
the contract, the specific interfaces you personally managed, the subcontractors
you ran, and how it ended. Four to six sentences of genuine detail beats a page
of positioning.

**Project two — the 628MW portfolio.** More than thirty new-build generation and
storage sites across England and Wales, built in sequence. You ran risk and
change, then pre-construction. Say what a site setup involved each time, and say
that it was assembled close to fresh on each one — because that observation is
the origin of the company and it demonstrates you understand repeatability at
volume, which is what a DPS framework buys.

**Label both plainly as yours personally**, delivered under previous employers'
contracts. That is the sentence the assessor already accepted once; do not blur
it now.

### 2. Expertise, mapped to the sub-lots you selected

Take the workstreams you actually ticked — Project Manager, Employer's Agent,
Clerk of Works, CDM Co-ordinator — and against each one write two or three
sentences on what you have personally done that evidences it. CHIC confirmed in
writing that contract examples must cover **every** workstream selected. If a
workstream cannot be evidenced, remove it from the selection now rather than let
it weaken the whole submission.

### 3. Delivery capability, stated honestly

One working director, and say so. Then say what carries it: the documented
method, the named associate arrangement, the written scope and checking regime,
and the capacity rule — that work which cannot be staffed is declined at
enquiry. The method statement already written covers all of this.

### 4. The policy set

Five signed and dated documents: health and safety, occupational health,
equality and diversity, environmental, and quality management arrangements.
These did not exist at first submission. They are now the strongest evidence of
capability you can attach, because each one names what is not yet in place —
and an assessor who reads that trusts the rest.

## Referees — the highest-value hour in this resubmission

They asked, they will call, and a verified referee converts your record from
assertion to fact. Aim for **three**, and get permission from every one before
the name goes anywhere.

### Who to ask

**People who watched you deliver.** Former managers and senior colleagues from
the turnkey work and the 628MW programme. A referee speaking personally, not on
behalf of their employer, is a far easier yes than a corporate reference — and
it is all CHIC asked for.

> **A referee request is not a sales approach.** This matters given the decision
> to leave the GE Vernova contacts alone until 2027. Asking somebody to confirm
> what you were like to work with is a personal favour that involves neither
> their employer's commercial position nor any business development. It is the
> one ask that does not breach the stand-down.
>
> **But the timing is now delicate.** If you have already sent the "last time
> from me, no reply needed" lines, asking for a referee immediately afterwards
> contradicts them. **If you have not sent them yet, send this instead** — a
> referee request is a far better use of a final contact than a graceful
> withdrawal, and it is more likely to be answered.

### The permission request

> Hi [name],
>
> A small favour, and you can say no without explaining.
>
> I am registering my company on a Birmingham housing consortium's framework and
> they have asked for referees they can call — somebody who can speak to what I
> was like to work with, personally rather than on behalf of [employer].
>
> Would you be willing to be named? It would be a five-minute call at most, and
> possibly none at all. Completely fine if not — I do not want to put you in an
> awkward position either way.
>
> [Your name]

"Personally rather than on behalf of your employer" removes the only real
objection. Expect a much higher hit rate than anything sent so far.

### What to submit for each

Name, job title, organisation, telephone, email, and one line on the
relationship — which project, what they saw. **Never submit a name you have not
asked.** A referee surprised by the call costs you the reference and the
credibility together.

## Section 2 — Economic and Financial standing

They asked for one or more of five. **Give three, and not the guarantor.**

| Document | Position |
|---|---|
| **Balance sheet** | Provide. As at a stated date, from the management accounts, however small the figures. |
| **Cash flow statement** | Provide. For the trading period to date. |
| **Forecast with assumptions** | Provide, alongside. Not on their list, which is why it is worth adding — it is the document that shows you can run a business rather than just report one. |
| Statement of turnover | Only if there is turnover to state. A nil or minimal figure stated plainly is better than the words avoided. |
| Profit and loss | Include if the accountant can produce one for the period. |
| **Guarantor statement** | **Do not pursue.** It is one option of five and you clear the section without it. Chasing a bank letter would delay a resubmission that can go this week. |

**The forecast is ready.** `node business/bids/build-forecast.cjs` produces the
workbook. Fill the shaded assumption cells and — this is the part that matters —
**fill the Basis column for every one**. An assessor knows a forecast from a
company with no trading history cannot be accurate. What they are testing is
whether it is reasoned.

**Leave the trough in.** If the model shows a month where cash runs short, show
it and say how it is funded. A stated, funded trough reads as competence. A flat
line reads as a model adjusted until it looked comfortable.

**Get the accountant to look at it before it goes.** This is the section where
a number that does not reconcile costs more than a modest one.

## The portal, and one process trap

**Resubmit on the CHIC eSourcing portal. Do not reply by email.** They said so
explicitly and a reply by email may not be recorded at all.

The route: **Participate → Register Interest → the relevant DPS should now say
"re participate" → the questionnaire reopens with your current answers in it.**

> **You must confirm every section again**, not only the two they asked about.
> The application has been returned in full, so an unconfirmed section elsewhere
> will hold the whole thing up a second time — and a second return on an
> administrative point reads very differently from the first.

Attach the project detail, the referees and the financial documents under
**Supplementary Information**, as they directed.

## What not to do

**Do not fabricate a testimonial.** They said references "may be contacted as
part of our verification process." A quote from somebody who did not give it is
discovered with one telephone call, and it ends more than this application.

**Do not present the GE Vernova or UK Power Reserve projects as the company's
own.** The assessor has already accepted your personal record described
honestly. Blurring it now, after they accepted it, is the one move that would
lose their goodwill.

**Do not chase a bank guarantee.** See above. It is not required and it would
delay you by weeks.

**Do not pad.** They said the detail was insufficient, not that the submission
was short. More words about positioning will fail the same test again. Specific
project facts, named referees and real financial documents are what "sufficient
detail" means here.

**Do not pay anything.** No fee to register, no fee to be listed.
