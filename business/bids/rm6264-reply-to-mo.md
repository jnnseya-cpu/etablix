## Why these two questions and no others

**The timescale is the only thing he skipped.** You asked for it in point 1
and the answer covered openness and eSourcing instead. Asking once, plainly,
is normal; not asking means you plan around a guess.

**The notification route is the question nobody asks and everybody regrets.**
He has told you customers can use their own portal and that shortlisted
suppliers are notified beforehand. If that notification is an email, it is
the single point of failure in the whole arrangement — and your domain's mail
authentication is exactly the kind of thing that quietly diverts one. Get the
address on the record confirmed now, while you have a named person answering
within a day.

**Do not re-pitch.** He is not the buyer and he is not evaluating you. He is
the person who tells you how the mechanism works, and he has done it well.
One line restating the narrow-admission position is enough, because it is the
thing you want on the record; anything more reads as a supplier who has not
listened.

## Two things to line up before you submit

- **The filters are the whole product.** Customers shortlist by Services,
  Location, Building Type and Annual Contract Value. A band you cannot
  service wastes an invitation; a band set too low keeps you out of the work
  you want. Decide the ACV band deliberately, and write down why, because you
  will be asked to justify it eventually.
- **Sub-service selection is a capability claim.** Every sub-service you tick
  is a statement that you can deliver it today. Tick what you can resource
  through your supply chain now, and add to it later — the filters are
  amendable at any time, which he has confirmed in writing in this thread.
  That email is worth keeping.
