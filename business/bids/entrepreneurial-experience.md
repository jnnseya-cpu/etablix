# "Entrepreneurial / scale-up experience" — Yes, with the split named

**The honest position is Yes to entrepreneurial and limited on scale-up.** This
is a scale-up programme, so they will probe it. Claiming prior scale-up
experience that does not exist is the claim most likely to unravel in a
conversation, and it would unravel in front of the people you most want to
trust you.

Separating the two, and then naming what is missing as a reason for applying,
is both honest and the strongest available answer.

---

## The answer — 108 words

> Yes on entrepreneurial. Limited on scale-up, and it is worth being exact
> about which.
>
> **Entrepreneurial:** founded the company and took it from nothing to an
> operating platform, two products, a published policy suite and live
> public-sector framework applications — alone.
>
> **Scale-up, in delivery rather than company-building:** ran pre-construction
> across a 628MW programme of more than thirty sites built in sequence, which is
> a repeatability problem at volume, and established a new subcontract
> capability inside a large EPC business.
>
> **Absent:** no prior venture through a funding round, and no experience of
> hiring and managing a growing team. That is a reason we are applying, not
> something to talk around.

---

## Why answer it this way

**"Limited on scale-up" is the sentence that protects everything else.** On a
programme built to teach scale-up, an applicant claiming to have done it
already is either overstating or in the wrong cohort. Saying it plainly means
nothing said afterwards has to be defended under questioning.

**The two kinds of scale-up experience are separated deliberately.** Running
thirty-plus sites in sequence is genuinely a repeatability problem at volume —
the same problem a scaling company faces, in a different medium. Claiming it as
delivery experience rather than as company-building experience is accurate, and
an assessor from infrastructure will recognise the distinction immediately.

**"Established a new subcontract capability inside a large EPC business" is the
intrapreneurial line.** Building something that did not exist, inside an
organisation that was not set up for it, is the closest thing to venture
experience most construction professionals have, and it is rarely claimed
because people do not think of it that way.

**The last line is the same move used across this application.** Name the gap,
then name it as the reason for applying. It appears in the track record answer,
the deployments answer and the support themes answer, and by this point it
reads as a consistent posture rather than a series of apologies.

## Before you paste it

**Confirm "alone" is accurate.** If anyone else contributed materially —
a co-founder, a developer, an adviser with equity — say so. It is checkable
against Companies House and against your own LinkedIn.

**Do not add a timeframe unless you are sure of it.** "Inside a year" and
similar are easy to say and easy to test against your incorporation date.

**If the form scores yes/no strictly and you are tempted to drop the
qualification** — do not. A bare Yes invites a question in interview that this
answer has already disarmed.
