# SQ question 6.1 — contract examples, and the explanation if you answer No

Two parts: a Yes/No, then a free-text box that only matters if you answer No.
The box says 500 words. The counter says 10,000 characters. **Obey the word
limit, not the counter** — 500 words is about 3,000 characters, so the counter
will still show thousands remaining when you are already at the limit. An
assessor who asked for 500 words and receives 1,500 reads it as an inability
to follow an instruction, which is the first thing this stage tests.

## Answering the Yes/No

The test is narrow and it is about the **company**, not about you.

Answer **Yes** only if JNN GLOBAL LTD is itself the named party on at least one
contract it has performed, and you can produce the client, the dates, the value
and a contact who will confirm it. Size does not matter and the sector does not
matter — the question allows public, private, voluntary, charity or social
enterprise, in any combination. One genuine engagement of the company's own is
enough to answer Yes.

Answer **No** if the only relevant delivery record is yours personally, from
before the company existed. That is almost certainly the position.

**Do not answer Yes and list the GE Vernova projects.** They were delivered
under GE Vernova's contracts, not the company's. The SQ carries a false
declaration warning and this is exactly what it is aimed at. Answering No costs
you nothing that answering Yes dishonestly would not cost you far more of: the
buyer has written this box precisely because they expect some applicants to be
new, and the guide expressly provides for a start-up with limited trading
results.

One warning from their own supplier guide: it says twice that **insufficient
detail means the application is declined and returned**. A two-line answer here
fails. This box is the whole submission.

## The explanation — draft

Fill the brackets. Delete nothing else. Check the word count before you paste.

**The word budget.** Everything below except the bracketed passages is about
300 words. That leaves you roughly 200 for the projects — enough for one told
properly and two named briefly, not three told properly. Spend the 200 on the
first one. If you overrun, cut the second and third projects before you cut
detail from the first: one project an assessor can picture beats three they
cannot.

> ETABLIX is a trading name of JNN GLOBAL LTD, incorporated in [month, year].
> The company is newly formed and has not yet performed a contract of the type
> this requirement describes, so I am answering No rather than submitting
> examples that belong to another organisation. The delivery record relevant to
> your assessment is mine personally, and I would rather set it out plainly
> than attach it to the company.

> visits and site reports behind it, and the management of the civils and
> site-services subcontractors who delivered it.

## Before you submit

**Name real projects or the paragraph is worthless.** The bracketed project
paragraph is the only part of this answer an assessor will weigh. Generic
description of a role reads as a job advert. A named site, a date range, a
scope boundary and a problem you personally resolved reads as a person who did
the work.

**Ask your referees first.** Naming someone who is then surprised by the call
costs you the reference and the credibility together.

**Say nothing you cannot evidence.** No accreditation that is pending as
though it is held, no insurance limit you have not checked on the schedule, no
turnover figure that does not come from the accounts.

**Select only what you can cover.** CHIC confirmed in writing that the
submission is assessed as a whole and that contract examples must cover every
workstream selected. Every extra workstream you tick widens what this one
paragraph has to carry. Tick the fewest that still make the application worth
making.
