## Why it is shaped this way

**The apology is one clause and then it is over.** The attachment was missing, she
had to ask, and a paragraph of contrition would cost more of her time than the
mistake did. "Apologies — the attachment did not go with the original. It is
attached now." Then straight to what is in it.

**The four points are in the email as well as in the attachment, deliberately.** A
category buyer processing market engagement responses may not open an attachment
from an unknown supplier on a first pass. If the email alone does not demonstrate
that this firm has thought about underground construction in her licence areas,
the attachment will not get opened at all. If it does, the attachment gets read
properly.

**Every one of the four is checkable against a named source with a date**, which is
the only reason to include them. The England and Wales reinstatement split, the
section 58 periods, the Schedule 4 notice period — a buyer who checks one of them
and finds it right will believe the other three, and that is worth more than any
claim about capability.

**The signature block is corrected without comment.** The original read "ETABLIX —
Integrated Site Services · Part of Groupe Nseya" and carried no company number.
Jessica Dyer is MCIPS and buys underground construction for a DNO; she will check
Companies House, and a trading name that does not resolve to a registered company
is where a serious procurement reader stops. The block is now "ETABLIX, part of JNN
GLOBAL LTD, company 15405437". Nothing is said about the previous one — the next
letter corrects it by being right.

**What is not held is in the email, not just the attachment.** No ISO, no SSIP,
and the sentence that follows: so it could not qualify as a delivery partner and is
not applying to be one. That is the opposite of a weakness here. A buyer's first
worry about an unsolicited approach to a contractor partnership is that a firm with
no accreditations is trying to get onto it; saying plainly that it is not removes
that worry in one line and makes the actual request legible.

**Employers' liability is in the attachment and not in the email.** It is held
nowhere, the company is exempt as a single-director company whose director holds a
majority of the shares, and no ETABLIX personnel undertake construction operations.
That is the correct legal position and it belongs in the standing table where a
buyer reads it with the insurance limits, not in a covering email where it would
read as the headline.

**The UVDB question is the one that actually moves this forward.** NGED sources
through the Achilles UVDB and applies UVDB Verify to higher-risk categories. Asking
which product codes the partnership will use is a question a Senior Category Buyer
can answer from memory in one line, and the answer either opens a route or closes
it honestly. "Please consider ETABLIX" asks her to do the thinking; this asks her
for the one fact she has and we do not.

**The second question pre-commits to the restraint.** Offering, unprompted, to
approach none of the delivery partners until NGED says that is the route costs
nothing and answers the thing she would otherwise have to wonder about. It is also
simply true: approaching her bidders during a live procurement is the way to be
remembered for the wrong reason.

**The supplier day is given back.** Asking twice for a place in a room that may be
reserved to prospective partners would be pushing. Saying that a written note of
the route would be more use is both honest and easier for her to action.

## What is deliberately absent

**No price, and no indication of one.** "Fixed scope, fixed fee" appears in the
attachment and no figure does.

**No reference to any named client or project.** Nothing in this approach rests on
a third party's name.

**No curriculum vitae and no general capability statement.** The general one
describes a company thinking about a single large compound for three years, and
sending it to a buyer of dispersed underground construction would have said,
accurately, that nobody had thought about her work. The specific statement replaces
it rather than supplementing it.

**Nothing claiming a CDM appointment, self-delivery, or that ETABLIX holds the
supplier contracts.** The finished PDF was read back through the position guard
before sending and returned clean.
