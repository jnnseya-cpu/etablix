# The CHIC supplementary information upload — one file, and how to build it

There is nothing to work around. One file is the rule, and the way to meet it
is to submit one PDF. The paragraph offers you a zip as an alternative; take
the PDF instead, for reasons below.

## First: are you directed to upload at all?

The field says **"only as directed by CHIC, the Authority."** That is a
restriction, not an invitation. It is not a place to add a capability
statement, a brochure or anything else you would like them to see. An
unrequested pack signals that you read instructions selectively, at the exact
stage where following instructions is being tested.

You do have a direction. Section 5 of their supplier guide prompts for
reasoning and supplementary information where the applicant is a start-up with
limited trading results. That is the direction, and it covers the documents
that support your 6.1 explanation — nothing wider.

**The upload must contain exactly what the 6.1 explanation said it would
contain.** If the text promises insurances, policies, CVs, a method and
financial documents, the file holds those and not a page more. A mismatch
between what you said you were attaching and what you attached is the kind of
inconsistency an assessor notices and cannot unsee.

## Why a PDF and not a zip

- The assessor opens a PDF in the browser. A zip has to be downloaded,
  saved, extracted and then opened file by file.
- Corporate IT commonly quarantines or strips zip attachments. A file they
  cannot open is a response you did not give, and their guide says an
  application with insufficient detail is declined and returned.
- A zip has no order. A PDF has the order you chose, with a contents page at
  the front, so the assessor finds the thing they are looking for.
- One PDF cannot arrive partially assembled. A zip of eleven files can.

## How to build it without a PDF merger

The problem people hit is that Windows has no built-in way to merge PDFs. You
do not need one. Build the pack as a single Word document and export it once.

1. **Open a new Word document.** This becomes the whole pack.
2. **Page 1 is the cover and contents** — template below.
3. **Paste in each document you authored** (the method, the CVs, the policies,
   the accounts commentary and the forecast), each starting on a new page.
   Insert → Page Break between them, so nothing drifts when the text reflows.
4. **For certificates and anything that only exists as a scan:** scan the
   whole set in one pass on the scanner, choosing multi-page PDF if it offers
   it — or scan each to an image and use Insert → Pictures to place it on its
   own page. Scan at 300 dpi. Do not photograph a certificate with a phone at
   an angle; a skewed, shadowed insurance schedule reads as carelessness about
   the document it certifies.
5. **Check every page renders** — no missing images, no text running off the
   edge, no placeholder brackets left in.
6. **File → Save As → PDF.** One file, one export.

**Do not put the financial documents through a free online PDF merging
site.** Management accounts, a cash position and a forecast are
commercial-in-confidence. Those sites keep uploads, and you have no agreement
with them. The Word route above never sends anything anywhere.

If you are on a Mac, Preview merges PDFs natively: open one, show thumbnails,
drag the others in, then File → Export as PDF. Same rule about the online
tools still applies.

## Size

The cap is 100 MB and you should be nowhere near it. Scans are the only thing
that can get you there. At 300 dpi greyscale a certificate is about 1 MB;
the same page as a phone photo can be 8 MB and look worse. **Aim for under
20 MB.** If the export lands above that, the cause is colour scans — re-scan
in greyscale rather than compressing the file until the text turns to mush.

## The file name

`ETABLIX-JNN-GLOBAL-LTD-supplementary-information.pdf`

Nothing else. No date stamp, no version number, no "FINAL-v3". The assessor
is filing it against your application, and a version number in the name
invites the question of what was in version 2.

Delete any row you are not actually attaching. A contents page listing a
document that is not in the file is worse than not having a contents page.

## Last check before you upload

Download your own file from the portal after uploading it, and open it. Not
the copy on your desktop — the one that came back. That is the only proof
that what the assessor will open is what you built.
