# "£8,000 demonstration proposal" — 300 words

For the field asking how the demonstration funding would be used.

**The demonstration is a retrospective run on the buyer's own pack.** That is
the whole idea and it is what makes this answer strong. It uses their
documents, needs no live project, no site access and no procurement decision,
and it is checkable against an outcome they already know. It is the
construction equivalent of a backtest, and it converts an assertion about
interfaces into findings they can mark themselves.

**Not a penny of the £8,000 goes on building.** The platform exists. Every
line answers a buyer objection instead, and the largest single line pays
somebody independent to check our results. Assessors read a great many budgets
that quietly fund development under another name.

## The answer — 294 words

> **The demonstration.** A retrospective engine run on an infrastructure
> client's own pre-construction or tender pack, for a project they have already
> let. The engines interrogate the pack and return the package boundaries with
> no named owner, the obligations and time bars it creates, and the interface
> gaps between temporary power, water, welfare, accommodation and logistics.
> The client compares our findings against what actually happened.
>
> **Why this demonstration.** It uses the buyer's own documents, so nothing is
> taken on trust. It needs no live project, no site access and no procurement
> decision. And it is checkable in the only way that matters — against an
> outcome they already know.
>
> **Use of funding.**
>
> Independent verification, £2,500. A chartered quantity surveyor or contract
> specialist, independent of us, reviews the findings and confirms which were
> valid. Self-assessed results persuade nobody.
>
> Information security and data handling, £1,200. Non-disclosure and data
> processing agreements, a segregated secure workspace, and external review of
> how we handle client pre-construction information. This is the first
> objection every infrastructure client raises, and it should be answered
> before it is asked.
>
> Buyer demonstration sessions, £1,500. Travel and attendance to run the
> comparison live with organisations introduced through the programme and
> others we approach directly.
>
> Demonstration pack and recorded walkthrough, £1,800. The person in the room
> is rarely the decision maker, so the artefact has to travel inside their
> organisation without us.
>
> Contingency and cohort demonstration day, £1,000.
>
> **What success looks like.** Three packs run for three separate
> organisations. Findings independently verified, with the proportion confirmed
> reported honestly whatever it turns out to be. Written feedback from each on
> whether the output would have changed a decision. One follow-on paid
> engagement is the target — and a documented reason for a no is still a result
> we would report.

## Why it is built this way

**The demonstration design is the answer.** Most applicants propose a demo of
their product. This proposes a test of their product against a known outcome,
run on the buyer's data. The second is the only one a sceptical infrastructure
client finds interesting, and it is the only one that can fail — which is
exactly why it persuades.

**£2,500 on independent verification is the line that wins the section.** It is
the largest item and it buys somebody to check our work. An assessor scanning
budgets is looking for the applicant who is not simply funding themselves, and
this is that line.

**The security line pre-empts the real blocker.** A client will not hand over
pre-construction information to a company of this size without a data
agreement and somewhere secure to put it. Budgeting for that says you have
been in the room before.

**"Reported honestly whatever it turns out to be" is deliberate.** Publicly
funded programmes want evidence, not marketing, and an applicant who commits to
publishing a poor result is more credible than one promising a good one. It
also protects you: if verification confirms fewer findings than hoped, you said
in advance that you would say so.

## Before you paste it

**Check the lines add to £8,000.** They do — 2,500 + 1,200 + 1,500 + 1,800 +
1,000. If the form asks for a table, keep the same five lines and the same
totals.

**If the form asks for the demonstration to be delivered within the programme
window**, October to March, say so in the first paragraph: three packs across
that period is comfortably deliverable and saying so removes a question.

**Do not name a client you have not asked.** The answer deliberately says
"organisations introduced through the programme and others we approach
directly". Naming a company that has not agreed is the one line here that could
be checked and found wanting.
