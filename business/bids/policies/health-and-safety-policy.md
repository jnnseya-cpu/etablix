## Part 1 — Statement of intent

JNN GLOBAL LTD, trading as ETABLIX, accepts its duties under the Health and
Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the regulations made under it. It is the
policy of this company to provide and maintain safe and healthy working
conditions for every person engaged in its work, and to take all reasonably
practicable steps to protect those who may be affected by it.

The company currently has one working director and no other employees. Section
2(3) of the 1974 Act does not oblige an employer with fewer than five employees
to record its policy in writing. This document is written and maintained
anyway, because the work is carried out on other people's construction sites
and because a policy that exists only in one person's head cannot be audited,
handed over or improved.

**What this company does, and what it does not do.** ETABLIX provides
construction management, commercial and site-services consultancy: project
management, employer's agent services and clerk of works inspection. It does
not carry out construction work, and it does not manage or control the way
construction work is carried out on site unless it is expressly appointed to do
so in writing. **The company does not accept the duties of Principal Contractor
or Principal Designer under the Construction (Design and Management)
Regulations 2015 by implication, by conduct or by silence.** Where any CDM duty
holder role is intended, it will be stated in the appointment, priced, and
accepted in writing only where the company is competent to discharge it.

Nothing in this policy, and nothing in any report, inspection record or advice
the company issues, transfers a client's or a contractor's own statutory duties
to ETABLIX.

The company will:

- assess the risks arising from its own activities and control them;
- work only where it is competent, and decline work where it is not;
- comply with the health and safety rules of every site it enters, and with the
  instructions of the contractor in control of that site;
- provide the information, instruction, training and equipment its people need;
- report hazards it observes to the party able to control them, and record that
  it did so;
- investigate incidents and act on what they show;
- review this policy at least annually and whenever the work changes.

## Part 3 — Arrangements

### 3.1 Risk assessment
A written risk assessment is prepared for each type of activity the company
undertakes: site inspection visits, site meetings, lone working, driving on
business, and display screen work. Assessments are reviewed annually, after any
incident, and whenever the activity changes. Site-specific hazards are taken
from the controlling contractor's assessments and method statements, which are
requested before the first visit.

### 3.2 Site visits
Before any first visit to a site the company obtains and reads the site rules,
the site induction requirements, and the access arrangements. On arrival the
person visiting signs in, completes the site induction, and works within the
permit and escort regime that site operates. No area is entered without the
permission of the contractor in control of it. Where a site declines to
induct, the visit does not take place.

### 3.3 Personal protective equipment
Provided by the company and worn as a minimum on every operational site: safety
helmet to EN 397, eye protection to EN 166, high visibility clothing to EN ISO
20471 class 2 or as the site requires, safety footwear to EN ISO 20345 with
toe and midsole protection, and gloves appropriate to the task. Additional
protection required by the site — hearing, respiratory, fall arrest — is worn
where the site requires it, and only where the wearer has been trained and the
equipment has been face-fit tested or inspected as applicable. PPE is inspected
before each use and replaced when damaged or out of date.

### 3.4 Competence and training
Records are kept for every person working for the company of: CSCS or
equivalent card and its expiry, site safety training, first aid, asbestos
awareness, and any site-specific or client-specific training. A person does not
attend a site without the card that site requires. Training needs are reviewed
annually and on every change of role.

### 3.5 Lone working
Most site attendance is by one person. Before each visit the expected arrival
and departure times and the site contact are recorded and shared with [named
person or arrangement]. Contact is made on arrival and on leaving. If contact
is not made within [30] minutes of the expected departure time, [named person]
telephones the site contact and then the emergency services if the person
cannot be reached. A charged mobile telephone is carried at all times.

### 3.6 Driving on business
Licence, insurance for business use, MOT and roadworthiness are checked
annually and recorded. Journeys are planned so that site attendance does not
require driving while tired. Mobile telephones are not used while driving,
hands-free included, for work calls.

### 3.7 Accidents, incidents and near misses
All accidents, incidents, near misses and dangerous occurrences are recorded in
the company's incident record, whether or not anyone was injured, and whether
they happened to our own person or were observed. Anything reportable under the
Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 is
reported to the Health and Safety Executive by the duty holder responsible; the
company confirms that the report has been made and records the reference. Every
incident is investigated for cause, and the finding is fed back into the risk
assessments.

### 3.8 Observed unsafe conditions on client and contractor sites
The company's people are on site to inspect, manage and report. Where an unsafe
condition or act is observed, the person:

1. stops any work they personally control;
2. reports it immediately to the person in control of that site;
3. records the observation, the time, and who it was reported to;
4. escalates in writing to the client if it is not addressed.

The company does not direct other employers' operatives and does not assume
control of their work. Observation and report is the duty it holds and it
discharges it every time.

### 3.9 First aid and emergencies
The company relies on the first aid provision of the site being visited, which

### 3.10 Occupational health
Exposure on site is short-duration and controlled by the host's arrangements.
Where work involves regular exposure to noise, vibration, dust or respiratory
hazards, health surveillance is arranged before that work begins. Face-fit
testing is completed before any tight-fitting respiratory protective equipment
is used.

### 3.11 Subcontractors and associates
Anyone engaged to work for or on behalf of the company is selected against
their competence, their own health and safety arrangements, their insurance and
their record. Evidence is obtained before engagement and held on file, and
their performance is monitored on the work. The company does not engage anyone
on price alone.

### 3.12 Consultation
With one working director, consultation is direct. As people are engaged, they
will be consulted on health and safety matters affecting them under the Health
and Safety (Consultation with Employees) Regulations 1996, and this section
will be reissued to say how.
