## 1. Position on certification

JNN GLOBAL LTD, trading as ETABLIX, **does not hold ISO 9001 certification and
does not claim it.** The arrangements described here are the company's own.

An ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001 roadmap sits in the company's compliance plan.
Until a certificate is issued by a UKAS-accredited certification body, nothing
this company issues will describe its quality arrangements as certified,
approved or compliant with the standard.

## 2. What quality means in this work

The company produces documents and decisions, not physical works. A defect
here is not a crack in a slab. It is a number that was wrong, an obligation
that was missed, a notice that went out late, or a report that described a site
as it was hoped to be rather than as it was found.

Quality is therefore defined as three things, and every arrangement below
serves one of them:

1. **The output is right** — the figures reconcile, the clause references are
   correct, and the finding matches what was actually observed.
2. **The output is traceable** — anyone can see where each figure and each
   statement came from, and check it.
3. **The output is on time** — a correct valuation issued after the payment
   notice deadline has passed is not a quality output.

## 3. The arrangements

### 3.1 Understanding the requirement before work starts
No engagement begins without a written scope: what is to be delivered, to whom,
by when, on what information, and what is excluded. Where a client's
instruction is ambiguous, the ambiguity is resolved in writing before work
starts, not assumed away. The written scope is the baseline against which the
output is later checked.

### 3.2 Competence
Work is undertaken only where the person doing it is competent to do it. Where
an engagement needs competence the company does not hold, the company either
declines it or brings in a named specialist and says so to the client. The
company does not learn on a client's project at the client's cost without the
client knowing.

### 3.3 Checking before issue
Nothing leaves the company unchecked. The check is against the written scope
from 3.1 and covers, as applicable: arithmetic and cross-casting; the source of
every figure; contract and clause references against the executed contract
rather than from memory; dates against the contract's own time bars; and
whether every question the client asked has actually been answered.

With one working director, the check is a separate, recorded pass over the
document by the author — deliberately separated in time from the drafting, and
initialled and dated. This is stated plainly rather than dressed up as
independent review, because it is not independent review. On the appointment of
a second competent person, checking becomes a second-person check and this
section is reissued.

Where the engagement value or the client's own procedures require independent
review, an external reviewer is engaged and named.

### 3.4 Document control
Every document the company issues carries its title, its revision, its date,
its author, and the name of the project. Superseded revisions are retained and
marked superseded rather than deleted, so that what was issued on any past date
can be reproduced. Nothing is issued without a revision reference.

### 3.5 Records, and how long they are kept
Records are kept of: the written scope, the information received and the date
it was received, the working files behind each output, the checked and
initialled issue copy, and all correspondence.

Records are kept for **twelve years** from completion of the engagement, which
covers the limitation period for a contract executed as a deed. They are held
[state where — the system and the backup arrangement], and backed up
[frequency]. They are not held only on one device.

### 3.6 When something goes wrong
Any error found in an issued document — found by the company, by a client, or
by anyone else — is recorded, corrected and reissued. The record captures what
the error was, how it reached issue, what the consequence was, and what changed
so that it does not recur. Corrections are notified to the client; they are
never made silently in a reissued document.

This applies to errors nobody else noticed. A register that only contains the
mistakes a client caught is a record of what was caught, not of what happened.

### 3.7 Client feedback
Feedback is sought at the end of every engagement and, on engagements longer
than [three] months, at the midpoint as well. It is recorded whether it is good
or bad, and anything adverse is treated under 3.6.

### 3.8 Suppliers, associates and subcontractors
Anyone engaged to deliver part of an ETABLIX output is selected against
competence, insurance and record; is given the same written scope; and their
output is checked by the company under 3.3 before it goes to the client. The
company's name on a document means the company has checked it, whoever drafted
it.

### 3.9 Review
The Managing Director reviews these arrangements annually, and after any error
recorded under 3.6 that reached a client. The review covers: the errors
recorded and their causes, client feedback, whether the arrangements were
actually followed, and what should change. The review is dated on the front of
this document.

## 4. What is not yet in place

Stated plainly, because an assessor will work it out anyway:

- No independent second-person check, until there is a second competent person.
  Mitigated as described at 3.3, and not overstated.
- No certified management system. See section 1.
- No internal audit programme against a standard. Reviews under 3.9 are
  management reviews, and are called that.
