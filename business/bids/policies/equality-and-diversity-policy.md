## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how JNN GLOBAL LTD, trading as ETABLIX, meets its duties
under the Equality Act 2010 and how it expects to be judged on them. It applies
to every person who works for the company or on its behalf — director,
employee, worker, agency worker, consultant, associate and subcontractor — and
to every relationship the company has with clients, suppliers, tenderers and
applicants for work.

It applies from first contact. A person who applies for a job, a supplier who
asks to be considered, and a subcontractor who is invited to price are all
covered by it.

## 2. The commitment

The company will not discriminate against any person because of a protected
characteristic under the Equality Act 2010: age, disability, gender
reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race,
religion or belief, sex, or sexual orientation.

This covers the four forms the Act recognises — direct discrimination,
indirect discrimination, harassment and victimisation — and applies to
recruitment, terms of engagement, pay, training, promotion, the allocation of
work, discipline, and the ending of any engagement.

## 3. What this means in the construction context

A policy that only restates the statute is worth little. In this industry the
commitment means specific things, and these are the ones this company holds
itself to.

**Recruitment.** Roles are advertised against the competence the work actually
needs, not against a profile of who has held it before. Requirements that
exclude without cause — an unnecessary years-of-experience floor, a driving
licence for a role that does not require driving, "must have UK main contractor
experience" where the skill is transferable — are removed before a role is
advertised.

**Site access and welfare.** Where the company manages or specifies welfare
facilities, it specifies facilities usable by the whole workforce, including
separate and secure sanitary and changing provision, and provision that does
not assume a single gender or a single body. Where it inspects welfare on
behalf of a client, inadequate provision is reported as a finding.

**Language and literacy.** Site information, inductions and safety material are
of no use to someone who cannot read them. Where the workforce on a project
includes people whose first language is not English, the company recommends and
supports pictorial and translated safety information rather than treating
comprehension as the worker's problem.

**Subcontractors and the supply chain.** Selection is against competence,
capacity, safety record, financial standing and price. It is never against the
ownership or composition of the firm. The company will not accept an
instruction from a client to exclude a supplier on grounds this policy
prohibits, and will say so in writing if one is given.

**Reasonable adjustments.** Where a person working for the company has a
disability, adjustments to working arrangements, equipment or premises are made
where reasonable. A person does not have to disclose a disability to ask, and a
request will not count against them.

**Flexible working and caring responsibilities.** Requests are considered on
their merits and against the needs of the work, not against an assumption about
who has caring responsibilities.

## 4. Harassment and bullying

Harassment related to a protected characteristic is unlawful and will not be
tolerated on any project this company works on, including where the person
responsible is employed by a client or another contractor.

Under the Worker Protection (Amendment of Equality Act 2010) Act 2023 an
employer must take reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment of its
workers. The company's steps are: this policy, stated at engagement; a named
route to raise a concern; a commitment to act on what is raised; and a
commitment to raise with the site's controlling contractor any conduct observed
on their site.

A person who raises a concern in good faith, or supports someone else's, will
not be disadvantaged for doing so. That is not a courtesy — victimisation is
itself unlawful.

## 5. Raising a concern

Any concern about discrimination, harassment or bullying should be raised with
the Managing Director, [name], at [email] or [phone]. Where the concern is
about the Managing Director, it should be raised with [an alternative route —
name a non-executive, an adviser, or state that the company will appoint an
independent person to hear it].

Concerns are taken seriously, handled confidentially so far as investigation
allows, and answered. Where a concern is upheld, the company acts — up to and
including ending an engagement.

A person retains the right to bring a claim to an employment tribunal, and
nothing in this policy restricts that right or requires them to exhaust an
internal process first. Advisory, Conciliation and Arbitration Service (ACAS)
advice is available independently of the company.

## 6. Responsibility, monitoring and review

The Managing Director owns this policy and is accountable for it. The company
currently has one working director and no other employees, so there is no
workforce composition to monitor and no figures are published: stating
otherwise would be a fiction, and this policy will not carry one.

As people are engaged, the company will record and review, at least annually:
the composition of applicants and of those engaged, any concerns raised and
their outcome, and any adjustment requested and whether it was made. Those
figures will be reported here from the first review after the first engagement.

This policy is reviewed annually, and whenever the law changes or the company's
size or work changes materially.
