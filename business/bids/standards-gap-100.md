# "Standards you do not currently meet" — Yes, and 100 words

## The Yes/No

**Yes.** None of the relevant certifications are currently held. Answering no
would be checkable in one search and would be the end of the application.

This is an easy field to score well on, because the assessor is testing whether
you know what you would need. Naming the right ones — in the right order, with
the information security ones first — proves you have thought past the sale to
the deployment.

---

## The answer — 98 words

> Yes — none are currently held.
>
> Information security binds first. A large client will not release
> pre-construction information without Cyber Essentials, and at tier one
> without Cyber Essentials Plus and ISO/IEC 27001, supported by an independent
> penetration test. ISO/IEC 42001 for AI management systems is emerging and we
> expect it to be asked for.
>
> On the service side: SSIP accreditation, ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001,
> Constructionline and Achilles UVDB, plus professional indemnity, public and
> employers' liability at each client's limits.
>
> Sequence: Cyber Essentials, then SSIP, then ISO 27001 and 9001 — each
> scheduled against revenue rather than assumed.

---

## Why it is ordered this way

**Information security first, not health and safety.** Most applicants in this
sector would lead with SSIP because that is the familiar gate. For a company
handing a client's pre-construction information into a platform, the binding
constraint is the one that decides whether the data can move at all. Leading
with it says you understand you are selling software into an infrastructure
client, not just services.

**ISO/IEC 42001 is the line that shows currency.** It is the AI management
system standard published in 2023, and very few applicants will name it.
Saying you expect it to be asked for — rather than claiming to be working
towards it — is both honest and forward-looking.

**"Scheduled against revenue rather than assumed" is the closing discipline.**
It says these cost money the company does not yet have, without asking for
sympathy. An assessor reading a list of certifications wants to know whether
the applicant has a plan or a wish.

**It connects back to the barriers answer.** The circular data-access problem
named there is the same constraint named here, from the other side. Two fields
telling the same story make an application read as one thing.

## Before you paste it

**Confirm nothing on this list is actually held.** If any accreditation or
insurance is already in place, move it out of this answer immediately —
listing something you hold as a gap is a smaller error than the reverse but it
still costs you.

**Do not add dates you have not booked.** "Scheduled against revenue" is
honest. "Cyber Essentials by December" is a commitment somebody may check, and
a missed self-imposed date in a funded programme is worse than no date.

**If the form gives you more room elsewhere for a compliance plan**, that is
where dates and costs belong — not here.
