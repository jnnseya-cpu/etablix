# Hitachi Energy — Gary Coleman's referral into HVDC supply chain

This is the most actionable reply of the seven. Gary has offered to forward
your details to the UK SCM managers in HVDC, which is an internal referral you
could not have bought.

Three things his reply told you, and the third is the one that matters.

**He is not your buyer, and he said so.** MEP and main installation. He is
being generous rather than interested, so do not sell to him. Thank him, make
it easy, and make him look good for forwarding it.

**He described the fragmentation without being asked.** A cabin and site
establishment provider on one contract, M&E separately, waste and security as
individual SCM contracts. That is a second independent confirmation of the
proposition, from a second tier-one OEM — and it tells you exactly how the
scope is bought today.

**Which means the realistic way in is one package, not the whole thing.** An
SCM manager who already has a cabin contract and a waste contract is not going
to consolidate them for an unproven supplier. They might, however, put a new
name on the next tender for one of them. Lead with that, and let the
integration argument be the second conversation.

## What to do next, and what not to

**Do not describe Hitachi's own contracting arrangement back to them.** Gary
told you how it works in a private message. The email above says what ETABLIX
offers and lets the reader map it onto their own arrangements. Repeating his
description to his colleagues would expose him for a kindness.

**Never pay to register on a supplier portal.** If registration carries a fee,
that is not a route to work.

**Declare only what you hold today.** Insurances, accreditations, ISO
certifications, memberships. "In progress, expected [month]" is a respectable
answer on a prequalification; a false one ends it, and in an SCM function
verification is routine.

**Answer any SCM request within one working day.** The speed of your first
return is the only performance data they have on you, and they read it as
such whether or not they say so.

**Tell Gary what happened.** Whatever the outcome, come back to him once and
say how it went. He spent a small amount of his own standing forwarding this;
people who hear back give a second referral, and almost nobody hears back.
