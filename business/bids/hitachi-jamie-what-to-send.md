# Hitachi Energy — Jamie Holiday has opened the door. What to send.

**Received from Jamie Holiday, Supply Chain Manager – Contracted Services,
Hitachi Energy**, following Gary Coleman's internal referral:

> Please find below the link to the Hitachi Energy supplier interest register.
> If you would like to share some case studies with me, which demonstrate the
> services available through Etablix, I can also discuss this with my supply
> chain colleagues.

Read what that second sentence actually is. It is not a polite close. A supply
chain manager for contracted services has offered to take your material to the
people who let the work. That is the most valuable sentence anyone has sent
this company.

## The problem, stated plainly

**There are no case studies.** ETABLIX has not completed a client engagement
under its own name, so there is no client to quote, no contract to cite and no
testimonial to reproduce. Inventing one is not on the table at any price, for
any amount of work, ever. A fabricated case study sent to a supply chain
manager who then forwards it to three colleagues is not a risk that can be
managed — it is the end of the relationship and of the referral chain behind
it, and Gary's name is on that chain.

So the answer is not to find a way to produce case studies. It is to send
something better and to say why in the first paragraph.

## The single most important design constraint

**"I can also discuss this with my supply chain colleagues" means the pack gets
forwarded.**

Whatever is attached will be read by people who never met you, never read your
covering email, and have no idea the company is new. If the honest statement
about having no case studies lives only in the email body, then the forwarded
PDF looks like a document claiming a track record it does not have — and the
person reading it third-hand draws exactly the wrong conclusion.

That is why the statement is section 1 of the document, not a line in the
email. The attachment has to be able to defend itself alone.

## What to send instead of case studies

Two attachments. Not five. A supply chain manager will open two.

**1. Delivery record and capability** — `ETABLIX-Delivery-Record.docx` / `.pdf`,
built by `build-delivery-record.cjs`. Eleven pages, and every bracketed field
is yours to fill:

- Section 1 states that the company has no case studies of its own, that what
  follows is the director's record from employment, and that every entry is
  verifiable against the employer named beside it.
- Section 3 is one block per scheme — name (or a precise description where the
  contract is confidential), employer, ultimate client, location, dates, scheme
  value, the value of the scope you were accountable for, your role, peak
  workforce, establishment duration, the scope delivered, what changed because
  of it, and a referee with contact details and the date they agreed.
- Section 5 is the standing position: insurances, employees, accreditations
  held and accreditations in progress with the stage each has reached, and the
  written statement that ETABLIX does not accept CDM Principal Contractor or
  Principal Designer by implication.
- Section 6 is what is not yet in place.
- Section 7 is the proposal that closes the gap.

**2. A specimen diagnostic** — the worked example on an illustrative scheme. It
shows what a client actually receives, which for a procurement audience is more
useful than a case study: a case study tells them it worked somewhere else, a
specimen tells them what lands on their desk. Keep the word ILLUSTRATIVE in
the body text and in the metadata of every page, not just a footer.

Do not send the policy set unless asked. It is ready, and offering it in one
line is better than attaching it.

## Build the delivery record once and it serves two buyers

CHIC returned the DPS application saying the technical and professional ability
detail was insufficient, and invited references with contact details for
referees. Jamie has asked for evidence of what the services are and who can
vouch for them.

**These are the same document.** Named projects, dates, values, scope, your
personal role, and referees. Filling in `ETABLIX-Delivery-Record` is the
highest-value hour available this week, because it unblocks the CHIC
resubmission and answers Hitachi Energy with one piece of work.

## Do the registration first, then reply

Register on the supplier interest register before you write back, so the reply
can carry the reference number. A supply chain manager who asked you to do two
things and gets both back has learned something about how you operate.

Three rules on the registry:

1. **Never pay anything.** If any part of it asks for a fee to register, to be
   listed, or to be "verified", stop and say so. Legitimate supplier registers
   for a business of this size do not charge.
2. **It is a declaration, not a form.** Anything claimed on a supplier register
   can be relied on contractually later. Do not tick an accreditation that is
   not held, do not round a turnover figure upward, and where there is a field
   for certifications put the honest position — "application submitted [date]"
   is credible and "ISO 9001" when it is not held is a misrepresentation that
   surfaces at audit.
3. **Have the answers to hand before you start**: company number 15405437,
   incorporation date, the financial position from the management pack,
   insurance details or the honest "to be placed on appointment", employee
   count, and the CDM position.

Record the reference number in the ledger the moment you have it.

## Why each part of that is there

- **The reference number first.** It closes his first request before his second
  one is discussed, and it takes one line.
- **The honest paragraph second, and never later.** It is the paragraph that
  makes every other claim in the pack worth reading. Procurement people spend
  their working lives reading embellished submissions; a volunteered gap is
  rare enough to be memorable, and it buys credit that a claim cannot.
- **"That is set out in the first section of the attachment"** tells him the
  document is safe to forward without a covering explanation. That is the
  sentence that gets it forwarded.
- **Two attachments, each described in one line.** He has to know what he is
  opening before he opens it.
- **The GPQS paragraph is the one that proves you did the work.** Most people
  referred into a company that size reply without knowing which half of it
  they fit. Naming the better fit and giving the reason — repetition, not
  scale — reads as judgement rather than eagerness. The reasoning is in
  `hitachi-gpqs-brief.md`.
- **The proposal has a rate, a date and an explicit statement that it does not
  carry forward.** A price with a reason and an expiry is a commercial
  decision. The same price without them is a discount, and a discount tells a
  buyer the first number was padded.
- **One ask, and it is small.** Twenty minutes, with him and one colleague. Not
  a meeting with the supply chain team, not a presentation.

## What not to do

- **Nothing from Gary's internal email gets characterised back to his
  colleagues.** Referencing that his note mentioned HVDC and GPQS is fine —
  Jamie received that note. Anything about how Hitachi Energy currently
  contracts its site services, or any suggestion that Gary indicated a problem,
  does not appear. Gary put his name to an introduction; he does not get
  quoted.
- **Do not send the fictional specimen without the label.** The NORTHREACH /
  Marrowbridge material is invented and must never be presented as, or built
  from, a real client's pack — not even anonymised.
- **Do not attach the policy set, the financial pack or the method statement
  unsolicited.** Offer them in a line if he asks what else exists.
- **Do not chase inside a week.** He has offered to speak to colleagues; that
  takes longer than a reply. If nothing comes back in ten working days, one
  short note asking whether anything further would help.
- **Do not quote a rate anywhere that gets published.** Pricing stays in
  documents issued to a named recipient.
