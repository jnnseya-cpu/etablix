# MaresConnect — the follow-up to Joseph Moneley, and the clause 4.2 request

## Why this email and not the one that was asked for

The target note says *target the EPC bidders for Lot 1A and Lot 1B*. That is the
right commercial instinct and it is the one approach ETABLIX has signed an
agreement not to make.

**NDA clause 4.1.5**, live since execution, says the Recipient shall not

> *initiate contact or communicate with any Representative, Adviser of the
> Discloser … or any landlord, lender, customer or supplier of the Group, in
> connection with the Proposed Transaction.*

The Lot 1A and Lot 1B bidders are suppliers of the Group in connection with the
Proposed Transaction. Writing to them about MaresConnect is the specific thing
4.1.5 prohibits. And the decline note already submitted through Aconex said, in
writing:

> We will approach no party about this project without the written consent
> contemplated by clause 4.2 of the non-disclosure agreement.

So a letter to the bidders now would breach a signed agreement under Irish law
with exclusive Dublin jurisdiction, and contradict a written undertaking given
to the counterparty days earlier. The second is worse than the first: it is the
kind of thing a procurement officer remembers about a supplier permanently.

**What is permitted.** Writing to MaresConnect itself. Joseph Moneley is the
Discloser's own named procurement contact, and communicating with the Discloser
is not what 4.1.5 restricts. PQQ §1.11.10(d) — contacting an officer or employee
of MaresConnect before award — bound only while ETABLIX held Candidate status,
and declining ended that. The NDA continues; the canvassing restriction does
not.

**And clause 4.2 is the route to the thing actually wanted.** It permits the
4.1.5 contact with prior written consent. So the email below asks for it. If it
is given, the bidder approach becomes available and documented. If it is
refused, we know, and we have not breached anything finding out.

The decline note left exactly two questions open — the supply-chain register and
how MaresConnect would prefer us to engage. This picks both up, which is why it
reads as a follow-up rather than a cold approach.

## Addressing

**To:** procurement@maresconnect.ie
**Cc:** joseph.moneley@maresconnect.ie

The formal inbox is the channel of record on a confidentiality-bound
procurement; the named address was published on the original notice, so copying
it reaches the person without bypassing the inbox. Do not send to the personal
address alone.

**Attach nothing.** PQQ §1.6 and §6 both say marketing material should not be
provided unless it supports a specific answer, and this is not an answer. The
capability note is *offered*, in the form they prefer, and sent only if asked
for. No capability statement, no CV, no specimen.

## Choices in it worth knowing about

**The lot structure is named, and that is safe here and nowhere else.** Lot 1A
and Lot 1B are MaresConnect's own; telling the Discloser about its own
procurement is not a disclosure. The same two sentences in a letter to a bidder
would be use of confidential information for a purpose outside the agreement,
which is a separate and worse problem from the 4.1.5 contact restriction. Keep
the distinction in mind if any of this text is reused.

**"The consequence is not poor work."** The argument is always that the scope
falls due at bid stage when nobody has time — never that a main contractor does
it badly. Moneley may well have run EPC bids himself, and the bidders are firms
he is about to appoint.

**No appointment is implied.** "We take no construction or design appointment"
is there because a reader of a letter about compounds and enabling works will
wonder about CDM. It says the answer before the question is asked, without
naming the regime and inviting a conversation about it.

**The refusal is pre-accepted.** "If consent is not something you wish to give,
that is an answer" removes the cost of saying no, which is the main reason a
procurement officer ignores a request rather than declining it. A recorded no is
more useful than silence: it closes the bidder route properly rather than
leaving it ambiguous.

**No date is asserted** for the decline note, because the sent date is not on
this record. Add it if you want it — "Further to our notification of 3 November"
reads better than "further to our notification" — but do not guess at it.

## What this does not do

It does not reach the EPC bidders. Nothing can, about this project, until Mares
answers question 2 in writing. If the answer is yes, the bidder letters are
quick to write and should name no confidential detail beyond what the public
notice carries. If the answer is no or nothing comes back, the Lot 1A/1B route
is closed and the target note should be amended to say so, rather than left
reading "first priority" against an approach that cannot be made.
