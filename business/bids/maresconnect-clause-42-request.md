# MaresConnect — the follow-up to Joseph Moneley, and the clause 4.2 request

## Why this email and not the one that was asked for

The target note says *target the EPC bidders for Lot 1A and Lot 1B*. That is the
right commercial instinct and it is the one approach ETABLIX has signed an
agreement not to make.

**NDA clause 4.1.5**, live since execution, says the Recipient shall not

> *initiate contact or communicate with any Representative, Adviser of the
> Discloser … or any landlord, lender, customer or supplier of the Group, in
> connection with the Proposed Transaction.*

The Lot 1A and Lot 1B bidders are suppliers of the Group in connection with the
Proposed Transaction. Writing to them about MaresConnect is the specific thing
4.1.5 prohibits. And the decline note already submitted through Aconex said, in
writing:

> We will approach no party about this project without the written consent
> contemplated by clause 4.2 of the non-disclosure agreement.

So a letter to the bidders now would breach a signed agreement under Irish law
with exclusive Dublin jurisdiction, and contradict a written undertaking given
to the counterparty days earlier. The second is worse than the first: it is the
kind of thing a procurement officer remembers about a supplier permanently.

**What is permitted.** Writing to MaresConnect itself. Joseph Moneley is the
Discloser's own named procurement contact, and communicating with the Discloser
is not what 4.1.5 restricts. PQQ §1.11.10(d) — contacting an officer or employee
of MaresConnect before award — bound only while ETABLIX held Candidate status,
and declining ended that. The NDA continues; the canvassing restriction does
not.

**And clause 4.2 is the route to the thing actually wanted.** It permits the
4.1.5 contact with prior written consent. So the email below asks for it. If it
is given, the bidder approach becomes available and documented. If it is
refused, we know, and we have not breached anything finding out.

The decline note left exactly two questions open — the supply-chain register and
how MaresConnect would prefer us to engage. This picks both up, which is why it
reads as a follow-up rather than a cold approach.

## Addendum 1, and what it is not

On 6 November at 20:44 MaresConnect issued Addendum 1 to contact@etablix.com:
editable versions of the PQQ documentation for all four LOTs, "identical in
content to the previously issued versions and ... provided in an editable format
to facilitate completion."

**This is not evidence that the decline was not read.** Three things say so:

- It is addressed **"Dear Candidates"**, plural. It is a distribution, not a
  reply.
- It carries **both** document sets, 1A/1B and 2A/2B, so it went to the whole
  PQQ list irrespective of which LOTs any Candidate is pursuing.
- Its content is purely administrative. Nothing in it responds to anything
  ETABLIX wrote.

And there is a reason a buyer deliberately does not prune that list. A
procurement team that drops a Candidate from an addendum distribution creates
the argument that an addendum was not issued to a Candidate -- a procedural
challenge nobody wants on a 750 MW interconnector. Leaving the list exactly as
issued is the careful thing to do, not an oversight. Reading it as a snub, and
answering in that register, would be the only way to turn it into one.

**The editable versions were checked rather than taken on trust**, because the
whole decline rests on two numbers and a buyer saying "identical in content"
while a threshold had moved is exactly the thing worth catching. Both documents
are still revision **P01**, and every decisive gate is unchanged:

| | LOT 1A / 1B | LOT 2A / 2B |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum turnover, in both of the last two audited years | EUR 500m | EUR 500m |
| Minimum net assets | EUR 200m | EUR 200m |
| Financial part completed by | each consortium member | each consortium member |
| Shortlist | up to three (3) | up to seven (7) |
| Section 5.2 capability | manufacture VSC HVDC / HVAC GIS technology | manufacturing capacity to fully develop and test 320 kV HVDC XLPE, 400 kV HVAC underground and 400 kV cables |

So there is nothing to re-assess and nothing to reconsider. The decline stands
on its own terms.

**What the addendum does usefully give us is a reason for the email below to
arrive now.** A follow-up two days after a decline needs no excuse, but one
prompted by an addendum is better: it is plainly administrative, it explains
itself, and it lets the two substantive asks travel without reading as a second
bite. Send one email, not two.

## Addressing

The formal inbox is the channel of record on a confidentiality-bound
procurement; the named address was published on the original notice, so copying
it reaches the person without bypassing the inbox. Do not send to the personal
address alone.

**Attach nothing.** PQQ §1.6 and §6 both say marketing material should not be
provided unless it supports a specific answer, and this is not an answer. The
capability note is *offered*, in the form they prefer, and sent only if asked
for. No capability statement, no CV, no specimen.

## Timing: the register ask now, the clause 4.2 ask later

An earlier version of this note said to send one email carrying both items.
That was right about not sending two emails days apart and wrong about when the
consent ask lands.

**The PQQ is live.** Returns are not in and no Candidate is shortlisted. Asking
a procurement lead, in that window, for written permission to approach their
Candidates is asking them to do the one thing their own §1.11.4 says they will
not do — enter into dealings with individual respondents — and to do it on
behalf of a third party. The likely answer is no, or nothing. A refusal
recorded during the PQQ is then the settled position, and the second ask months
later has to argue with it.

**After shortlisting the same question is easy to grant.** The field is fixed
and public to the parties, and an offer made on the same terms to every
shortlisted bidder does not favour any of them. It arguably helps MaresConnect:
the Enabling Works line at §2.5 becomes comparable between bids instead of each
bidder carrying its own allowance on its own assumptions. That is the version a
procurement lead can say yes to.

So: **send the short reply below now.** Hold the consent request until
shortlisting for LOTs 1A and 1B is announced, then send the longer version that
follows it. Nothing is lost by waiting — clause 4.1.5 prevents the approach
either way, and the decline note's undertaking is a statement of compliance,
not a promise to ask by a date.

**Everything in it is zero-effort for the reader.** "None of which needs a
decision from you today" is the load-bearing sentence: a procurement lead
mid-PQQ has no authority to grant anything and no time to consider it, so every
item is written to be read now and acted on later or not at all. Nothing can be
refused, so nothing goes on file as refused.

**The request to come off the Candidate distribution is gone.** It was in an
earlier draft and it was wrong twice over: it asks the reader to do something,
and it removes ETABLIX from the information flow on a project it wants to stay
near. Being on the list costs nothing but an obligation already owed under the
agreement, and the line about handling the documentation covers that. Nothing
in this letter closes anything.

**The order matters.** The decline comes first, in two sentences, because it is
the only thing the addendum needed answering and burying it under an offer
would look evasive. The three doors come after, as separate short paragraphs
rather than a numbered list, so each can be read on its own by somebody
skimming.

**The three are deliberately different in kind.** One is a document they file.
One is an act they might perform at a stage named from their own document. One
is a standing availability with no occasion attached. A reader who has no use
for the first two can still say yes to the third, and a letter offering only
one thing gets one answer or none.

**THE INTRODUCTION IS THE BETTER ASK, AND IT NEEDS NO CONSENT AT ALL.**

The consent route asks MaresConnect to permit something ETABLIX would then do.
The introduction route asks MaresConnect to do it. The second is easier to
grant, and it is easier for a reason that matters: **clause 4.1.5 restricts the
Recipient from INITIATING contact.** If Mares makes the introduction, Mares
initiated it. There is nothing for ETABLIX to seek consent for, because the
prohibited act never happens. An introduction from the Discloser is not a
4.1.5 breach by the Recipient under any reading of it.

So this is not a weaker version of the consent ask. It is a route around the
clause rather than through it, and the sentence "we will not approach any party
about this project ourselves" is what makes that explicit — it reassures the
reader that the offer is not a request to be let off the restriction.

It also costs the reader nothing today. There is no decision to make, so there
is nothing to refuse, and nothing goes on file as refused. It sits in Moneley's
mind until the Preferred Bidder exists, which is the only moment it could ever
have been useful.

**And it is pitched at pre-FID, not at award, deliberately.** §2.5 places the
Enabling Works Contract with the Preferred Bidder at pre-FID stage. By contract
award the EPC has already priced the compound into its bid and the number is
fixed; at Preferred Bidder it is still being settled. Naming the stage from the
document also shows the PQQ was read, which after declining it is worth more
than it costs.

**What the consent ask is still for.** The commercially valuable moment is
earlier than either — shortlisting, when several bidders are pricing the same
enabling works line and none of them has settled it. Reaching them needs 4.2
consent and nothing else will do, because there ETABLIX would be the one
initiating. So both remain live: the introduction offered now as a standing
one, and the consent request held for shortlisting. They are not alternatives
and the first does not spend the second.

**Attach nothing**, for the same reason as before: §1.6 and §6 say marketing
material is not provided unless it supports a specific answer. The capability
note is offered and sent only if asked for.

## Choices in it worth knowing about

**The lot structure is named, and that is safe here and nowhere else.** Lot 1A
and Lot 1B are MaresConnect's own; telling the Discloser about its own
procurement is not a disclosure. The same two sentences in a letter to a bidder
would be use of confidential information for a purpose outside the agreement,
which is a separate and worse problem from the 4.1.5 contact restriction. Keep
the distinction in mind if any of this text is reused.

**"The consequence is not poor work."** The argument is always that the scope
falls due at bid stage when nobody has time — never that a main contractor does
it badly. Moneley may well have run EPC bids himself, and the bidders are firms
he is about to appoint.

**No appointment is implied.** "We take no construction or design appointment"
is there because a reader of a letter about compounds and enabling works will
wonder about CDM. It says the answer before the question is asked, without
naming the regime and inviting a conversation about it.

**The refusal is pre-accepted.** "If consent is not something you wish to give,
that is an answer" removes the cost of saying no, which is the main reason a
procurement officer ignores a request rather than declining it. A recorded no is
more useful than silence: it closes the bidder route properly rather than
leaving it ambiguous.

**The decline note is no longer dated in the text at all.** Opening on Addendum
1, which has a date we hold, removes the need for one — so the gap that would
otherwise have had to be filled in by hand has gone. Nothing is guessed at.

**The distribution-list request is an offer, not a demand.** "If it is simpler
to leave the list as issued, that is entirely fine" is there because the buyer
has a procedural reason to keep it intact, and asking them to take a step that
creates challenge risk for them would be a poor first impression from a firm
that wants to be on their supply-chain register.

## What this does not do

It does not reach the EPC bidders. Nothing ETABLIX initiates can, about this
project, until Mares answers the consent request in writing — but an
introduction made by Mares is not something ETABLIX initiates, which is why the
standing offer above is in the reply going now. If the answer is yes, the bidder letters are quick to
write and must name no confidential detail beyond what the public notice
carries. If the answer is no or nothing comes back, the route is closed and the
target note should be amended to say so, rather than left reading "first
priority" against an approach that cannot lawfully be made.

**A correction, recorded because it was first recorded the wrong way round.**
An earlier version of this note said the target note described LOT 1B wrongly.
It does not. The PQQ names the four LOTs:

| LOT | Title as the PQQ gives it |
|---|---|
| 1A | EPC for HVDC Converter Stations |
| 1B | EPC for Irish 400 kV AC Tail Substation |
| 2A | EPC for the HVDC Subsea and Underground Cable System, and the associated HVAC underground cable system |
| 2B | EPC for the HVAC underground cable system in Ireland (Dublin) |

The target note's "Lot 1A, covering both converter stations, and Lot 1B,
covering the Irish 400 kV tail substation" is right. What was confused with it
is §5.2, which sets out what a Candidate must be able to MANUFACTURE to
prequalify for a LOT — VSC HVDC for 1A, HVAC GIS for 1B. The LOT is an EPC
contract for a substation; the gate on bidding for it is switchgear
manufacture. Two different statements, both true, and the position note's own
table is correctly headed "What §5.2 requires the Candidate to be able to do".

**The one correction that does stand** is that the bidder approach cannot be
"first priority" while clause 4.1.5 stands. The consent that would make it
possible is.
