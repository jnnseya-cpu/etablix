# Hitachi Energy — the referral has landed. What to send.

Gary Coleman has asked Jaime, internally, to advise you how to register for the
Hitachi UK supply chain, and he has framed the proposition himself: **"site
services as a single point of contact rather than multiple Subcontracts"**, for
**HVDC and GPQS** projects.

This is the referral that has been open in the outreach ledger. It has moved,
and it moved because Gary chose to put his own name to it internally. Everything
below is written to protect that.

## Three rules before you write a word

**1. Do not sell to Jaime.** Jaime's job in this thread is to tell you how to
register. That is an administrative question and it deserves an administrative
answer. A pitch here converts a favour into a sales approach, and it makes
Gary's recommendation look like something he was talked into.

**2. Use Gary's own words and do not improve on them.** "A single point of
contact rather than multiple subcontracts" is how a Hitachi Energy person
described it, to a Hitachi Energy colleague, unprompted. That sentence is worth
more inside their organisation than any phrasing of yours. Echo it.

**3. Say nothing about how Hitachi currently contracts this scope.** Gary told
you privately that they hold separate contracts for cabins and site
establishment, M&E, waste and security. That was a private explanation to a
former contact, not intelligence to display. Repeating it to his colleague — even
as evidence you understand their business — exposes where you learned it and
costs Gary something he cannot get back. **The temptation to demonstrate
knowledge here is the single biggest risk in this email. Resist it.**

### Why each part is there

**"The question is an administrative one."** It tells Jaime in nine words that
this will be quick, which is what decides whether they answer today or next
month.

**The description is one sentence and borrows Gary's framing.** "Under one
contract rather than several" is his point, in your words, without any claim
about what Hitachi does now.

**The details block is a paste-ready supplier record.** Jaime may have to
forward this to whoever owns registration. A block they can copy costs them
nothing; a paragraph they have to summarise costs them five minutes and might
cost you the reply.

**"Whether HVDC and GPQS use the same one"** proves you read the email and asks
something genuinely useful. Gary named two programmes and one of them is new to
you — the question is real, not decoration.

**"I will complete it and come back to you when it is done rather than take
more of your time"** is the line that makes Jaime willing to help. You have
promised to go away, and that is what an inbox wants.

**No attachment.** One link. A document from an unknown sender is filtered
before a person sees it, and nobody asked for one.

## The five things to watch for next

**Never pay to register.** A fee to be listed on a supplier portal is not a
route to work. If a charge appears, that is a stop and not a cost.

**List only what is genuinely held.** Registration will ask about insurances,
SSIP and ISO certifications. You hold none of them yet, the standards answer
written for the Catapult application already sets out that position honestly,
and Gary can verify a claim in five minutes. "Not held, and here is when" beats
a tick you cannot support.

**Your prequalification pack is ready, which it was not a week ago.** The five
branded policies — health and safety, occupational health, equality and
diversity, environmental, and quality management arrangements — are exactly what
a tier-one supplier registration asks for. Send them when they are asked for,
never before, and sign and date them first.

**Expect information security to come up.** For anything involving HVDC project
data, Cyber Essentials will be asked about before ISO 27001 is. That is already
named as the first compliance gate in the funding application.

**Find out what GPQS is before your next message.** Gary named it and you did
not ask. It is a programme you were not aware of and a reader can tell the
difference between someone who knows and someone repeating an acronym.

## The message that earns the next referral

Not now. **When registration is complete**, send Gary one line privately:

> Gary — registered, and Jaime made it painless. Thank you for putting your name
> to it. I will let you know if anything comes of it.

That is the whole message. He took a small risk on somebody he had met once, on
a platform, and the thing that makes a person do it again is finding out it went
somewhere. Almost nobody closes that loop, which is exactly why it works.
