# "Leadership team domain experience" — Yes, 100 words

**Say "team is one person" before the assessor works it out.** An answer opening
"our leadership team has deep experience" that turns out to describe one
director reads as a small deception, and it is the sort that colours everything
after it. Naming it in the first clause costs four words and buys the rest.

The substance is genuinely strong, and the framing is the point: three sides of
the same contract.

---

## The answer — 110 words

> Yes — though "team" is one person today, which is stated rather than implied.
>
> [Name] MCIOB has spent his career on exactly the scope this solution
> addresses, from three sides of the same contract. Consultancy at Turner &
> Townsend. Client side at UK Power Reserve, running risk and change and then
> pre-construction across a 628MW programme of more than thirty new-build
> generation and storage sites. Contractor side at GE Vernova as construction
> subcontract manager on EPC and full turnkey projects across the UK, Ireland
> and Europe, including a 36-month project carrying a 309-bed workforce village
> inside the turnkey scope.
>
> Interfaces are arbitrated by people who have sat on every side.

---

## Why this framing

**"Three sides of the same contract" is the whole answer.** Most people in this
industry see the interface problem from one seat — consultant, client or
contractor — and argue their side of it. Someone who has held all three is the
person who can define a boundary both parties will accept, which is precisely
what the solution does. The career becomes an argument for the product rather
than a list of employers.

**Every claim is checkable.** MCIOB is a register. The employers are on a
LinkedIn profile. 628MW, thirty-plus sites, 36 months and 309 beds are the same
figures used in the track record and deployments answers, so three fields agree
rather than drifting.

**The closing line does the work of a paragraph.** "Interfaces are arbitrated by
people who have sat on every side" is the sentence an assessor remembers, and
it converts a CV into a reason to believe the mechanism.

**It does not claim a team that does not exist.** The planned hires belong in a
different field. Inventing a leadership team is the single easiest thing on this
form to disprove, because Companies House lists the directors.

## Before you paste it

**Verify the Turner & Townsend detail.** It comes from my earlier notes rather
than from you. If the role or the dates are different, correct it or cut the
clause — the answer still works on two sides instead of three, and an
unverifiable employer is worse than a shorter list.

**Add your role at Turner & Townsend if there is room.** "Consultancy at Turner
& Townsend" is thin next to the other two. Two or three words naming what you
did there would strengthen it, and you have one word of headroom.

**Do not add the planned hires here.** An assessor asking about domain
experience wants who is there now. Hiring plans answered in this field read as
padding the CV of a company of one.
