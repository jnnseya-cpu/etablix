## 3. Mobilisation — the first ten working days

1. **Written scope agreed.** What is to be delivered, to whom, by when, on what
   information, and what is excluded. Nothing starts without it.
2. **Contract read in full.** The executed contract, its amendments and its
   schedules — not the summary. Obligations, notice requirements and time bars
   are extracted into an obligations register on day one, because a time bar
   discovered in month three has usually already passed.
3. **Information received and logged**, with the date each item arrived. Gaps
   are raised in writing immediately rather than at the first report.
4. **Key dates diarised** — payment notice dates, valuation dates, reporting
   dates, and every contractual deadline that carries a consequence.
5. **Site induction completed** and the access regime agreed, before the first
   inspection.
6. **Communication protocol agreed** — who instructs, who receives, what goes
   in writing, and how quickly.

## 4. Project Manager

**What the role does here.** Administers the contract, controls time, cost and
change, and gives the client a position they can act on rather than a narrative
of what happened.

**How it is delivered.**

- **Programme.** The contractor's programme is interrogated rather than
  received: logic checked for open ends and negative lag, critical path tested
  against the actual constraints, float ownership identified, and resource
  levels tested against the output rates the programme assumes. Findings are
  recorded and issued, and the same interrogation is repeated at each revision.
- **Change.** Every instruction, variation and potential change is registered
  on the day it arises, with its contractual basis, its status and its notice
  position. Where a contract bars a claim that is not noticed within a period,
  the bar date is on the register and is checked weekly.
- **Cost.** Committed cost, forecast final cost and the movement between
  reports are reported every period, with the reason for each movement stated.
  A forecast that moves without an explanation is not issued.
- **Risk.** A live risk register with owner, mitigation and cost and time
  exposure, reviewed at every progress meeting, not refreshed before reports.
- **Reporting.** A monthly report covering progress against programme, cost
  against budget, change, risk, and the decisions the client needs to make,
  with a date against each decision.

## 5. Employer's Agent

**What the role does here.** Acts for the employer in the administration of a
design and build contract, and protects the employer's position at the points
where it is actually lost — the Employer's Requirements, the Contractor's
Proposals, and payment.

**How it is delivered.**

- **Employer's Requirements against Contractor's Proposals.** A line-by-line
  comparison, producing a schedule of divergences with the commercial
  consequence of each. Divergences are resolved before contract where possible
  and recorded where not. This is where design and build contracts are won or
  lost and it is done properly or not at all.
- **Payment.** Interim valuations assessed against the contract's own
  mechanism, on the contract's dates. Payment notices and pay-less notices
  drafted to the contract's timetable, because a notice a day late has the same
  effect as no notice at all.
- **Instructions and change control.** Instructions issued only where the
  contract empowers them, valued under the contract's own rules, and recorded.
- **Extensions of time.** Assessed on the contractor's substantiation against
  the contract's relevant events, with the assessment reasoned in writing.
- **Completion and defects.** Practical completion assessed against the
  contract's definition rather than against pressure; the defects list issued
  with the certificate; the rectification period administered to its end and
  the final certificate issued on evidence.

## 6. Clerk of Works

**What the role does here.** Inspects the works on the employer's behalf and
reports what is there. The value of the role is that the report is
independent of the party building it.

**How it is delivered.**

- **Inspection regime agreed with the client** at mobilisation: frequency,
  the hold and witness points, and what triggers an additional visit.
- **Inspection against the specification and approved drawings**, with the
  revision of each document recorded on the report, so that an inspection can
  be re-read years later against what was current on the day.
- **Records.** Every visit produces a dated report with the weather, the
  operations in progress, the labour and plant on site, the work inspected, the
  observations made, photographs referenced to location, and the status of
  previous observations. Reports are issued within [two] working days of the
  visit — a site report issued a fortnight later has lost most of its use.
- **Non-conforming work** is recorded, notified to the contractor and the
  client on the day, tracked to closure, and reported open until it is closed.
  It is not closed on a verbal assurance.
- **Health and safety observations** are reported to the person in control of
  the site and recorded, under section 3.8 of the company's health and safety
  policy. The company inspects and reports; it does not direct other employers'
  operatives.
- **What the role does not do.** The clerk of works does not instruct
  variations, does not accept work on the employer's behalf beyond the
  authority given in writing, and does not relieve the contractor of its own
  obligation to build to the specification. That is stated to every client at
  appointment.

## 7. Associates and subcontracting

CHIC accepts suppliers who subcontract, provided the submission describes how
those relationships are managed. The company's arrangement:

- Associates are engaged by name, against evidenced competence, professional
  indemnity cover and record — never on availability alone.
- The associate receives the same written scope the client received.
- Every output an associate produces is checked by the company before issue,
  under section 3.3 of the quality management arrangements. The company's name
  on a document means the company has checked it.
- The client is told when an associate is used and who it is. The company does
  not present another party's work as its own.
- The company remains the contracting party and carries the liability. A client
  has one point of responsibility.

## 8. Records, confidentiality and handover

All project records are held for twelve years, covering the limitation period
for a contract executed as a deed, and are backed up rather than held on a
single device. Client information is treated as confidential and is not used on
another engagement. On completion or termination, the complete record is handed
over in a usable format at no additional charge — a client's own project
information is theirs.

## 9. Capacity, and when the company will decline

The company will decline an engagement where it cannot staff it, where it is
not competent to deliver it, or where accepting it would prevent it from
delivering an existing commitment. It will say so at the point of enquiry
rather than accepting and under-delivering.

This is stated because a one-person company that claims unlimited capacity
tells an assessor either that it has not thought about capacity or that it will
say what it needs to say to win work. Neither is a good answer, and the honest
one is more useful to CHIC than either.
