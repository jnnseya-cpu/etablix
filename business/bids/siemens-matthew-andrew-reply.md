# Siemens Energy — Matthew Knight's internal referral to Andrew

**What happened.** Matthew Knight at Siemens Energy (C A Parsons Works,
Newcastle) has forwarded ETABLIX internally to a colleague, Andrew, with one
line: *"This company specialises in site establishment for projects. Is it of
interest to you?"*

That is the second internal referral inside a fortnight, at a second tier-one
original equipment manufacturer. Hitachi Energy is live through Gary Coleman to
Jaime. This one is live through Matthew to Andrew.

---

## Read this before you write anything

**You hold private intelligence from a programme director at Siemens Energy,
and you are now inside Siemens Energy by referral.** Those two facts together
are the risk in this email.

Do not mention that exchange. Do not name the person. Do not reference the
conversation, quote it, allude to it, or use any part of it as evidence that
the proposition is sound. It was a private reply given to a former colleague,
not a reference offered for reuse, and using it inside his own organisation
would be using it as a sales lever — which is exactly what it must never be.

The observation itself is yours. You put the hypothesis; he agreed with it. So
describe it from your own GE Vernova record and let Andrew map it across, which
he will do in one line and by himself. **Say nothing about how Siemens Energy
prices or contracts this scope.** You have not been told that by anyone whose
permission you have to repeat it.

## Second check, before you reply

**Is this Andrew the same Andrew Miller whose national grid post you commented
on?** Andrew Miller is an Offshore Manager and Client Rep. It may well be
somebody else entirely, and Siemens Energy is large. But find out before you
send anything, because if it is the same person the two approaches need to read
as one person being consistent rather than two unrelated contacts arriving in
the same week.

## Who the ball is with

Matthew has asked Andrew a question. The next move is Andrew's, not yours. If
you pitch Andrew before he has answered Matthew, you step on the introduction
Matthew just made for you.

But Matthew has sent it to you, which is an invitation to be useful. The reply
therefore does one job: **give Andrew enough to answer Matthew's question
without needing a call.** Unlike the Hitachi thread — where Jaime was asked an
administrative question and substance would have been a pitch — Andrew is being
asked whether this is of interest, and that cannot be answered without knowing
what it is. That is the licence for one paragraph of substance, and no more.

## The reply — to Matthew, copying Andrew

> Matthew — thank you for passing this on.
>
> Andrew, so you can answer Matthew without needing a call: ETABLIX takes site
> establishment as one accountable package rather than several separate orders
> — compound, temporary power and water, welfare, waste, logistics and worker

>
> Where it tends to matter on turnkey work is that this scope sits inside the
> main contract from the day it is signed. It gets priced internally by
> engineers who have other jobs on, and then re-priced from scratch by whoever
> eventually takes it. I spent my career on that side of it — construction
> subcontract manager at GE Vernova, on EPC and full turnkey projects across the
> UK, Ireland and Europe, including a 36-month project carrying a 309-bed
> workforce village inside the turnkey scope.
>
> Happy to answer anything by email, or to go through supplier registration if
> that is the more useful first step. Either way I will not take more of your
> time than the answer needs.
>
> Kind regards,
> [signature block]

### Why each part is there

**"So you can answer Matthew without needing a call."** It tells Andrew the
purpose of the email in nine words and makes clear you are not trying to convert
a forward into a meeting. It also does Matthew a favour, which is the person who
took the risk.

**One sentence of what it is, in plain scope words.** Compound, power, water,
welfare, waste, logistics, accommodation. No model names, no product names, no
platform. Andrew needs to recognise a scope, not learn a brand.

**The second paragraph is the mechanism and it is the strongest thing in the
email** — priced internally by people with other jobs, then re-priced by
whoever takes it. It describes GE Vernova and says nothing about Siemens Energy.
Andrew will make the connection himself, and his conclusion is worth more than
your claim would be.

**The credential is one clause with two checkable numbers.** 36 months, 309
beds. A job title at a named employer, not an adjective.

**Two exits, one of them administrative.** Answering by email or going through
supplier registration both give Andrew an easy positive answer to Matthew.
Registration is the lower-friction yes and it needs nobody's goodwill, which is
why it is offered.

**"I will not take more of your time than the answer needs."** The line that
gets replies. You have promised to be brief and to go away.

## What must not be in it

- **Nothing from the private Siemens Energy exchange.** See above. This is the
  one rule that cannot be bent for any commercial reason.
- **No price, no rate, no fee.** Not in a first email into an organisation.
- **No attachment.** Nobody asked. If Andrew wants detail he will ask, and the
  policy set is ready for when he does.
- **No claim of a company delivery record.** The record is yours personally and
  the email says so by naming the employer. Never blur that.
- **No mention of the Hitachi Energy referral.** Two tier-one OEMs are
  competitors. Telling one that the other is looking at you is both indiscreet
  and unnecessary.

## Then update the ledger

Two live internal referrals at two tier-one OEMs, from two cold approaches
neither of which asked for one. That is the outreach working, and it is worth
recording as such — the ledger existed because a run carrying replies felt like
a run carrying none.
