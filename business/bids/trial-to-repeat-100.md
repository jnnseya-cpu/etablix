# "Barriers from trial to repeat deployment" — 100 words

A different question from the earlier barriers field. That one was about
getting in at all. This is about what stops a successful trial becoming a
second one, which is where most pilots in this sector die.

The honest answer is that a trial is bought by one person out of a discretionary
budget and a repeat deployment has to survive procurement, assurance and
somebody's annual plan.

---

## The answer — 100 words

> Three.
>
> **Pilot purgatory.** A trial is bought by one sponsor from a discretionary
> budget. A repeat crosses into procurement, needs a contract vehicle, and waits
> for a budget cycle. Many trials succeed and stop there.
>
> **Assurance at the second gate.** Nobody audits a trial. A deployment brings
> ISO/IEC 27001, Cyber Essentials Plus and penetration testing — which CONSTRUX
> does not yet hold, so success at trial creates the very requirement that
> blocks repeat.
>
> **Sponsor mobility.** The person who championed the trial moves, and the
> relationship does not transfer. Without the result written into a document
> their successor inherits, it restarts.

---

## Why these three

**"Pilot purgatory" names the thing everybody in this sector has watched
happen.** A trial is one person's discretionary decision; a deployment is an
organisational one. Naming the transition as the barrier, rather than the
technology, tells an assessor you have thought past the demonstration they are
funding.

**The second one is the sharpest and it is deliberately self-incriminating.**
Succeeding at trial is what creates the certification requirement that then
blocks the repeat. Saying that plainly does two things: it shows you understand
the assurance ladder, and it makes the standards-gap answer earlier in the form
read as a plan rather than an admission. The two fields now support each other.

**Sponsor mobility is the one applicants forget.** Construction and
infrastructure move people constantly. A result that lives in a relationship
dies with the relationship; a result written into a document the successor
inherits survives. It is also a quiet argument for the product — a system of
record is exactly how a result outlives its sponsor.

**It names CONSTRUX once, in the sentence where the platform is the subject.**
Not in the other two, which are organisational rather than technical.

## Before you paste it

**Keep "which CONSTRUX does not yet hold".** The temptation is to soften it to
"which we are pursuing". Do not. The unhedged version is what makes the
paragraph credible, and the standards field has already said the same thing.

**If the form places this next to the earlier barriers question**, check the two
do not repeat. They should not — that one is about entry, this is about
repetition — but read them together once before submitting.
