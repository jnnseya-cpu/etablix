# "Describe your solution" — 250 words

For the application form field: *what it does, how it works, and how it
addresses the challenge. Focus on the mechanism.*

**It answers as one solution, not three products.** The field asks for a
mechanism, and three separate pitches crammed into 250 words reads as a company
that could not say what its mechanism is. One mechanism expressed at three
levels reads as a company that knows.

**It claims no deployment.** Every sentence describes how the thing works, not
where it has run. That is what the question asked for, and it is the only
version of this answer that survives a check.

---

## The answer — 246 words

> The mechanism is boundary ownership, applied at three levels.
>
> Infrastructure productivity is lost at the gaps between packages, not inside
> them. On EPC and turnkey projects the civils, site management and site
> infrastructure are priced from first principles on every job, handed to
> sourcing, then repriced by whoever takes them — while the interfaces between
> power, water, welfare and logistics belong to nobody. Rework, variations and
> standing time follow from that, not from poor workmanship.
>
> ETABLIX (etablix.com) closes the gap contractually. Site establishment
> becomes one accountable package — compound, temporary power and water,
> welfare, waste, logistics, accommodation — with a single owner and a defined
> boundary at every interface, agreed before packages are let rather than
> argued after.
>
> CONSTRUX (construxvg.com) closes it in data. Commercial, programme, site,
> safety, supplier and information control sit in one system of record, so the
> project position is computed rather than reassembled each month.
> Deterministic engines run before any AI model and produce the findings; the
> model writes the report from them and cannot recompute them, so every output
> traces to the rule and record behind it.
>
> VERYX (veryxjnn.com) closes it at portfolio level. The same governed data
> rolls up, so executives see what projects see and a risk raised on site
> reaches the board without retyping.
>
> Each works alone. Together they form one chain: the boundary defined at
> package stage becomes the data measured on site and the position reported to
> the board, with no reconciliation step and nowhere for a gap to hide.

## Why it is built this way

**The first line is the answer.** An assessor reading forty of these decides in
one sentence whether there is a mechanism here or a description of software.
"Boundary ownership, applied at three levels" is a mechanism, and everything
after it is evidence for that sentence.

**The diagnosis is not ours alone.** The framing — that the civils are rarely
the problem and the interfaces are — came back unprompted from a programme
director at Siemens Energy and was independently described by a subcontract
development manager at Hitachi Energy. Do not name either of them, and do not
attribute it. But it is why the paragraph reads as observed rather than
invented, and it will read that way to anybody who has run these projects.

**The CONSTRUX paragraph is the technical differentiator.** Deterministic
engines running before the model, with the model forbidden from recomputing the
findings, is an architectural claim about auditability rather than a claim about
AI. Assessors are reading a great many applications that say "AI-powered". Very
few say what stops the AI from being wrong.

**The last paragraph answers the unasked question.** Any assessor reading three
product names wonders whether this is one thing or a portfolio being passed off
as one. "Each works alone. Together they form one chain" settles it before they
have to ask.

## Before you paste it

**Re-read the challenge statement above the field and make the second paragraph
echo its language.** If the form frames the challenge as productivity, keep
"productivity". If it frames it as cost certainty, programme risk or fragmented
supply chains, change that one word to match. An answer that uses the
assessor's own word for the problem scores better than a better answer that
does not, and it is a thirty-second edit.

**Check the word count in the form, not here.** Forms count differently —
hyphenated words, the em dashes, the URLs. Paste it, read the counter, and cut
from the first paragraph if you need to.

**Do not add a sentence claiming projects delivered.** The company has none
under its own name yet. This answer never claims one, and adding one to
strengthen it would be the sentence that loses the application rather than the
one that wins it.
