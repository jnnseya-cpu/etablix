## Why applying anyway would have been a mistake

Not because rejection costs anything in itself. Because of what it costs
elsewhere.

**An application that plainly fails the published criteria is read as not
having read them.** At a selection stage that is the first competence being
assessed, and the catapult world is small. Connected Places Catapult runs many
programmes, and the same small team reads across them.

**The £8,000 is not the prize and should not be the motive.** It is a
demonstration budget for a solution that already has buyers to demonstrate to.
Chasing it without the demonstration to give is how a company spends a week
producing an application that argues against itself.

**There is an opportunity cost measured in the only currency that is short
here.** The hours this would have taken are hours not spent on the CHIC
submission and the RM6242 questionnaire, both of which this company can
genuinely win and one of which has a financial assessment that needs real work.

## What the criteria are actually worth

Read them as a specification rather than as a rejection. They describe, in
somebody else's words, what "ready" looks like:

- **One real project with evidence.** This is the single unlock. It satisfies
  the TRL bar, it satisfies the evidence bar, and it converts every application
  this company makes from an argument into a record. Everything else follows
  from it.
- **£500k to £1m revenue, or equivalent traction through public contracts.**
  Note the second half. Admission to a DPS and work won through it is the
  stated alternative to revenue — which makes the CHIC and RM6242 applications
  the direct route to eligibility here, not a detour from it.
- **TRL 8.** Reached by running the platform on a live engagement, not by
  building more of it.

## What is worth doing instead, at no cost

1. **Register as an SME with Connected Places Catapult.** Their SME pages and
   open calls list carry every future opportunity, and being on the list costs
   nothing and needs no eligibility. This is the version of "registering" that
   is worth the time, and it is about ten minutes.
2. **Watch for this programme running again.** It is described as a pilot.
   Pilots that work are repeated, and a repeat would land when this company
   might actually qualify.
3. **Look at the earlier-stage calls instead.** The Catapult also runs
   feasibility-level competitions — the Transport Research and Innovation Grants
   competition was open at the same time as this — which are aimed at ideas
   rather than at proven revenue. Those are the right shelf for a company at
   this stage. Check the eligibility on each before spending an hour on it.
4. **Never pay to apply.** This programme is free, which is normal and correct
   for a catapult. Anything of this shape that asks for a fee is not the same
   kind of thing.

## The decision rule this leaves behind

Before any programme, grant or accelerator, check three things in this order,
and stop at the first failure:

1. **The deadline.** Before reading anything else. An hour spent assessing a
   closed call is an hour gone.
2. **The hardest eligibility number** — revenue, headcount, TRL, or a required
   reference. If the company misses it by a distance rather than a hair, stop.
   Eligibility criteria for funded programmes are administered strictly,
   because they are audited.
3. **The cost, in both directions.** Any fee to apply or to be listed is a
   refusal. And the hours are a real cost, chargeable against the applications
   that can actually be won.

Applied here, this stops at step one.
