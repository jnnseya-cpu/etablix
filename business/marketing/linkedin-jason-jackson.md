# LinkedIn — Jason Jackson, Programme Director, Siemens Energy

He is a 2nd-degree connection, so this is two messages, not one. The
connection request note is capped at **300 characters** and its only job is to
get accepted. The real message goes after he accepts.

Why he is worth the effort: a Programme Director at Siemens Energy carries the
same structural problem you carried at GE Vernova. On EPC and turnkey
contracts the civils, the site management and the site infrastructure sit
inside the scope of a business that is not a site-establishment business.
That is not a pitch you have to construct — it is a description of his job,
and he will know within one line whether you have lived it.

---

## Step 1 — the connection request note

Pick one. All three are inside the 300-character limit; the counts are exact.

**Option A — the peer opening (297 characters).** Recommended.

> Jason — I spent my career on the construction side at GE Vernova: turnkey
> jobs where the civils and site infrastructure sat inside our scope. I've
> since built a business doing that one piece of work. Not selling anything —
> I'd value being connected to someone doing the same job at Siemens Energy.

**Option B — the short one (262 characters).**

> Jason — ex-GE Vernova construction side, on turnkey contracts where the
> civils and site infrastructure sat inside our scope. I now do that piece of
> work for others. Would value the connection with someone running programmes
> on the same problem at Siemens Energy.

**Option C — opens with the mechanism (265 characters).** The most likely to
get a reply and the most likely to be read as a sales approach. Use it if you
would rather have a conversation than a connection.

> Jason — I ran construction subcontracts at GE Vernova on turnkey jobs where
> site establishment sat inside our scope: priced in-house, then handed to
> sourcing to find a civils contractor. Curious whether it still works that
> way your side. Would value the connection.

**Do not** attach a pitch, a link or a document to the request. One line about
who you are, one line about why him. The note is a door, not a room.

---

## Step 2 — the message, once he accepts

Send it the day after he accepts, not the same minute. Same-minute is a
sequence; a day later is a person.

> Thanks for connecting, Jason.
>
> I'll be direct about why I got in touch, because you will have worked out
> that it wasn't at random.
>
> On the turnkey work I did at GE Vernova, the civils, the site management and
> the site infrastructure were inside our contract from the day it was signed —
> and on one project the worker accommodation as well. Every time, the same
> thing happened. Engineers who had other jobs priced the site establishment
> from first principles. People flew out to survey it and wrote it up. Sourcing
> then went looking for a civils contractor, who re-priced the whole thing from
> scratch anyway. An equipment and EPC business is not a site-establishment
> business, so that scope got built from nothing on every single job.
>
> I left to do that one piece of work properly, and I'd genuinely value your
> read on two things:
>
> Is it still done that way — priced in-house at bid stage, then handed to
> sourcing to find someone to own it? And when it goes wrong on site, is it
> usually the civils themselves, or the interfaces around them: power, water,
> welfare, accommodation, logistics, the things nobody's package quite covered?
>
> No pitch attached. I'm building the thing and I would rather hear from
> somebody who lives with the problem than keep guessing at it.

That is the whole message. It ends on a question he can answer in three lines
from his phone, and it asks for his opinion rather than his budget.

---

## Step 3 — if he replies

Answer his answer first, properly, before you say anything about ETABLIX. He
has given you his time; spending it on a pitch is the fastest way to lose the
second reply.

Only if he asks what you do:

> Short version: a fixed-fee report, ten working days, on a live bid or a
> project about to mobilise. It reads the client's own information pack against
> itself and says where the site establishment doesn't hold — the consents
> nobody worked backwards from, the welfare sized to the wrong headcount, the
> packages with no owner. The appendix is a list of which of their own
> documents contradict which. Happy to send the scope if it's ever useful.

## If he does not reply

One follow-up, at most, two weeks later, and only if you have something new to
say — a post of yours he might have seen, or a genuine question. Then stop.
A Programme Director at Siemens Energy is worth being patient about, and the
one thing that would close the door permanently is chasing.

---

## Three things to keep out of it

- **Any suggestion you know Siemens Energy's business.** You know GE Vernova's.
  Describe that, and let him map it across — he will, immediately, and it will
  be his conclusion rather than your claim.
- **Confidential detail from your old employer.** Your role, the shape of the
  scope and the pattern are yours to talk about. Project values, supplier
  names and commercial terms are not.
- **A capability statement, a link, or a deck in the first message.** It turns
  a conversation between two people who do the same job into a sales sequence,
  and he has met a hundred of those.
