# Facebook carousel: subcontractor register

## A correction to the previous pack

The Diagnostic carousel said paid subcontractor recruitment was not worth
spending on. On reflection that was the wrong call, and **this is the better
Facebook campaign of the two.**

**The audience is actually on Facebook.** Owners of small civils, fencing, plant
hire, welfare, waste and modular businesses use it daily. Commercial directors at
tier-one contractors do not. Meta cannot target a job title, but it reaches a
small business owner in the Midlands with an interest in construction plant very
cheaply — and that is exactly who should be on the register.

**And the register is an asset that compounds.** CHIC returned the application
for insufficient detail on *delivery capability*. A named, prequalified
subcontractor with current insurances on file, in a category matching the scope
being bid, is a direct answer to that — verifiable by telephone. Three
procurement threads need it. It is the only thing available right now that is
cheap to build and gets more valuable the longer it runs.

**The client carousel moves to LinkedIn**, where the buyer actually is.

## The constraint, and it is harder in a paid ad

**There are no live packages, and the carousel has to say so — on a card, not
buried on the landing page.** An organic post that is honest and an ad that is
not is worse than never running the ad. Card 4 exists for this and must not be
cut to improve conversion.

The honesty is also the hook. Every subcontractor reading this has filled in
registrations that went nowhere. Leading with respect for their time is the one
thing none of their other registrations did.

## The sequence

| Card | Image | Overlay |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A stack of completed supplier questionnaires on a van dashboard | `Another form. Another silence.` |
| 2 | A compound going in — cabins craned, fencing up | `We buy the temporary.` |
| 3 | The permanent structure rising behind it | `Not the permanent.` |
| 4 | The empty field, before anything | `Nothing live today.` |
| 5 | A phone ringing on a van dashboard | `Be on the list first.` |

**Cards 2 and 3 are a pair and the swipe is the point** — the contrast does the
filtering that three paragraphs of explanation would otherwise have to do.

**Card 4 is the honest one.** It is also the one that makes cards 1 and 5
believable.

## The house look — in every prompt

Deliberately warmer and more workmanlike than the client carousel. These are
people being invited in, not a buyer being shown a loss.

> Shot on a 35mm lens, available light, bright overcast UK daylight. Working
> palette of hi-vis orange, galvanised steel, fresh timber, wet gravel and
> grey-white sky. Documentary realism, mild grain, nothing staged or glossy.
> Square 1:1 with the subject off centre and clear sky or flat surface at the top
> for a text overlay.

> **Negative, every card:** no logos, no company names, no readable signage, no
> legible text or numbers, no identifiable real location, no faces in sharp
> focus, no HDR, no lens flare, no stock-photo smiling, no illustration, no 3D
> render, no watermark.

## The copy prompt

> You are writing a five-card Meta carousel for ETABLIX — Integrated Site
> Services, a trading name of JNN GLOBAL LTD — inviting subcontractors onto its
> specialist supply chain register at
> https://etablix.com/subcontractors#register
>
> **Who is reading.** The owner or director of a small or medium UK subcontracting
> business: civils and groundworks, fencing, temporary power, water and
> wastewater, modular buildings, welfare, plant hire, security, catering,
> cleaning, waste, traffic management, workforce transport. They are practical,
> time-poor, and have filled in prequalification forms that led nowhere. They do
> not respond to corporate language. They respond to being told precisely what a
> buyer buys.
>
> **What ETABLIX buys, and this distinction is the whole ad.** ETABLIX is not a
> main contractor and does not tender permanent works. It buys the temporary site
> environment around the permanent works — the compound, welfare, utilities,
> access, operation and reinstatement. If they build the structure, ETABLIX is not
> their client. If they establish, service, operate or remove the site around it,
> ETABLIX is.
>
> **Who their customer would be, if asked:** under the Management Integrator model
> the contract is placed in the client's name and ETABLIX runs the enquiry,
> evaluation, award recommendation and performance management. Under the Prime
> Service Contractor model ETABLIX contracts with every supplier directly and is
> the paying party. Which applies depends on the engagement.
>
> **The five cards, in order:** (1) registrations that went nowhere; (2) a
> compound being established — what we buy; (3) a permanent structure — what we
> do not; (4) an empty field, nothing live today; (5) a phone ringing, be on the
> list before the enquiry comes.
>
> **Produce:**
> - **Primary text:** 50 to 70 words. The first sentence must work alone, because
>   the rest sits behind "see more".
> - **Five card headlines**, 5 words maximum each, following the sequence.
> - **Five card descriptions**, 20 characters maximum each.
> - **One call to action on card 5 only:** Sign Up, Learn More or Apply Now.
>
> **Absolute constraints — breaking any makes the ad unusable:**
> - **Card 4 must state plainly that there are no live packages at present.** Do
>   not soften it, do not replace it with "limited opportunities", and do not
>   remove it. An honest organic post followed by a dishonest ad is worse than no
>   ad.
> - **Never promise work, imply a pipeline, or suggest packages are imminent.**
>   Registration is a route to the enquiry, not a guarantee of work, and the copy
>   must be capable of being read a year later without embarrassment.
> - **Never invent a figure** — no number of projects, suppliers, sites or values.
>   None exist.
> - **Never name a client, a scheme or a former employer.**
> - **Never claim an accreditation.** ETABLIX holds no ISO certification and no
>   SSIP registration.
> - **Say "defined packages, not labour-only"** somewhere in the primary text.
> - **No exclamation marks**, and none of: exciting, opportunity of a lifetime,
>   join the journey, partner with us, world-class, leading, unlock.
>
> **Register.** Plain, direct, British English. Short sentences. Trade nouns —
> compound, welfare, hardstanding, off-hire, reinstatement, enquiry. Respect the
> reader's time in the first line and the ad has already done something none of
> their other registrations did.

## Targeting and spend

**This is where Meta is actually good.** Target UK small business owners with
interests in construction plant, groundworks, plant hire, trade suppliers and the
trade press. Layer on business-owner and small-business-admin behaviours. Radius
targeting is less useful here than for home services: a subcontractor who travels
to site is reachable nationally.

**Expect cheap clicks and a low conversion rate, and accept it.** A registration
form asking for insurances and accreditations is a high-friction conversion by
design — it should be. The register is only worth something if the people on it
are real.

**And answer every registration the same day**, from the template at
`business/marketing/supply-chain-reply-template.md`. Paying for a registration
and then leaving it unanswered is the most expensive mistake available here.
