## The problem with this one, and the technique that fixes it

**It is a roundup, not a post.** There is no argument to engage with and no
claim to extend. A comment under a corporate newsletter link is filler, and
filler under a 400,000-follower page is filler in front of a lot of people.

**But the reach is the best of the day.** Twenty-two hours old on a page that
size means the comment will actually be seen.

**So do not comment on the newsletter. Comment on ONE item in it.** Pick the
item you have something real to say about, name it, and say the thing. That
reads as somebody who opened the newsletter, which is exactly what the page
wants and almost nobody does.

The item to pick is North West London. It is UK, it is urban, and the site
problem there is one we can speak to without inventing anything.

## Why the urban point is the right one

The site-establishment problem scales in two directions, and most people only
think about one. Remote and linear projects are hard because there is nothing
there. Dense urban projects are hard because there is no room, and that is
the less discussed half:

- the compound footprint is whatever the highway authority will let you close
- welfare has to be compliant on that footprint, not on a sensible one
- storage is off-site, so deliveries become a scheduling system rather than a
  delivery date
- traffic management, noise consents and working hours drive the programme
  more than the electrical works do
- the neighbours are a stakeholder with a real veto

None of that is a claim about National Grid's work. It is what the work is.

## National Grid as a target, for the record

**Client side, not contractor side.** National Grid is a transmission owner.
It buys through framework delivery partners rather than engaging small
suppliers directly for construction, so the realistic route is the delivery
partner, not the page that posted this.

**The Great Grid Upgrade** is National Grid's publicly branded UK
transmission programme and it is the largest pipeline of this kind of work in
the country. Its delivery partners are named publicly. Those are the
approachable parties.

**Two honest routes:**

1. **The delivery partners**, as a Tier 2 or 3 supplier on the site
   establishment for a specific project. This is the realistic one.
2. **Advisory to the owner**, on establishment and accommodation strategy
   across a programme rather than a project. Higher value, much longer sales
   cycle, and it needs a credential we do not yet have on a scheme of that
   size. Worth naming as an ambition, not as a pitch.

**Never pay to be on anybody's list.** National Grid and its partners run
free supplier registration. Anything charging for inclusion is selling a
list, not access.

**What must not be claimed:** no transmission or distribution network
experience, no HV competence, and nothing that implies ETABLIX could hold a
CDM 2015 Principal Contractor role on a network project. The compound, the
welfare, the accommodation and the logistics around the works are the offer.
The works themselves are not.
