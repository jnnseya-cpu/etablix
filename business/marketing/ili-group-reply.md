## What ILI actually is, from the public record

A Scottish developer that takes energy storage schemes through land rights,
consent and grid connection, and then sells them. Pumped storage hydro and
utility-scale battery. The pattern is visible in their own announcements: the
450 MW Red John scheme at Loch Ness, later Loch na Cathrach, consented in 2021
and sold to Statkraft; 50 MW battery sites sold to Eelpower, to Field, and to
JLEN with Foresight; a second tranche of around 350 MW of Scottish battery sites
released for sale in 2025 with connection dates from 2026 to 2028. The largest
in the portfolio is Balliemeanoch at Loch Awe, a Section 36 application for
around 1.5 GW with a five-year construction programme, AECOM engaged as technical
consultants.

**So Chris is correct, and the introduction was aimed at the wrong stage.** The
original email described three ways to engage on a construction-phase scope. ILI
does not hold a construction-phase scope. Arguing with him would be both wrong
and obvious.

**But the thread is not closed, for two reasons.** First, ILI is not the buyer —
the acquirer is, and the acquirers are a matter of public record. Second, there
is one piece of ETABLIX work that genuinely belongs at the development stage
rather than the construction stage, and it is worth one short paragraph.

## Why it is written this way

**Conceding the point in the first line buys the rest of the email.** He gave a
courteous brush-off to a message that misread his business. A reply that argues,
or that re-sends the three models, confirms he was right to brush it off. A reply
that says *you are right and I aimed at the wrong stage* earns the one paragraph
that follows.

**Only Model 01 appears.** Advisory is the only one of the three that a
pre-construction developer can buy, because it is the only one that does not
require a construction contract to exist. Repeating Models 02 and 03 to a
developer is the original mistake made twice.

**The real prize is the question at the end, not the offer in the middle.** ILI
develops and sells; the acquirer builds. Statkraft, Field, Eelpower, JLEN and
Foresight have all taken ILI schemes on, and every one of them is a better
prospect for ETABLIX than ILI itself. Chris can answer the question honestly
without disclosing anything, and the answer reorganises the whole pipeline entry:
the developer is a signpost, not a customer.

**The accommodation argument is made as a valuation point, not a safety or
compliance point.** A developer whose entire business is the sale price of a
consented scheme cares about what an acquirer deducts for. That is the register
his attention is in.

**What the reply withholds.** No price. No statistic and no case study — the
cost-of-a-gap argument is stated as a tendency, not a number, because no number
exists that can be stood behind. No client, scheme or former employer of the
director is named. No accreditation is claimed. Nothing implies a Principal
Designer or Principal Contractor appointment, and the advisory route is stated as
leaving every contract with the buyer precisely so it cannot be read that way.
Their own projects are referred to only in the terms they have themselves
announced publicly.

## What to do after sending

Treat ILI as a signpost and work the acquirers directly, whatever Chris answers.
The parties who have bought ILI schemes are public, and an owner who has just
acquired a consented multi-year build in a remote location is at exactly the
moment when the site establishment question becomes theirs and nobody has yet
answered it. That is a better-aimed approach than the one that produced this
reply, and it came out of a message that failed.

**So the ask sits entirely on the public side of the line.** Once the press

release distribution costs him a click and no risk at all. That is a request he
can grant immediately without consulting anybody, which is the only kind worth
making of somebody who has just explained his constraint.

## Why it is written this way

**The NDA sentence is the most important line in the email.** He raised a
confidentiality obligation unprompted. Naming it back, and saying plainly that
nothing is wanted before completion, is what makes the request that follows
easy to grant. A reply that skated past it would have read as someone looking
for a way round.

**The ask is deliberately trivial.** A line to an address, or a name on a
distribution list. No meeting, no call, no introduction to arrange, no judgement
for him to make about whether we are any good. He can do it without asking
permission, which is why he probably will.

**The timing paragraph is the only selling in the letter, and it is aimed at
him, not at the acquirer.** It tells him why the favour is worth doing — the
window is weeks, not months — without claiming anything about ETABLIX. It is
also true: once a contractor is appointed, the site establishment is inside
somebody else's contract and the conversation is a different, worse one.

**What it withholds.** No price. No acquirer named, even though the past ones
are public, because showing homework at this point would look like we are
working around his NDA rather than respecting it. No promise and no pipeline
claim. No accreditation. Nothing implying a CDM duty holder appointment.
