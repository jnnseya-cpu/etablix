# LinkedIn comment — Christian Bruch, Siemens Energy, the Iraq framework

**Post:** Christian Bruch, President and CEO of Siemens Energy, 2nd degree. A
strategic framework signed with the Iraqi Ministry of Electricity during a
state visit to Germany, covering generation, transmission and long-term
services, with the potential to add up to 14 GW.

## Read the object before writing the comment

This is not the Samuel Neadley post and it cannot be handled the same way. It
is a chief executive announcing a state-level agreement, photographed with two
heads of government. The comment section will be read by the Siemens Energy
communications team, by his network of peers, and by anyone inside the company
who follows him.

Three constraints follow, and they are absolute.

**1. No pitch. None.** A supplier angling for work under a photograph of a
state signing looks opportunistic, and that impression does not wash off. The
comment mentions no service, no company, no capability and makes no ask.

**2. Nothing from the private Siemens Energy exchanges goes anywhere near
this.** Jason Jackson's reply is not a lever and never becomes one. Matthew
Knight's referral to Andrew is live and unanswered. A comment that reads as
pitching on the CEO's post would embarrass the person who made that
introduction, which is the single most expensive mistake available here.

**3. No claim of capability in Iraq, and no comment on Iraq.** ETABLIX is a UK
business and cannot service an Iraqi programme; implying otherwise would be a
false claim. Nothing is said about Iraqi politics, the state visit, Germany, or
any party to the agreement. The comment stays entirely on delivery
engineering. The Prime Minister is not named and the geopolitics are not
touched.

Do not correct anything in the post. Not a name, not a figure, not a framing.
There is no version of correcting a chief executive on his own announcement
that ends well.

## Why comment at all

A post like this collects several hundred variations of "Congratulations,
Christian!". One comment carrying an actual delivery thought is visible in that
field, and it reads as an industry voice rather than a supplier — which is
exactly the impression worth having inside Siemens Energy while a referral is
sitting in someone's inbox.

The hook is in his own text: he listed **long-term services** as part of the
value chain, which most announcements of this kind leave out. That is the
thread to pull, and pulling it is agreement, not correction.

## The comment

> The services leg is the one that decides whether this ends up as resilience
> or just capacity.
>
> Generation and transmission can be delivered by a mobilised project team.
> Long-term services cannot — they need a permanent local base, technicians
> trained on the specific installed fleet, and a spares and logistics chain
> that outlives the handover. That is a build in its own right, and it runs on
> a longer clock than the plants do.
>
> And 14 GW is not one project; it is a great many simultaneous mobilisations.
> A framework converts into megawatts at the rate sites can be established,
> rather than the rate equipment can be shipped. Encouraging that this one is
> written across the whole chain and not just the generation end of it.

## Why it is shaped this way

- **It opens on his own word.** He wrote "resilience". The first line turns
  that into the question the whole framework will be judged on, and it is the
  part LinkedIn shows before "see more".
- **It agrees with the post's most distinctive feature** — that the framework
  spans the whole value chain — instead of adding a caveat. Nobody is
  corrected and nothing is claimed to have been overlooked.
- **The insight is real and it is his own field, not ours.** Frameworks convert
  to installed capacity at the rate of mobilisation, not the rate of
  manufacture. Anyone who has run enabling works on a turnkey programme knows
  it; most people commenting will not say it.
- **"Established" does the work silently.** It is the only word in the comment
  that touches ETABLIX's ground, it is the natural word for the sentence, and
  it is doing nothing more than being accurate.
- **No figures added.** The 14 GW is his.

## If someone from Siemens Energy replies

Answer the engineering point and stop there. Do not convert a reply on a CEO's
post into a conversation about work — not in the thread, not in a follow-up
comment. If somebody opens the door, it moves to a direct message, and even
then it is a conversation about UK schemes, because that is what the company
can actually deliver.

Matthew Knight's referral is the live route into Siemens Energy. This comment
exists to make that route easier, not to compete with it.
