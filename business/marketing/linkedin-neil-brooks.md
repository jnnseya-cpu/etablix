# LinkedIn — Neil Brooks, Managing Director, Root-Power

**Of every contact on this list, this is the one with the most to work with.**

He was your line manager at UK Power Reserve for the 628MW programme — thirty-
odd new-build generation and storage sites across England and Wales — and you
ran risk and change, then pre-construction, on it. That means two things no
other contact gives you:

**He has seen you do the exact work.** Everywhere else you are describing your
record. With Neil you are recalling something he watched. Nobody needs to take
your word for anything.

**Those sites are the use case.** Thirty-plus distributed sites, built one
after another, each needing access, a compound, temporary power and water,
welfare, security and logistics before anything of value happens. That is not
an analogy for what ETABLIX does — it is the thing itself, and he ran the
programme that did it thirty times.

So the message can be direct and warm at once. One caution: **do not tell him
what went wrong on his own programme.** You were the junior; he ran it. Put the
pattern to him as a question and let him confirm, correct or dismiss it. If he
confirms it, the confirmation is worth ten times more than the assertion — and
if he corrects you, you have learned something about your own market from
somebody who knows.

## Why it is shaped like that

**"I'd value your read more than your time"** sets the ask at the right level.
You are not asking for work from a former manager in the first message; you
are asking whether you have understood the problem correctly.

**"Am I remembering that right, or is that hindsight tidying it up?"** is the
most important sentence in the message. It puts a claim about his programme in
his gift rather than in your mouth. He can confirm it — in which case you have
a validated origin story from the person who ran the programme — or correct
it, which is more useful still.

**The Root-Power question is genuine and open.** You do not know his business
and should not pretend to. Asking what he is building is how you find out
whether he is a client, a route, or neither, without proposing anything.

## What his answer could be worth

**If he confirms the pattern** — ask if he would say so on the record. A line
from a former Head of Projects with a 628MW portfolio behind him, describing
the problem in his own words, is the most credible thing you could put in front
of a client, and you have no delivery record of your own yet. Do not push if he
hesitates: a private confirmation is still worth having.

**If Root-Power builds sites** — that is a potential first engagement, and the
best possible one, because he already knows how you work. Let him raise it. If
he asks what it would look like:

> A fixed fee, ten working days, on a site or a portfolio about to mobilise.
> We read the information pack against itself and report where the site
> establishment doesn't hold — consents nobody worked backwards from, welfare
> and accommodation sized against a headcount that has moved, packages with no
> owner — with an appendix listing which of your own documents contradict
> which. You keep it whatever you decide to do next.

**If he offers an introduction** — take it, and report back afterwards.

## What to keep out

- **Any criticism of how UKPR ran.** You were in charge of risk and change
  under him. Describing failures in his programme, to him, is the one thing
  that would cost you this relationship.
- **Confidential UKPR or GE Vernova detail.** Your role, the scale and the
  shape are yours to describe. Contract values, supplier names and commercial
  terms are not, and Root-Power is in the same sector as some of them.
- **A pitch before he asks.** He knows your work. Let that do the selling and
  keep the first message to a question.
