# Public copy update — September 2026

New marketing material, drawn from the policy documents and the funding
application written this month. Everything here is concrete, checkable and
earned; nothing asserts a delivery record, quotes an unsourced statistic or
publishes a price.

## What was applied

### 1. Homepage — a new section: "Six decisions taken on paper, months before anyone arrives"

`frontend/public/index.html`, inserted before the sectors section.

**This is the most persuasive thing on the site, and it is the only part that
demonstrates expertise rather than asserting it.** Every other section makes an
argument. This one shows six specification decisions a practitioner would know
and a competitor's marketing department would not:

| Written into the specification | What it decides on site |
|---|---|
| Blackout, acoustic separation, controllable ventilation | Whether a night shift worker can sleep at two in the afternoon |
| Where accommodation sits relative to the gate | An hour each way adds two hours to every shift, unpriced |
| Catering hours against shift hours | A canteen shut before the late shift returns |
| Protective equipment sized to the person, not the average | Equipment that does not fit does not protect |
| Lighting on the routes people walk at shift change | Slips happen on the unlit shortcut, not at the lit work face |
| Welfare siting relative to the work face | Facilities that cost a break in walking stop being used |

Closing line: *"None of these is expensive on paper. Every one of them is
expensive on site. That gap is the whole argument for bringing the
specification and the delivery under one owner."*

Buyers do not believe claims. They recognise details, and these are details
only somebody who has stood on the compound at shift change would write.

### 2. CONSTRUX — the auditability differentiator

`frontend/public/construx.html`, two new checklist items.

**"The engine runs before the model."** Sixteen deterministic engines produce
the findings; the AI writes the report from them and cannot recompute them, so
every output traces to the rule and the record behind it. Closing line:
*"Plenty of platforms say AI-powered. Very few can tell you what stops the AI
being wrong."*

**"Engines refuse rather than warn."** A claimed change on a contract that bars
it, with no notice on file, is rejected rather than flagged. *"A warning nobody
reads is a control that does not exist."*

This is the sharpest competitive line the company has, and it was on no page
before today. Every competitor claims AI. Naming the architectural constraint
that makes the output trustworthy is a claim almost none of them can copy
without rebuilding.

### 3. VERYX — bitemporal state

`frontend/public/veryx.html`, added to the boardroom section.

*"What was known, and when. VERYX holds the position bitemporally: not only
what is true today, but what the record showed on any past date. In a contract
dispute that is the question that actually decides it, and it is the one most
reporting systems cannot answer."*

Aimed squarely at anyone who has been in a dispute. It converts a technical
property into the commercial consequence that makes it matter.

## Available and not yet applied

Strong lines produced this month that have no home on the site yet. Each is
true and none needs evidence the company does not have.

**On the proposition, for the homepage hero or About:**
> Productivity is lost at the gaps between packages, not inside them.

> One owner instead of six orders, with the gaps between them belonging to
> nobody.

**On the interface register, for the H&S or how-it-works page:**
> The interface register is the most useful safety document we produce, and it
> is almost never recognised as one. Most site-establishment incidents happen
> at a boundary nobody owned.

**On temporary power, for the what-we-offer page:**
> A generator sized from a load schedule that had already been superseded is a
> defect that grows quietly for the whole project.

**On the policy set, for About or a trust section:**
> Our health and safety, occupational health, equality, environmental and
> quality documents each end with a section headed "what is not yet in place."
> A supplier who tells you what they do not have is the one you can believe
> about what they do.

That last one is unusual and worth considering seriously. Publishing the fact
that the policies name their own gaps is a trust signal no competitor will
copy, because most of them cannot.

**On fatigue, for the accommodation or welfare page:**
> Fatigue management begins at the specification, months before anybody
> arrives. A workforce that cannot rest is a workforce whose judgement is
> impaired on a construction site the following morning.

## What was deliberately kept out

**Anything traceable to the private LinkedIn exchanges.** Two senior people at
tier-one manufacturers confirmed the diagnosis this month. Those were private
replies to a former colleague, not endorsements offered for reuse, and putting
their words or their companies on a public website would be using them as a
sales lever. The underlying observation stands as ETABLIX's own framing, in
ETABLIX's own words.

**Statistics.** No percentage of projects that overrun, no industry waste
figure, nothing. Every number invites a question about its source and a public
page is the worst place to be unable to answer one.

**Any implied delivery record.** The worked-example section already labels
itself an illustrative model rather than a client case study, and the new
section makes no claim about projects delivered under the company.
