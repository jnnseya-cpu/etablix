# AtkinsRéalis: the notice, the reply, and the rule that follows

AtkinsRéalis procurement has issued a supplier-code notice. It is worth reading
closely before deciding what to do, because **what it says and what it means are
different things**.

## What the notice actually does

It refuses us as a **recruitment agency**. Every term in it is a recruitment
term: agencies, Preferred Suppliers List, speculative candidate information,
CVs, hiring managers, the approved recruitment channel. It tells us that a
speculative CV will be treated as a gift — a standard device for defeating an
agency's later claim to an introduction fee — and it extends the notice to the
entire organisation, subsidiaries and parent companies, asking that it be shared
internally so that nobody makes contact again.

**ETABLIX is not a recruitment agency and has never applied to be one.** So this
is not a rejection of what we do. It is a misclassification of who we are, and
it is now recorded against every address AtkinsRéalis holds for us.

That is worse than a decline. A decline expires. A supplier-code notice sits in
a procurement system and is what the next person sees.

## Why it happened

The playbook already carried the two rules that would have prevented it —
*"skip the recruitment inboxes"* and *"no attachment, one link"*. Something
carrying the director's curriculum vitae reached a recruitment-facing queue, and
from that queue there is only one thing it can look like.

The underlying error is a category error, and it is easy to keep making. The
director's record is the strongest evidence this company has: it is the whole
basis of the DPS submissions, section 4.1 of *Director's Relevant Experience*,
and the answer to SQ 6.1. All of that is correct **inside a submitted document**.
The same material in a cold email is not evidence of a company's competence. It
is a person being offered.

## The rule

> **Never attach a curriculum vitae to an introduction, and never describe the
> director as available.** The director's record belongs in a submitted
> document — a selection questionnaire, a tender response, a request to
> participate. Where competence has to be evidenced in an introduction, describe
> what the company **did**, never who is **available**.

Now added to the pre-send rules in `cold-introduction-500.html`.

## Whether to reply at all

Yes, once, and then stop.

The case for silence is that they have told us to go away and any reply risks
reading as a push-back. The case for replying is stronger: they have invited
questions, and the record they are about to keep is factually wrong about what
kind of company this is. Correcting it costs one short email and closes the
matter properly.

**The reply must therefore ask for nothing.** No request to reconsider, no
request to be added to any list, no request to be told when the PSL reopens, no
description of the service beyond what is needed to correct the category. The
moment it contains an ask, it becomes the thing they just told us not to send,
and it proves their classification right.

## Not selling this one, deliberately

The standing rule is that anything touching what we do should also sell us. It
does not apply here and applying it would be actively harmful. A cease-contact
notice from a procurement team is one of the named exceptions in the house
style: the correct response to being told to stop is to stop.

## What it costs, and what it does not

AtkinsRéalis is closed for the foreseeable future, and that is a real loss — a
tier-one designer with reach across exactly the sectors in the plan.

What it does not cost is the method. Nothing in the notice says the service is
unwanted or the approach was wrong for a supplier; it says we were assessed as
the wrong kind of supplier entirely, through a channel that could only reach that
conclusion. The fix is channel discipline, not a change of proposition.
