## Where the addition is

**First of a kind changes the back end of the establishment, not the front.**

A site compound is sized and programmed against the construction peak and then
wound down. That is correct for a repeat build, where commissioning is short
and predictable because it has been done before.

A first-of-a-kind carbon capture train is not that. Commissioning, testing and
optimisation run considerably longer than on a repeat build, and they need
people on site after construction is nominally complete. Welfare, access,
temporary supplies and security all have to survive into that phase. The
extension is rarely in anyone's original budget line, because the original
budget was built from a repeat-build shape.

That is the ETABLIX argument — a cost line with no owner — told through a
property of this specific project rather than through the general case.

**Second, the cluster.** Teesside is not a standalone site. Several major
programmes draw on the same regional labour, accommodation and logistics
capacity. Whose peak lands when is a real planning question there and a
non-question on an isolated scheme.

Stated as a question, not as a claim about specific programmes clashing —
nothing here asserts a timing conflict that has not been verified.
