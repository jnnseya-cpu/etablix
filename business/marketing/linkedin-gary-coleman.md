# LinkedIn — Gary Coleman, Subcontract Development Manager, Belfast

He accepted, so the next message is the one that matters.

**He is not a prospect for the diagnostic. He is the door into a supply
chain.** A Subcontract Development Manager finds, develops and qualifies the
subcontractors who take scope — which means the useful ask is not "let me
review your bid", it is "what can you never find anyone competent to take, and
how do I get properly registered".

That also makes him a peer rather than a target. You sat on the other side of
his desk: your packages at GE Vernova were the ones sourcing had to go and
find somebody for. Say that, and the conversation starts in the middle
instead of at the beginning.

## When he answers

**If he names a package he cannot place** — that is the most valuable sentence
anybody has given you this month. Ask one follow-up about why: is it capability,
capacity, compliance, or geography? Then say honestly whether you can take it.
Saying "not that one, but the three around it" builds more credit than a yes
you cannot deliver.

**If he sends you a prequalification pack or a portal link** — do it properly
and quickly, because a slow return is the first data point he has about how you
perform. Two standing rules:

- **Never pay to register on a supplier portal.** A fee to be listed is not a
  route to work.
- **List only accreditations you actually hold.** "In progress" is an
  acceptable answer and a false one is disqualifying. He checks.

**If he asks what you have done as a company** — be straight about it: the
company is new, the delivery record is yours personally, and you can evidence
that in detail. The capability statement at etablix.com is the thing to send,
and only when he asks for it.

## What to keep out

- No attachment and no link in the first message. He did not ask for one, and
  a document in an opening DM reads as a mailshot.
- Nothing confidential from GE Vernova: your role and the shape of the scope
  are yours to describe, project values and supplier names are not.
- No claim to hold accreditations, insurances or certifications that are not
  in place today. In his job, verifying that is a five-minute task, and it is
  the first thing he does.
