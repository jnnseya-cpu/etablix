# LinkedIn comment — Samuel Neadley, AI Growth Zones and the regional pipeline

**Post:** DataVita secures £300m for a data centre rollout in Scotland's AI
Growth Zone; Vantage and Nebius deploy in the South Wales AI Growth Zone.
Argues 77% of existing UK capacity sits in London and the South East, that
grid constraints, land costs and planning pressure are pushing developers
further afield, and that the projects going up outside London create real
opportunity across regional supply chains.

**Who he is:** Data Centre Recruitment Specialist, 2nd degree. His world is
labour availability, which is exactly the hinge of the point worth adding.

## Where the addition is

His closing line — regional supply chains, contractors and engineers who don't
want to work in the South East — is right, and it is only half the picture.
The labour existing in a region is not the same as the labour existing within
commuting distance of a grid-adjacent site in a valley or on the central belt.

That gap is site establishment. At data centre scale the peak workforce will
outrun the local travel-to-work population, and at that point the compound has
to supply what the location does not: accommodation, coach runs, catering hours
that open before six, welfare sized for people who have already travelled an
hour. All of it is preliminaries, all of it is set at bid stage, and it is
routinely benchmarked off a scheme in a place with a completely different
labour catchment.

This is the strongest commenting position we have on the data centre pipeline,
because the establishment consequence of moving a scheme north or west is a
direct, unarguable function of the thing he already said: the location changed.

## The comment

> Moving a scheme out of the South East changes more than land cost and grid
> connection. It changes the logistics baseline.
>
> In London and the South East you can broadly assume a travel-to-work
> population big enough to staff the peak. At data centre scale on the central
> belt or in the South Wales valleys, peak headcount will outrun what is
> commutable — so the site has to carry what the location doesn't provide.
> Accommodation, coach runs, catering that opens before six, welfare sized for
> people who left home at five.
>
> That is all preliminaries, and it usually gets priced off a benchmark from
> somewhere with a different labour catchment entirely. Which is how a site
> establishment number that was perfectly defensible in Slough comes up short
> two hundred miles north.
>
> Fixable, and cheaply — but only at bid stage. Once the fence is up, the
> decision has already been made.

## Why it is shaped this way

- **It leads with the substance, not with praise.** LinkedIn truncates a
  comment at roughly two lines. "Moving a scheme out of the South East changes
  more than land cost and grid connection" is the whole argument compressed
  into the visible part.
- **It agrees with him and then extends.** He said developers are moving out.
  The comment says what that does to the compound. Nobody is corrected.
- **No named party is criticised.** Slough is used as a generic South East
  benchmark because it is the UK's largest cluster; no comment is made on
  DataVita's, Vantage's or Nebius's schemes, their pricing or their
  contractors. "Two hundred miles north" stays deliberately unspecific.
- **No invented figures.** The 77% is his. Nothing numeric is added.
- **No pitch.** The profile does that. The last two sentences are the
  positioning and they are earned by the four above them.
- **"Left home at five" is the line that will be remembered.** It is concrete,
  it is true, and it is the difference between talking about welfare as a
  compliance item and talking about it as a fatigue and productivity item.

## If he replies

He is a recruiter, so the natural reply is about labour supply rather than
preliminaries. If he asks how it is fixed, the answer is the single-site
diagnostic: the establishment requirement worked out from the programme and
the labour catchment before the preliminaries are priced, so the number in the
bid is the number the site needs. Do not put that in the first comment.
