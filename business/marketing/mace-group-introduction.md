## Why the introduction is written this way

**It is short, and it is built to be routed.** Diana described the process:
somebody reads it, decides whether there is a need, and forwards it to the
relevant person. That reader is sorting, not buying. So the category line comes
second, in their vocabulary rather than ours, and the three models are one line
each — a person deciding where to send this should be able to do it in twenty
seconds without reading the whole thing.

**Three models, not one.** For a tier-one contractor all three are plausible
and which one fits is a project-by-project question, so presenting only one
would invite the wrong no. Advisory is listed first because it is the easiest
thing for a main contractor to buy: no supply-chain contract changes hands.

**The "never holds your money" line earns its place.** It is the single
sentence that distinguishes Model 02 from an ordinary intermediary, and it
answers the first objection a commercial reader raises before they raise it.

**What it carefully does not say.** No accreditation is claimed — ETABLIX holds
no ISO certification and no SSIP registration, and the letter does not gesture
at either. **No insurance is implied**: the line is that policies and company
information will be provided in whatever form the process needs, not that
particular cover is in place today. No price. No client, scheme or former
employer is named, and nothing suggests a reference that cannot be given. No
curriculum vitae is attached and the director is not described as available —
this is a company introducing a service, not a person looking for a role.
Nothing implies a Principal Contractor or Principal Designer appointment under
CDM 2015.

**And it closes on the realistic outcome.** Diana said introductions are
assessed against current or future need. Asking to be held on file against a
future one costs nothing, matches what she actually described, and is far more
likely to be granted than a meeting.
