# Link outreach — six asks, written to be sent

**Prepared 10 September 2026.**

## Why this is the one that matters

Everything built on the site is now done and gated. It will not, on its own,
produce a ranking. What moves position on a domain nobody has linked to is
other people linking to it, and no script can make somebody do that.

**One link from a trade body, a client, a framework operator or the trade press
is worth more than every technical change made this week.** That is not
modesty about the technical work; it is how the ranking systems are built.

Six asks follow. They are ordered by how likely they are to land, not by how
much authority they carry, because an ask that does not land is worth nothing
whatever the domain rating.

---

## The rules, before anything is sent

**Never buy a link.** Construction has an active market in them. They are
detectable, they are cheap because they are worthless, and the penalty outlasts
the benefit by years.

**Never pay to be listed in a supplier directory.** This is already the
standing rule on supplier portals and it applies here for the same reason. A
directory that charges for inclusion is selling placement, which is what a
search engine discounts.

**Never invent a relationship.** Do not describe an organisation as a partner,
a client or a member unless it is true and they would say the same. The whole
value of the article is that it is checkable.

**Ask for the thing, not for a link.** Nobody grants "a backlink". People
publish articles, list suppliers they work with, and answer questions. The link
is a by-product.

## 1 · The CIOB, and Construction Manager

**Why this is first.** Justin is MCIOB. That is standing, not a cold approach,
and Construction Manager is the CIOB's own magazine. It runs opinion and
technical pieces from members, and the subject — where AI genuinely helps on a
construction project and where it must be stopped — is squarely what its
readership argues about.

**What to send.** A pitch, not the article. Editors want to shape it.

> **Subject:** Pitch — what AI agents should never be allowed to do on a project
>
> Dear Editor,
>
> I run a site-services business that uses AI agents across its own delivery —
> requirements packages, tender packs, bid evaluation — and I would like to
> pitch a piece on the part of that nobody writes about: what the agents are
> refused, and who enforces the refusal.
>
> The argument is that the interesting question is no longer whether a model
> can draft a specification, because it can. It is what has to be true around
> the model before that specification is safe to issue. Four categories where I
> think the line has to be absolute: no agent awards a contract or commits
> money; no agent accepts work or closes a defect; no agent holds a statutory
> duty, because CDM duty holders are people and organisations rather than
> software; and no agent resolves a life-safety question.
>
> I would also make a distinction that I think is missing from the debate: the
> difference between a control and a promise. An instruction in a prompt is a
> promise. A function that checks the finished document and refuses it is a
> control. On our tender packs, the scope sheets and the pricing schedule are
> reconciled by machine before the pack can be issued — a scope item with no
> priced line comes back after award as a variation, and a priced line with no
> scope item is priced by every tenderer on a different assumption.
>
> Around 1,200 words, from practice rather than from theory, and I would be
> happy to have it challenged in the edit. A longer version is at
> https://etablix.com/blog/ai-agents-in-construction if it is useful to see the
> shape first.
>
> Justin Ngolu Nseya MCIOB
> Managing Director, ETABLIX — Integrated Site Services
> contact@etablix.com · +44 7493 216101

**What to watch for.** If they take it, ask that the byline carries the company
name and the URL. Most trade titles do this as standard; a few do not, and it
is much easier to ask before publication than after.

## 2 · The other trade press

Same pitch, adjusted. Send them one at a time, a week apart, and say plainly
if a piece is under consideration elsewhere — construction publishing is small
and being caught double-pitching costs more than the link is worth.

| Title | Angle to lead with |
|---|---|
| **Construction News** | The procurement angle: why returns cannot be compared, and what that costs at award. |
| **Building** | The commercial angle: what a variation at the contractor's rate costs against a tendered one. |
| **Infrastructure Intelligence** | The programme angle: consents worked backwards from an access date. |
| **New Civil Engineer** | The technical angle: welfare sizing, and why CDM 2015 sets no ratios. |

**The welfare point is the strongest cold pitch of the four**, because it is
specific, checkable and mildly contrarian: Schedule 2 of CDM 2015 requires
facilities that are suitable and sufficient and sets no numeric ratios at all.
The one-per-seven figure everybody quotes comes from the Approved Code of
Practice to different regulations. Tender documents cite the wrong instrument
for their own requirement routinely. That is a 900-word piece on its own and
it is the second article to write anyway.

## 3 · Suppliers already in the supply chain

**Why it works.** Suppliers maintain "who we work with" and "sectors" pages,
and being listed there is normal, honest and mutual. This is the easiest link
in the list and the one most people never ask for.

**Who to ask.** Any supplier who has been through prequalification and worked
on something. Not a cold list.

> **Subject:** Would you list us on your site?
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Quick and slightly cheeky one. We list the standards we hold our supply chain
> to at https://etablix.com/subcontractors and I would be glad to reference
> [company] there where it is accurate to do so.
>
> If you keep a clients or sectors page, would you be willing to do the same
> for us? Plain listing, our name linked to https://etablix.com — nothing
> either of us needs to write copy for.
>
> No obligation at all if it does not fit how you present the business.
>
> Justin

**Reciprocal listing is fine.** Two businesses that genuinely work together
listing each other is not a scheme; it is what a supply chain page is for. What
is a scheme is a ring of unrelated sites swapping links, and the difference is
whether the relationship is real.

## 4 · Framework and DPS supplier directories

**Why it works.** Framework operators publish supplier lists, and inclusion is
earned by qualification rather than bought. These are among the best links a
construction business can hold because the association is meaningful.

**What to do.** For every framework, DPS or buying group ETABLIX is admitted
to, check whether the operator publishes a supplier directory and whether the
entry carries a website field. Fill it in. Most people leave it blank.

**The standing rule applies.** If a directory asks for payment to be included
or to have the link made live, do not pay it. Qualification is the price of
admission; a fee for the entry itself means the entry is advertising.

## 5 · Greater Birmingham Chambers of Commerce and regional bodies

**Why it works.** Membership directories carry a website field, the association
is genuine, and local signals carry weight on any query with a place in it —
which is most of what a client in the West Midlands searches.

**What to do.** Check the membership terms first. Where membership is worth
having for its own reasons, the directory entry comes with it. Where it is not,
do not join for the link.

**Do this at the same time as claiming the business listing**, because both are
matched against the same name, address and phone. The site now publishes one
form of all three, and the audit fails if a second one appears:

```
ETABLIX — Integrated Site Services
Groupe Nseya House, Kingstanding, Birmingham B44 8DJ
+44 7493 216101
contact@etablix.com
```

**Use exactly that, character for character, in every directory.** Two
spellings of one address do not reinforce each other, they compete.

---

## 6 · Answering the question in public

**Why it is last and still matters.** Not a link, strictly, but the mechanism
that produces the others. Every time somebody asks in public what AI can
actually do on a project, answer it properly — the argument in the reply, the
link at the end if it adds something.

**Where the questions are.** LinkedIn posts from main contractors and
consultants about AI adoption, CIOB community discussions, and the comment
threads under the trade press pieces above.

**What not to do.** Do not reply "great post, we wrote about this here". That
is the behaviour every platform now suppresses and every reader ignores. Reply
with the substance and let people find the rest.

## What to expect

Of six asks, one or two landing is a good result, and the two that land are
almost always the ones where a relationship already existed — the CIOB and the
existing suppliers. The trade press is a numbers game measured in months.

Three real links is the threshold where the technical work built this week
starts to have something to act on. Below that, the site is correct and
invisible.
