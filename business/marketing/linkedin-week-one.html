Day 2 · Tue

The problem

Post 07:45

Job: teach the frame. No selling — a senior reader should finish this nodding, because they've lived it. Posts that name a familiar pain travel further than any announcement.

Post copy
The project rarely fails because a supplier is incompetent.
It fails because nobody owns the complete system.

The cabins arrive on programme — but the power supply was sized off a spec nobody updated after the village grew.

The generator supplier did exactly what their order said. The accommodation supplier did exactly what theirs said. The interface between them belonged to no one.

Every site set-up has dozens of these seams: power to welfare, data to gatehouse, water to village, security to access, waste to everything. Each package has an owner. The seams don't.

That's where the delay comes from. Not from a bad supplier — from an unowned interface, discovered the week you needed it working.

Genuine question for those running live projects: who on your site owns the interfaces between your temporary services — by name, in writing?

Justin Nseya MCIOB
Founder & MD, ETABLIX

Ground rules

The standard that makes the Thursday post true. Break one and the whole "check us first" position collapses.

  • Never fabricate proof. No invented testimonials, borrowed logos, or implied clients. The Day 3 model is labelled as a model — keep labelling it.
  • One post per day, maximum. A second post cannibalises the first's reach and reads as desperation.
  • Reply to everything. Every comment within two hours where possible, every DM same day. In week one, responsiveness is the product demo.
  • Personal profile leads, company page follows. Company pages get a fraction of personal reach. Never post to the page first.
  • No engagement pods, no bought followers, no hashtag walls. Three hashtags maximum; the Day 2 and Day 4 posts carry none by design where they'd dilute the voice.
