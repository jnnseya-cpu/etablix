# LinkedIn — James Maguire CEng FICE, Project Leader, Laing O'Rourke

Two things about this one shape the message.

**He is a Fellow of the Institution of Civil Engineers.** That is a senior
technical credential, not a job title, and it tells you how to write: precise,
short, no marketing vocabulary at all. An FICE project leader can smell a
value proposition through a screen and will file it accordingly.

**Laing O'Rourke self-delivers.** They own their plant and their manufacturing
and take work in-house that other tier ones subcontract, which means "let us
take your site establishment package" is likely to be answered with "we do
that ourselves" — and you will have spent your one opening on a proposition
he had to decline. Do not lead with it, and do not lead with the diagnostic
either: a main contractor's project leader is not buying a report about his
own bid.

What you actually have that he does not is **the view from the other side of
the contract**. You were the OEM-side subcontract manager buying civils and
site infrastructure into a turnkey scope; he is the man delivering it. You are
counterparts across a boundary that goes wrong in predictable ways, and the
question below is the one nobody asks him.

---

## The message

> Thanks for connecting, James.
>
> Quick note on why, since it wasn't random. My background is the other side
> of your contract: construction subcontract manager at GE Vernova, on EPC and
> turnkey projects across the UK, Ireland and Europe where the civils, the site
> management and the site infrastructure sat inside our scope and were bought
> out. I priced that scope, wrote the site reports behind it, and then managed
> the contractors who delivered it.
>
> I have since set up ETABLIX to do the site-establishment piece properly for
> clients who carry it without being set up for it. But that isn't why I'm
> writing — you self-deliver, and I'm not going to pitch you something you
> already do in-house.
>
> What I would value is the answer to the question I never got straight while
> I was on the buying side: from where you sit, what does the client or the
> OEM consistently get wrong in what they hand over to the civils? Not the
> commercial terms — the information. Is it the interface levels and loadings
> arriving late or arriving twice, the temporary works assumptions nobody
> owned, the access and possession dates that were never worked backwards from
> the consents, or something more boring and more expensive than any of those?
>
> I ask because I am building the thing that is supposed to fix it, and I would
> rather hear it from someone who has been handed the consequences than keep
> inferring it from my own side of the fence.
>
> Good to be connected either way.

---

## Why this shape

**Naming his self-delivery model before he has to** does more for your
credibility than any claim about your own capability. It says you know who
Laing O'Rourke is, and it removes the thing he would otherwise be bracing for.

**The question is specific enough to be answerable and open enough to be
interesting.** Four candidate answers are offered, which makes replying cheap —
he can pick one and add a sentence. An open "what goes wrong?" gets nothing;
a menu with a door left open gets a paragraph.

**"I would rather hear it from someone who has been handed the consequences"**
is the line that gets the reply. Engineers answer that. It concedes he knows
something you do not, which is both true and unusual in an unsolicited message.

---

## If he answers

His answer is the product. Whatever he names is a section of the diagnostic
that should exist, or a check that should be in it — write it down verbatim
and use his framing, not yours.

Then reply properly: engage with the substance, add what you saw of the same
problem from the buying side, and stop. Do not attach anything. Do not ask for
a meeting. A second exchange with an FICE project leader about how information
handover fails is worth more than a calendar invitation you had to ask for.

**Only if he asks what ETABLIX actually does:**

> Fixed fee, ten working days, on a live bid or a project about to mobilise.
> We read the client's own information pack against itself and report where
> the site establishment doesn't hold — consents nobody worked backwards from,
> welfare and accommodation sized to a headcount that has since moved,
> packages with no owner, and an appendix listing which of their own documents
> contradict which. It exists because on every turnkey job I priced, that work
> was done at speed by people who had other jobs.

## What to keep out

- **Any pitch for site-establishment scope on a Laing O'Rourke project.** If
  there is ever a route in, it will be through their supply chain team and
  after you have a delivery record, not through a project leader's inbox.
- **Confidential GE Vernova detail.** Your role and the pattern are yours; the
  project values, supplier names and commercial positions are not.
- **Any claim about how Laing O'Rourke does things.** You know your own side.
  Ask about his rather than describing it back to him.
