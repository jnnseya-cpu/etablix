# Kanadevia Inova UK — introduction for their UK energy-from-waste plants

**Status: DRAFTED, NOT SENT. The address is the one thing missing and it has
not been guessed.**

Sector confirmed by the director on 19 November 2026: energy from waste. The
letter is written for the UK business rather than the Zurich head office, and

## The address, and why it is blank

`kanadevia-inova.com` and `hz-inova.com` are both refused at this network's
gateway — `403 to CONNECT`, a policy denial, the same block that stops
`ico.org.uk` and the trade-press feeds. Their contact page could not be read,
and **no address has been invented to fill the gap.** An invented address on a
cold introduction either bounces or lands with somebody who did not ask for it,
and both are worse than a day's delay.

**Four ways to get the right one, in the order they are worth trying.**

1. **Ask the UK office.** This is what worked at Mace: the switchboard named
   `scm@macegroup.com` and said introductions to `info@` are not reviewed the
   same way. One call asking "which address do supplier introductions go to,
   and do you run a registration portal" beats any amount of searching, and the
   reply is a record worth keeping. The thank-you in
   `mace-group-introduction.md` is the pattern.
2. **Their own supplier route.** An EPC contractor of this size usually runs a
   vendor registration portal. If one exists, use it — a letter sent outside a
   process that exists reads as somebody who did not look.
3. **Companies House, for the UK entity.** The company they would contract
   through is a UK-registered subsidiary, and the filing gives the exact legal
   name, the registered office and the officers. That is the name to use on the
   letter and the address to post to if email gets nowhere. **It is not
   guessed here** — search Companies House for the UK Kanadevia Inova or
   Hitachi Zosen Inova entity and read it off the record.
4. **LinkedIn, for the person rather than the address.** The target is whoever
   owns site establishment or indirect procurement in the UK business. A
   head-office inbox in Zurich is the least likely route to a UK project team.

**Do not send to a personal address found by guessing a format.** Role address,
or an answered enquiry, as everywhere else.

## What the letter asserts, and what it will not

**Asserted:** they build energy-from-waste plants in the UK, as an EPC
contractor, formerly Hitachi Zosen Inova. That is who they are.

**Not asserted, deliberately:** no plant, no site, no value, no date, no
headcount and no UK entity name. None could be verified from here, and a letter
that names a scheme they are not on ends at that sentence.

**And no energy-from-waste reference is claimed or implied.** The letter's
authority rests on construction logic that holds on any EfW build, not on a
track record it does not have. A procurement team may well ask which EfW plants
we have done; that is answered honestly when asked. Volunteering it unprompted
in a first letter hands them the reason to stop reading, and nothing in the
letter invites the assumption either way. **That is a decision, not an
oversight** — if you would rather say it up front, say so and it goes in.

## Why two engagement models and not three

`mace-group-introduction.md` names all three and declines the third in the same
breath, which was honest in October. Prime Service Contractor is now internal
description and planning only and is never offered to a prospect, so it is
absent here rather than raised and withdrawn. Raising something that must then
be declined spends the one job a first letter has. If they ask, it is answered
then.

**And the legal name is correct here.** That file says "ETABLIX Limited", which
is neither the trading name nor the registered company. The next letter
corrects it by being right; nothing already sent is rewritten to pretend
otherwise.

> **To:** _(the supplier-introduction address at the UK office, once asked for)_

> accommodation, UK energy-from-waste plants
>
> Dear Supply Chain team,
>
> I am the Managing Director of **ETABLIX, part of JNN GLOBAL LTD, company
> 15405437**. We work on site establishment for energy and infrastructure
> projects: the temporary system a site runs on, taken as one accountable scope
> rather than as ten separate packages. I am writing to the UK business rather
> than to a plant, for the reason below.
>
> **The category, so this can be filed correctly.** Site set-up and site
> services — compound and welfare, temporary power and utilities, security,
> waste, catering, workforce accommodation, logistics and transport, and
> demobilisation and reinstatement.
>
> **On an energy-from-waste build the accommodation requirement does not end
> when the construction peak passes. It changes shape.** Erection draws a large
> trade workforce for a defined period. Cold commissioning, first fire, hot
> commissioning and the reliability run then draw a much smaller team, often
> international, staying considerably longer. Fewer people for more weeks is a
> different product from a bulk arrangement struck for the erection peak, and
> it is usually bought late, locally and at the worst available rate — because
> the tail was never in the original requirement. The compound has to carry
> that tail as well, so demobilisation falls later than the construction
> programme implies.
>
> **The plant repeats; the site does not.** That is the case for one
> arrangement across your UK plants rather than a decision taken afresh on each
> one. The output specification is written once. What changes from site to site
> is access, the temporary utility position, the local accommodation market and
> the travel time for the workforce — four variables, rather than a new scope
> every time.
>
> **Two ways to engage, so you can place us against whichever suits a given
> plant.**
>
> 1. **Advisory** — we write the strategy, output specification, package
>    boundaries and tender documents. You contract with every supplier and keep
>    full contracting control. Fixed scope, fixed fee.
> 2. **Management Integrator** — you contract directly with each supplier and
>    we run the procurement, the mobilisation, the interfaces between
>    suppliers, commercial administration and supplier performance through to
>    handback.
>
> In both, you hold every supplier contract; we never hold your money.
>
> **What we are not.** A supplier line in your construction phase plan. Not a
> main contractor, not CDM Principal Contractor or Principal Designer, and
> nothing to do with designing, building or commissioning the plant itself.
>
> **Insurance and registrations, stated plainly rather than on request.**

> 26 October 2026. No employers' liability policy: the only employee is the
> director, who holds a majority of the issued share capital and is exempt

> the Central Digital Platform. We hold no ISO certification and no SSIP
> registration today, and I would rather say that here than at prequalification.
>
