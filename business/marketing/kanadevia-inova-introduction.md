**Status: DRAFTED, NOT SENT. The address is the one thing missing and it has
not been guessed.**

## The address, and why it is blank

`kanadevia-inova.com` and `hz-inova.com` are both refused at this network's
gateway — `403 to CONNECT`, a policy denial, the same block that stops
`ico.org.uk` and the trade-press feeds. So their contact page could not be
read, and **no address has been invented to fill the gap.** An invented address
on a cold introduction either bounces or lands with somebody who did not ask
for it, and both are worse than a day's delay.

**Three ways to get the right one, in the order they are worth trying.**

1. **Ask.** This is what worked at Mace: the switchboard named
   `scm@macegroup.com` and said introductions to `info@` are not reviewed the
   same way. One call to their UK office asking "which address do supplier
   introductions go to, and is there a registration portal" produces a better
   answer than any amount of searching, and the reply is itself a record worth
   keeping. The thank-you reply in `mace-group-introduction.md` is the pattern.
2. **Their own supplier route.** An EPC contractor of this size usually runs a
   vendor registration portal or a supplier page. If one exists, use it — a
   letter sent outside a process that exists reads as somebody who did not look.
3. **LinkedIn, for the person rather than the address.** The target is whoever
   owns site establishment or indirect procurement for the UK business, not the
   Swiss head office. A generic head-office inbox for a Zurich-headquartered
   group is the least likely route to a UK project team.

**Do not send to a personal address found by guessing a format.** Role address
or an answered enquiry, as everywhere else.

## What is asserted about them, and what is not

**Asserted:** they are an EPC contractor building energy-from-waste and
renewable gas plants, formerly Hitachi Zosen Inova, and they build in the UK.
That is who they are, not a claim about a project.

**Not asserted, deliberately:** no UK project is named, no plant, no site, no
value, no date and no headcount. None of it could be verified from here, and a
letter that names a scheme they are not on ends at that sentence. The letter is
specific about the SHAPE of a process-plant build instead, which is true of
every one of them and is the part that earns the reading.

**One thing to check before sending.** The sector description above is from
general knowledge and was not confirmed against their own site, because the
site is unreachable from here. If renewable gas is the larger part of their UK
work, the second paragraph still reads correctly; if neither is right, stop and
tell me.

## Why two engagement models and not three

`mace-group-introduction.md` names all three and declines the third in the same
breath, which was an honest thing to do in October. The standing rule is now
that Prime Service Contractor is internal description and planning only and is
never offered to a prospect, so it is absent here rather than raised and
withdrawn. Raising something that must then be declined spends the one job a
first letter has. If they ask, it is answered then.

**And the legal name is correct here.** That file says "ETABLIX Limited", which
is neither the trading name nor the registered company. The next letter
corrects it by being right; nothing already sent is rewritten to pretend
otherwise.
