**The post:** LS ELECTRIC announces, at CIGRE 2026 in Paris, an agreement with
GE Vernova to establish **Grid X Technology**, a joint venture for
Voltage-Sourced Converter (VSC) HVDC. First named programme: Korea's West
Coast Energy Highway. Liked by Mark Stone.

**Audience in the comments:** LS ELECTRIC and GE Vernova grid people, CIGRE
delegates, transmission engineers. Technical, and they can tell in one
sentence whether the commenter knows anything.

---

## The rule under a post like this

Do not pitch. A joint-venture announcement is a corporate milestone and the
comment section is full of the people who negotiated it. The only comment
worth leaving is one that adds a dimension the post did not, from a place the
poster does not occupy.

We occupy exactly one such place: what happens on the ground around a
converter station, for the eighteen months to three years it takes to build
one. That is not a pitch, it is the observation.

## The commercial read, for the record and not for the comment

Converter stations are large, long, and frequently remote. A VSC programme
makes that *more* true rather than less, because the whole point of VSC is
that it works where a conventional scheme will not. Anywhere a link lands on
a weak grid or comes ashore is, almost by definition, somewhere without a
workforce nearby.

So the site-establishment problem on an HVDC programme is at the harder end
of the range: multi-year duration, remote location, sizeable peak workforce,
and a station that cannot slip because it is one half of a matched pair.

**Where ETABLIX could sit:** never with the JV, which sells converters. With
the **civils or EPC contractor** building the station, or with the
**transmission owner** as advisory on the establishment and accommodation
strategy before the main works are let. That is Model 01 or Model 02, and the
route in is the contractor, not the technology partner.

**Not a UK opportunity as posted.** Korea's West Coast Energy Highway is a
Korean government programme with a Korean supply chain. The relevance here is
that GE Vernova has UK grid work, and the UK has HVDC in the pipeline. This
comment is about being a recognisable name in that room a year from now, not
about this project.
