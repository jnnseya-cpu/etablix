# EGL3 — BAM appointed converter station construction partner

**The post.** An Offshore Transmission Project Director announcing BAM's
appointment as converter station construction partner on Eastern Green Link 3,
described as the UK's largest electricity transmission infrastructure project.
Subject to consent. Scotland to England. Signing photograph, National Grid and
SSEN banners. 428 reactions, 11 comments, 2 days old.

**What is in it, and what is not.** The post gives three usable facts: a
converter station package, a Scotland–England link, and that the whole thing is
subject to consent. It does not mention anything about the temporary estate,
which is correct for a milestone post and is where the one thing worth adding
lives.

## The angle, and why it is not one already used

An HVDC link converts at both ends. A link between Scotland and England
therefore puts a converter station in each jurisdiction — and the construction
compound that serves each one answers to a different building-control regime
and a different environmental regulator. A building warrant and SEPA north of
the border; Building Regulations and the Environment Agency south of it.

**That is a real trap and it is specific to a cross-border scheme.** One
compound specification, drawn once, gets two different answers on consents,
discharge and building control, and the difference is usually discovered by
whoever lets the package rather than designed in. It is not on the list of
angles already used, and it could not have been written for any of the others:
every previous comment was about a single site in a single jurisdiction.

**What is deliberately not claimed.** No regulation number. The exemption for
site accommodation differs between the two regimes and sleeping accommodation
changes the answer in at least one of them, but a statutory citation that could
not be verified from a primary source here does not go in a public comment
under a client's post. "Answers to" is the accurate verb and survives the
detail being checked.
