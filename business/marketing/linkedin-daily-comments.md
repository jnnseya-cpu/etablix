# The daily comment — five posts a day

Commenting is the highest-return marketing available to a company with no
delivery record. A post reaches your followers, which is nobody. A good comment
on someone else's post reaches *their* audience, which is thousands of the
exact people you are trying to meet — and it arrives as expertise rather than
as an advertisement.

Four rules, and they hold for all five posts every day.

**Answer the question the author asked.** Most commenters do not. If the post
ends in a question, the first line of your comment is your answer to it. That
alone puts you above ninety per cent of the thread.

**Say one thing they did not.** Not agreement, not a summary, not "great post".
A specific observation from your own experience that the post does not contain.
If you have nothing to add, scroll past — a filler comment costs more standing
than no comment.

**Never pitch, never link, never name ETABLIX.** Your profile is one click away
and it says what you do. A comment that sells converts a contribution into an
advertisement, and people who would have looked you up now will not.

**Stay in your lane, and say where the lane ends.** You are construction and
site establishment, not commissioning, not design, not M&E. Speaking from the
buying and construction side of someone else's discipline is credible and
welcome. Pretending to their discipline is transparent and fatal.

Length: three short paragraphs at most. No emoji, no hashtags. Comments longer
than the post are not read.

## Day 1 · Mehdi Nasimi — pre-commissioning readiness

**The post:** pre-commissioning readiness has to be built long before mechanical
completion, through commissioning strategy, systemization, turnover philosophy,
readiness criteria and an integrated schedule. He asks two questions: when does
commissioning formally enter planning, and what is the earliest trigger that
pre-commissioning needs management attention.

**Why this one is worth your time.** He is a commissioning lead and you are not,
which is exactly why you have something to say. His whole post is about
readiness of systems. Nobody in that thread will talk about readiness of the
*conditions* those systems need — power, water, access, welfare — because that
scope belongs to nobody and is therefore nobody's subject. It is yours.

### The comment

> On the projects I worked, commissioning entered planning early on paper and
> late in reality. The strategy existed at bid stage; the conditions it depended
> on were bought much later, by somebody else, against a different set of
> assumptions.
>
> The pattern I saw from the construction side: the commissioning team arrives
> as the construction peak demobilises, and the temporary services were sized,
> let and programmed against the construction headcount. Auxiliary supplies,
> water for flushing, welfare, access — all scoped to the wrong curve. The first
> person to notice is a commissioning engineer who cannot get a supply, and by
> then it is a variation.
>
> So on your second question, the earliest trigger I would use is not on site at
> all. It is in procurement. When the temporary services package goes to market
> without the commissioning load and duration written into it, systemization
> will slip later — and the cause was set months before anything visible went
> wrong.

### The shorter version

If you would rather not post three paragraphs on a first interaction with him:

> Earliest trigger I would use is not on site — it is in procurement. The
> commissioning team arrives as the construction peak demobilises, and the
> temporary services were sized and let against the construction headcount.
> Auxiliary supplies, water, welfare, access, all scoped to the wrong curve. If
> the temporary services package went to market without the commissioning load
> and duration in it, systemization slips later and the cause was set months
> earlier.

### If he replies

Answer his answer. Do not move to what you do. He is a 3rd-degree connection, so
a reply from him is the opening to connect — send the request the day after,
referencing the thread in the note, and keep the note to one line. The
conversation belongs on his post until he moves it.

His discipline is oil and gas; yours is power and infrastructure. If he says the
pattern is different in his sector, take it — that is worth more than being
right, and it is the second exchange that makes the connection real.
