## What registration actually involves — and where it will stop

From Siemens Energy's own published supplier process, not from guesswork:

1. **Registration runs through SAP Ariba.** Company details, an administrator,
   business data, product and service categories, ship-to locations.
2. **Registering is not the thing that matters.** Suppliers then complete
   qualification modules chosen by their product or service portfolio: the
   **Sustainability Self-Assessment**, plus further modules on **health and
   safety** and **cyber security**.
3. **"Ready-for-Business" (R4B) is the gate.** That status is what permits a
   supplier to take part in electronic invitations to tender and auctions. A
   registration that never reaches R4B is a database row.

**So the real work is the modules, and two of the three touch a known gap.**

- **Sustainability Self-Assessment** — a self-assessment, not a certification.
  Answerable honestly today, and the honest answers are acceptable.
- **Health and safety** — this is where the absence of SSIP registration or a
  documented equivalent will be felt, along with accident and enforcement
  history. The documented health and safety policy already built covers the
  substance; what is missing is third-party verification.
- **Cyber security** — the module that makes **Cyber Essentials** worth buying
  sooner than planned. It is the cheapest certification on the list and it
  answers a named gate directly, rather than generally improving the paperwork.

## Two standing cautions

**Never pay to register.** Registration as a supplier to a buyer is free. If any
step asks for a subscription, a listing fee or a paid upgrade to be visible,
stop and check what is being bought — that is a different product from being on
Siemens Energy's supplier list.

**Claim nothing in the modules that is not held.** No ISO certification and no
SSIP registration exist, and a questionnaire answer is a declaration. Answer
"documented, not certified", attach the policy, and let the gap be visible. A
discovered overclaim closes a door permanently; a visible gap only narrows it.
