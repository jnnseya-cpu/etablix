What actually happened, and why it is the whole message

Not a market insight. A description of their Tuesday, written by someone who was in the room for it.

  1. The site infrastructure scope was priced in-house. Hours of engineering and commercial time spent building a package for work the business was never going to self-deliver.
  2. The site visits and the site reports were done in-house too. Travel, time and cost, to produce information that a civils contractor would later go and gather again for itself.
  3. Then it went to sourcing to find somebody to take it. Martin and Mark inherited a priced package and the job of finding a contractor willing to own it — who then re-priced it from scratch anyway.
  4. The fix was already identified before you left: one contractor across the C programme instead of that cycle repeating on every job. Nobody had built the thing that made it possible. That is what ETABLIX is.

So the message is not “here is what we do”. It is “the thing we were trying to set up before I left — I have built it, and I want to know whether it went anywhere without me”. That is a question only they can answer, it costs them nothing to answer it, and it is genuinely interesting to you either way.

Three things to keep out of these messages

Each of these is what made the first attempt read like a mailshot.

  1. No introduction and no credentials. No “founder and managing director” block, no company boilerplate, no explaining your background. They know it. A signature that formal on a message to a former colleague is the tell.
  2. No attachment and no deck. If they want to see the standard of the work, offer it in a line and let them ask. Sending it unasked turns a colleague into a lead.
  3. Nothing confidential. Talk about the way of working — which is your own experience and yours to describe — and keep project values, supplier names and internal figures out of writing entirely. Sourcing people notice the difference immediately, and it is the difference between a former colleague and a risk.

Before you send
  • Use their own words for the programme. If it was not called the C programme by the time you left, say what it was called — getting that wrong undoes the whole effect.
  • Sign it “Justin”. No title, no company line, no website under it. The email address carries the domain already.
  • If they were emailed separately last week, send two separate replies on different days.

Optional P.S., only if you want proof of standard: “If it is useful to see the level we write at now, I have a full Employer’s Requirements set for a 225-bed workforce village — civils, modular, kitchen, FM — and the gap register we ran against our own document.
