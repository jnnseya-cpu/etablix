The observation the whole sequence rests on

Say this once, plainly, and there is nothing left to explain about what ETABLIX is for.

An EPC or full-turnkey contract brings in a scope the business is not set up to deliver. The civil works, the site management and the site infrastructure are inside the contract from the day it is signed. On Skye the worker accommodation was inside it as well. But an equipment and EPC business is not a site-establishment business — so every single time, that scope has to be built up from nothing: priced by engineers who have other work, surveyed by people flown out to do it, written up into a site report, and then handed to sourcing to go and find a civils contractor who will own it and who re-prices the whole thing from scratch anyway.

That is not a procurement failing and it is not anybody’s fault. It is a missing capability, and it repeats on every job that carries that scope.

Say it in their language, not in yours
  • Never write “integrated site services” or name a model. Write “the civils, site management and site infrastructure that sit inside the turnkey scope”. That is what it is called where they work.
  • Skye is shorthand between the four of you. Use it as a reference point, never with a value, a supplier name or a commercial detail attached.

The mapping that makes this easy to say

You do not have to explain what ETABLIX does. You already did all three of these things by hand, with them, and each one is now a model with a price on it.

The move this unlocks: Model 03 is the version that answers the whole problem, and it is also the biggest ask you could make of a supplier nobody has bought from yet. So lead with Model 01 in every message. It takes the hours off the engineering team without moving a single contract, it is a fixed professional fee, and it is the one sourcing can agree to without a governance argument. Model 03 is where it ends up, not where it starts.

Three things to keep out of all three messages

Each of these is what made the first attempt read like a mailshot.

  1. No introduction and no credentials. No “founder and managing director” block, no company boilerplate, no explaining your background. They know it. A signature that formal on a message to a former colleague is the tell.
  2. No attachment and no deck. If they want to see the standard of the work, offer it in a line and let them ask. Sending it unasked turns a colleague into a lead.
  3. Nothing confidential. Describe the way of working — which is your own experience and yours to describe — and keep contract values, supplier names, rates and internal figures out of writing entirely. Sourcing notice that distinction immediately, and it is the difference between a former colleague and a risk.

And they talk to each other. Three near-identical emails arriving in the same business is worse than one. Each message below is written to that person’s actual job, and the order and spacing at the bottom of this page matter as much as the words.

Order, spacing, and when to stop

Three messages into one business in one morning reads as a campaign. Spread over ten days, in this order, it reads as someone working their way round people they know.

  1. Tuesday — Martin. The closest relationship and the person whose cost it is. He can answer without asking anyone.
  2. Wednesday or Thursday — Mark, on LinkedIn. Different channel, different altitude, and it does not matter if he and Martin compare notes because the messages are about different things.
  3. The following Tuesday — Marc. By then you may have something from one of the others, and a sourcing conversation that starts with “Martin suggested I come to you” is a different conversation entirely.
  4. Then one line each, three weeks later, and stop. “No reply needed — I know what your inboxes look like. If the site infrastructure scope ever becomes somebody’s problem again, you know where I am.” With people you used to work with, a fourth email costs you the relationship you are actually trying to keep.
If it all goes quiet
  • Change the route, not the message. An industry event, a mutual contact, a comment on something one of them posts. Reconnection works better in public than in private.
  • Keep the formal supplier registration moving in parallel. It does not need anybody to reply to anything.
  • Log all three contacts as an account in CONSTRUX with the dates, so the next approach starts from a record rather than from memory.
