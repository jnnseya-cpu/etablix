# LinkedIn — Andrew Miller, Offshore Manager & Client Rep

**The post:** Andrew shares a Guardian piece, *"Mapped: the £150bn megaproject
that aims to protect Britain from energy…"*, with his own line about the
transition from strategically placed local power stations to a truly national
grid, moving electricity from Scotland and the East Coast to where people live.
One reaction, two comments. Second-degree connection, not connected.

## Why this one is worth more than the corporate posts

A results announcement from a tier-one is a broadcast with hundreds of
comments. **This is a person sharing something he found interesting, with two
comments on it.** A good reply here is a conversation, not a contribution to a
pile — and he is an Offshore Manager and Client Rep, which is a genuinely
relevant seat.

Comment first. Do not send a connection request ahead of it. If he replies, the
request follows naturally and gets accepted; sent cold beforehand it is one more
notification from a stranger.

## Why it works

**It adds the thing the map cannot show.** Everybody else will comment on
routes, capacity or planning reform. Nobody will say that a national grid rebuilt
around wind is, by definition, built where the fewest people live — and that the
workforce problem follows from the geography rather than from bad planning.

**It puts him above us, deliberately.** "Offshore has always had to solve that"
is true and it makes him the expert in the exchange. People answer questions
where answering makes them look knowledgeable, which is most of why replies
happen at all.

**The question is the whole point.** "Planned as a programme or still site by
site" is a practitioner's question a stranger could not ask, and its answer is
the one piece of market intelligence that decides whether there is a business
here. Same question as the GPQS one, and it works for the same reason.

**No statistic of our own.** £150bn comes from the headline he posted. Nothing
else numerical appears.

## What must not be in it

- **No ETABLIX, no link, no service.** Not on a first comment to somebody you
  are not connected to.
- **No claim of offshore experience.** He does that work. The comment credits
  offshore with having solved the problem first, which is both true and the
  opposite of claiming it.
- **Nothing about interfaces or single accountable packages.** That vocabulary
  belongs in a conversation he has invited, not in a comment under an article.

## If he replies

Answer his answer first and properly. If it goes two exchanges, send the
connection request then — and still say nothing about what you sell until he
asks. A client rep who explains how a programme is actually being planned has
given you something worth more than a pitch would have earned.
