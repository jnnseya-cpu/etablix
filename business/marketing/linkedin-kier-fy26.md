# LinkedIn — Kier Infrastructure, FY26 results post

**The post:** Kier Infrastructure announcing Kier Group FY26 results. Order book
£7.4bn, c.95% of forecast FY27 revenue already secured. Recent wins named:
Thames Water, up to £280m quality improvement at Maple Lodge; Transport for
Greater Manchester, accessibility improvements at six railway stations. Liked by
Kier Group.

**Audience in the comments:** Kier people, their supply chain, infrastructure
clients, and competitors watching. Results-day comments are read by investor
relations and by the executive whose name is on the release.

## The rule under a results post

Do not pitch. A results announcement is a corporate milestone and the comment
section is full of people who worked on the number. The only comment worth
leaving adds a dimension the release did not, from a place the poster does not
occupy.

We occupy exactly one: what has to happen on the ground before any of that
secured revenue can be earned.

---

## The comment

> Congratulations to the team. The number worth sitting with is c.95% of FY27
> revenue already secured — at that level the risk moves from winning work to
> starting it, and those starts land in the same window competing for the same
> cabins, temporary power and welfare supply chain. The two wins named here show
> how differently that problem presents: Maple Lodge is establishment inside a
> live operational asset, while six stations is the same compound solved six
> times on sites that have to keep running. Interested to see how mobilisation
> gets sequenced across a book that full.

### Shorter, if the thread is already long

## Why it works

**It engages with their strongest number rather than congratulating past it.**
Everyone else will write "well done" under £7.4bn. The 95% figure is the one
with a consequence attached, and naming the consequence is the whole comment.

**The observation is true and not in the release.** A secured order book
converts commercial risk into mobilisation risk. That is obvious to anybody who
has stood up a site and invisible in a results deck, which is precisely the gap
a comment should fill.

**The two named wins do the work.** Maple Lodge is establishment inside a
running operational asset. Six stations is the same compound solved six times on
sites that stay open. Reading their own two examples back as two different
establishment problems demonstrates more than a paragraph of capability would,
and it is drawn entirely from what they published.

**It ends on interest, not on an offer.** "Interested to see how mobilisation
gets sequenced" is a comment. "Which is why we exist" is an advert, and the
audience can tell in one word which one they are reading.

## What must not be in it

- **No mention of ETABLIX, no link, no service.** The profile does that job.
  A results post is the worst possible place to sell.
- **No claim about how Kier mobilises.** We do not know, and asserting it in
  front of the people who do is the fastest way to be wrong in public.
- **No numbers of our own.** Not a percentage of projects that overrun, not a
  cost of late mobilisation. The comment sits under a results release; anything
  unsourced next to audited figures reads badly.
- **Nothing that could be read as criticism.** The comment describes a
  structural consequence of success, not a failing. Kier people should be able
  to read it and agree.

## After posting

If somebody from Kier replies, answer their answer properly before saying
anything about what you do. A results-day comment that turns into a conversation
is worth more than the comment, and turning it into a pitch on the second
message ends it.
