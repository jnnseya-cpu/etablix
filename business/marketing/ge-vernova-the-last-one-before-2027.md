# GE Vernova — one email to the construction and sourcing team

The decision in `ge-vernova-stop-and-reroute.md` stands: no chasing, no
second approach to Marc, Martin or Mark, who already had their closing lines.
This is one email to the **construction and sourcing function**, showing the
worked example and asking for nothing.

## The correction that matters, recorded because the first draft got it wrong

The first version of this email announced the stand-down: *"the next time you
hear from me will be in 2027."* It also said *"file it or bin it"*, *"there is
nothing I need from you"* twice, and *"I will not chase it"*.

All four are wrong, and for one reason.

**Parking them until 2027 is our discipline, not their news.** It is an
internal decision about where effort goes. Saying it out loud converts a
sensible plan into a statement, and the statement reads as a shelf — *you are
on it, see you in fifteen months*. Worse, it forecloses the only outcome worth
having: somebody reads the sample, remembers a compound problem coming up in
November, and replies. After that sentence, replying would mean breaking the
sender's stated plan.

The rest failed the same test in a smaller way. "Bin it" is a harsh verb to
put in a former employer's inbox — written to make the no-ask credible, it
reads as a flounce. "Nothing I need from you", said twice, lands as pointed
rather than generous, and to somebody who never answered the earlier emails it
can sound like an accusation dressed as politeness. "I will not chase it"
raises a worry nobody had.

The light version of that device worked in `linkedin-network-introduction.md`
because it was one warm line. Here it was carrying too much weight.

**The rule: the stand-down is how we behave, not what we say.**

## One thing to check before sending

A six-month restriction running from a February leaving date expired in
August. It is now September, so on the face of it the period has run. That is
an observation about dates and not legal advice — if the contract carried a
longer restriction, a different trigger date, or a non-solicitation clause
worded separately from the non-compete, read it before sending.

## What must not be in it

- **Nothing may suggest ETABLIX delivered anything for GE Vernova.** The
  turnkey work was Justin's, as their employee. "Some of you will know me from
  the turnkey projects" is true and safe. Anything that reads as a credential
  for the company is not.
- No reference to any private exchange with anyone, at GE Vernova or
  elsewhere. Not one.
- No pricing. This is not a proposal.
- No mention of who has or has not replied to earlier emails. Nobody is owed
  an explanation, and raising it makes the email about the sender.
- No announced return date. See above.

## Why it is shaped this way

- **One light line carries the no-ask.** "No reply needed — I know what a
  sourcing inbox looks like in September" is a courtesy to a busy reader
  rather than a point being made. It asks for nothing without drawing
  attention to the fact.
- **"I left in February and set up ETABLIX" does the credential work without
  making a claim.** They can place him in a second, and nothing is asserted
  about what the company has done.
- **One insight, not a capability list.** The Section 278 paragraph is
  concrete, it is a failure every one of them has seen, and it demonstrates
  the method instead of describing it. Everything else was cut to make room.
- **It ends on an open door and a human sentence.** "It was a pleasure working
  alongside you all" is true and appropriate to a former employer, and "if it
  ever becomes somebody's problem" is an offer rather than a request. That is
  the line that makes a reply possible in six weeks without anybody having to
  climb over a declaration.
