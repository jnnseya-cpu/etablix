# LinkedIn — Alasdair Mackintosh, Director of Opportunity Management, Exyte

**His reply:** *"Hi Justin, thanks for reaching out. Super, do you work in the
UK only or throughout Europe?"* First-degree connection.

## Why this is the best-matched contact so far

**"Director of Opportunity Management" is a pursuit role.** He identifies and
shapes opportunities before they become bids — which means he sits at precisely
the point where the site establishment number gets built, by people with other
work on, from documents that do not agree with each other. Of every job title
encountered in this campaign, this is the one closest to the problem.

**Exyte is a serious fit.** Founded in Stuttgart in 1912, around 10,000 people,
targeting €10bn turnover by 2027. They build the most site-establishment-heavy
facilities there are: semiconductor fabs, data centres, pharmaceutical
cleanrooms. Very large peak workforces, very long programmes, often on
constrained or industrial sites.

**And his question is a real qualification question, not small talk.** Exyte's
growth is continental. They have just taken three AI data centre contracts in
Greater Frankfurt worth close to €750m for 2026 — one of their largest data
centre intakes — and are recruiting around 250 people in Germany to deliver
them. A UK-only supplier is worth less to him than a European one. So answer it
honestly, because the answer decides whether the conversation continues.

>
> Which markets are you asking about? If there is a region in mind I will tell
> you straight whether we could cover it.

### Why it is written this way

**It splits advisory from delivery, and that split is the honest answer.** The
diagnostic and specification work genuinely travels, because it is documents
and site visits. Physically delivering compound, welfare and accommodation in
Germany would need German supply chain that does not exist yet. Saying both is
accurate; saying "yes, all of Europe" would be discovered in one follow-up
question by somebody who runs pursuits for a living.

**The European record answers his real concern.** He is asking whether you are
parochial. The GE Vernova clause says no, and it says it with a job title at a
named employer rather than an adjective.

**"I will tell you straight whether we could cover it" is the strongest
sentence available to an unproven supplier.** It offers the one thing he cannot
get from a large competitor: somebody who will tell him where the limits are
before he relies on them. Senior pursuit people have been let down by suppliers
who said yes to everything.

**The question back qualifies him.** If he names Germany, the conversation is
about the Frankfurt programmes and it is real. If he says "just asking", it is
early. Either answer is worth having, and it costs one line.

## What to keep out of this reply

- **Do not mention the Frankfurt data centre contracts.** You know about them;
  raising them unprompted in a two-line exchange reads as having just looked
  them up. Hold it for when he names a market — then it is knowledge, not
  research.
- **No links, no attachment, no product names.** He asked a geography question.
- **No claim of semiconductor, cleanroom or data centre experience.** You have
  none. The site-establishment observation holds for large complex facilities
  generally, and that is why it works.
- **No price.**

## For the message after this one

If he names Germany or continental Europe, the honest and strong position is
that the advisory work travels, and that a European delivery capability would
be built around a named local supply chain on a specific project rather than
claimed in advance. Offering to do the first piece as advisory, where geography
genuinely does not constrain you, is the route that does not require a promise
you cannot keep.

Their Frankfurt position is also the interface argument in its purest form:
three large concurrent data centre builds in one region, recruiting 250 people,
competing for the same local supply chain, welfare and accommodation in the same
window. That is worth saying once he has opened the door to specifics — and not
before.
