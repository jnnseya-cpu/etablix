# Woolavington — Balfour Beatty, National Grid, the Gravity Enterprise Zone

**The post.** Balfour Beatty plc, four hours old, edited. Supporting National
Grid on a new 400/132kV substation at Woolavington in Somerset, in the Gravity
Enterprise Zone, connecting Agratas' new electric vehicle battery
manufacturing facility to the high-voltage transmission network. Main
construction now underway. Aerial photograph showing the compound, its
boundary, laydown and car parking in open fields.

## The angle

**An enterprise zone is not one project, it is a place several of them land
in.** This substation exists to serve a gigafactory on the same zone, and the
post itself says the facility is new and its operations are future — so the
factory and the connection are on the same ground at the same time, and more
will follow, because that is what an enterprise zone is for.

**The local market does not see two employers, it sees one peak.** Beds, plant
hire, security, catering, and the roads around Woolavington are a single pool
drawn on by every compound on the zone. Each project plans its establishment
against its own peak, which is a market the others are bidding into on the
same dates. Whoever books late pays the difference, and on a zone with a fixed
energisation date for the anchor tenant nobody can simply move their peak to
avoid it.

**Why it is new.** The closest used angles are remoteness with no local supply
chain, and scale against walking distance. Both are about one project against
its surroundings. This is about several projects against each other in the
same surroundings, which only arises where an enterprise zone or a cluster
puts them there — and it could not have been written for any of the others.

**Why it is not a criticism.** "Each usually planned against its own peak" is a
statement about how the industry plans, not about this project or this
contractor. Nobody is named as getting it wrong, and nothing claims to know
what Balfour Beatty or National Grid have or have not done here.
