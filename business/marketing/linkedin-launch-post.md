# LinkedIn — the ETABLIX launch post

The temptation with a launch post is to announce a company. Nobody cares that
a company exists. What they respond to is a problem they recognise, described
by someone who was clearly in the room — and then, almost incidentally, the
thing that was built to fix it.

So the post opens on the observation, not the announcement. The first two
lines are all most people see before "…see more", and they have to earn the
click on their own.

**Length: 2,050 characters.** LinkedIn allows 3,000; the sweet spot is between
1,300 and 2,100. Three hashtags, no emoji, no "excited to announce".

## The post

> Every EPC and turnkey contract I worked on carried the same hidden scope.
>
> The civils. The site management. The site infrastructure. Inside the contract
> from the day it was signed — and owned by a business that was never set up to
> deliver it.
>
> So it got built from nothing, every time. Priced by engineers who had other
> jobs. Surveyed by whoever could get on a plane. Written up into a site report.
> Then handed to sourcing to go and find a civils contractor, who re-priced the
> whole thing from scratch anyway.
>
> That is not a procurement failing. It is a missing capability, and it repeats
> on every job that carries the scope.
>
> I spent years on the inside of it — construction subcontract manager at GE
> Vernova, on projects across the UK, Ireland and Europe. Before that, risk and
> change management and then pre-construction at UK Power Reserve, on a
> programme that built more than thirty new generation and storage sites across
> England and Wales.
>
> Today I am launching ETABLIX.
>
> We do one thing: the serviced environment around a live project, as one
> accountable package. Site establishment and compound. Temporary power and
> water. Welfare and washroom provision. Waste and cleaning. Logistics and
> security. Workforce accommodation where the labour cannot travel daily.
>
> Not building fabric. Not permanent works. We are not a main contractor and
> will not pretend to be.
>
> Most people will start with a Site Systems Diagnostic — a fixed fee, ten
> working days from the day the information pack lands, twelve deliverables. It
> reads your own documents against each other and reports where the site
> establishment does not hold: the consents nobody worked backwards from, the
> welfare sized against a headcount that has since moved, the packages with no
> owner. The final appendix lists which of your own documents contradict which.
> You keep it whatever you decide to do next.
>
> If you are carrying that scope on a live bid or a project about to mobilise,
> I would like to hear how you are handling it.
>
> ETABLIX — Integrated Site Services. Birmingham. A trading name of JNN GLOBAL
> LTD, part of Groupe Nseya.
>
> #Construction #Infrastructure #SiteServices

## The shorter version

If you would rather lead with something people finish reading, this is 940
characters and loses none of the argument:

> On every turnkey contract I worked on, the civils, the site management and
> the site infrastructure were inside our own scope from day one — and owned by
> a business that was never set up to deliver them.
>
> So that scope got built from nothing, every time. Priced by engineers who had
> other jobs. Surveyed by whoever could get on a plane. Then handed to sourcing
> to find a contractor who re-priced it from scratch anyway.
>
> It is not a procurement failing. It is a missing capability, and it repeats on

## Posting notes

**Put the link in the first comment, not the post.** LinkedIn suppresses posts
that send people off-platform. Post the text, then immediately comment with:
"More on how it works: etablix.com". Pin that comment.

**Tuesday to Thursday, 07:30–09:00 UK.** Construction is awake early and reads
on the way in. Avoid Friday afternoon and avoid Monday before nine.

**Answer every comment on the first day, personally and at length.** The
first two hours decide how far it travels, and a real reply is worth more than
any amount of pre-planning. Do not reply "thanks!" — reply with a sentence
that adds something.

**Ask five people beforehand, privately.** Not "please like my post" — send it
to five people who know the problem and ask whether the description is right.
The ones who agree will comment on their own, and a comment from a name your
sector recognises does more than a hundred likes.

**Do not tag companies or people who have not agreed to it.** Tagging GE
Vernova, a former employer, or a contact who has not seen the post first is
the fastest way to make a launch look like an ad.

**Update your headline and the company page before you post,** not after. The
first thing anybody does with a post like this is click the name.

## What is deliberately not in it

- **No price.** The fee follows the pack and the project. A number in a launch
  post invites a comparison against whatever the reader has in mind for a
  document, and this is not a document.
- **No client names, no case study, no testimonial.** There is no delivery
  record under the company yet. The credibility here is the work you did, which
  is real; borrowing anything beyond it would be the one thing that undoes it.
- **No "excited to announce", no rocket, no journey.** The people you want
  reading this price site establishment for a living and have seen a thousand
  of those.
