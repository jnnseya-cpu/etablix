# Answering an inbound subcontractor approach

First one received 8 October 2026: JP Civils & Groundworks, John Pickthall,
asking about defined civils and groundworks packages and about live or
forthcoming opportunities. There will be more, so the shape of the answer is
recorded here.

## The one thing that must not be fudged

**There are no live packages, and the reply must say so in those words.**

The temptation is real. A capable civils subcontractor on the register
strengthens the supply chain answer in every bid, and a vaguer reply — "nothing
immediate, but we have a strong pipeline" — would hold his interest longer. It
would also be a lie by implication, and the cost lands later: a subcontractor who
registers on the strength of an implied package and hears nothing for a year
tells other subcontractors. This is a small market and the register is only worth
something if the people on it trust what they were told.

Say it plainly, early, and in the paragraph that answers the question he actually
asked. Everything after it is then believable.

## Three things to explain, because most people assume otherwise

1. **ETABLIX is not a main contractor.** It buys the temporary site environment
   around the permanent works. If they build the structure, we are not their
   client; if they establish, service, operate or remove the site around it, we
   are. That one sentence saves a wasted conversation.
2. **On most engagements the client contracts directly with each supplier.**
   Under the Management Integrator model we specify the package, run the enquiry,
   assess returns and recommend award; the contract is placed in the client's
   name. So the register is a route to the enquiry, not a route to a contract
   with us.
3. **Registration is not a promise of work**, and saying so costs nothing. A
   supplier who is told that and registers anyway is a supplier who will still
   answer the phone in a year.

## And ask for the documents anyway

Insurances, accreditations and a capability statement at registration, not later.
When a package does arrive it arrives with a short runway, and the suppliers
already prequalified are the ones who get asked. That is also true, which is why
it works as a reason.

## Make it two-way

A subcontractor already working on schemes sees establishment being assembled
package by package with nobody holding it together. That is a lead source, and an
introduction from someone inside a project is worth more than a registration.
Invite it — once, lightly, at the end.
