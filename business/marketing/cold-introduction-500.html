500 addresses, one job: get forwarded

Almost none of these inboxes belongs to the person who buys this. A switchboard address and a recruitment address have one thing in common — whoever reads them decides in four seconds whether to forward or delete, and they cannot buy anything either way. So the first email is not a pitch. Its entire job is to name the person it needs to reach clearly enough that a stranger can forward it without thinking. The pitch is the second email, and it goes to a named person who is expecting it.

Before you send one of them

Two of these will decide whether the other 500 words are ever read.

  • Prove the authentication on a real message first. The DNS is in better shape than it looked: SPF is published, and the selector hostingermail-a carries a real DKIM key (the other two Hostinger slots are empty, which is normal and harmless — a signature is checked against the selector it names). What DNS cannot tell you is whether your mail is actually SIGNED with that key. Send one message to a Gmail address, open it, choose Show original, and read the authentication results. Send nothing to this list until that line says dkim=pass spf=pass dmarc=pass.
  • Turn on DMARC reporting before the first batch, not after. The policy is published but names nobody to report to, so if 500 messages start failing authentication, nothing tells you. Add rua=mailto:dmarc@etablix.com to the _dmarc record and you find out from the receivers rather than from a customer who never replied.
  • Send from the company domain, never from a free mailbox. A message about construction commercial risk from a gmail.com address answers the credibility question before the reader reaches the second line, and answers it the wrong way.
  • Forty a day, by hand or by staged send. A new domain that emits 500 messages in an hour is indistinguishable from a compromised one. Four hundred a fortnight arrives; five hundred in one morning does not.
  • Skip the recruitment inboxes, or send them the two-line version. Business development into a recruitment queue is the fastest route to a spam complaint, and a complaint costs more than the address is worth. Where recruitment@ is the only address you hold, use the short variant below and expect nothing.
  • No attachment, one link. An attachment from an unknown sender is filtered before a person sees it. One plain link to etablix.com is enough; a person who wants the detail will click it.
  • Every message carries the legal footer and a real way to opt out. Corporate addresses at limited companies may lawfully be emailed for business purposes under PECR, and that permission depends on an unsubscribe route that works and a sender who is identified. Both are in the footer below. Honour an opt-out the day it arrives and remove the address from the file, not just from the next send.
  • Record who asked to be left alone. One list, one column, checked before every send. Emailing somebody twice after they have said no is the only thing on this page that turns a marketing decision into a regulatory one.

Email 1 — the one reception reads

Under 150 words. It has to fit on one phone screen, because that is where it will be read and forwarded from. Notice that it asks for a redirection, not a meeting: the person reading it can give you a redirection.

Subject line

Pick one and keep it for the whole list

A/B on the first 100
Option A — the redirection
For whoever prices site setup on your major bids
Option B — the question
Who owns site establishment pricing at bid stage?
Option C — the plainest
Please forward: pre-construction / site infrastructure

Why these work in a switchboard inbox. None of them says who you are, because the reader does not care yet. Each one names a department, which is the only decision the reader has to make. Avoid anything that reads as a service offer — "introducing", "our services", "partnership opportunity" — because those are the words the delete key is trained on.

Body

The forwardable email

142 words
Send as plain text
Hello,

Could you please forward this to whoever owns site setup and temporary works at bid stage — pre-construction, work winning, or the commercial lead on major projects. It is not a recruitment enquiry and I am not selling labour.

I was a construction subcontract manager at GE Vernova, on EPC and turnkey projects across the UK, Ireland and Europe, where the civils, the site management and the site infrastructure sat inside our own contract. The site establishment number in those bids was always built at speed, from client documents that did not agree with each other, by engineers who had other work on.

ETABLIX does that one piece of work properly, as a fixed-fee report in ten working days.

If you can tell me the right name, I will take it from there and stop emailing this address.

Thank you,

What each part is doing. The first line is an instruction a stranger can act on. The second sentence closes down the two wrong assumptions a reception desk makes about an unknown sender — agency and recruiter — before either has time to form. The credential is one clause, not a paragraph, and it is a job title at a named employer rather than an adjective. The last line is the part that gets it forwarded: you have offered to go away, which is the only thing that inbox actually wants.

The two-line variant for recruitment@ addresses
  • Subject: Wrong department — please redirect
  • Body: This is not a recruitment enquiry. Could you pass it to whoever owns site setup and temporary works pricing at bid stage? I will not email this address again. Thank you. Then the footer.

Model 01 · Advisory — the Site Systems Diagnostic

290 words
Send as plain text
Dear [First name],

Thank you for taking this. I will be direct about what it is.

On every major bid, somebody has to say what it costs to establish and run the site: the enabling civils, the compound, the temporary power and water, the welfare, the logistics, the accommodation where the labour cannot travel daily. That number is usually built in a fortnight, from a client information pack whose documents were written months apart by different people who have never read them side by side. The programme says one thing, the planning conditions say another, and the utility lead times say the access date was undeliverable before anybody priced it.

Nobody inside the bid has time to read all eight documents against each other. That is the whole of what we do.

The Site Systems Diagnostic is a fixed-fee report delivered in ten working days from the day your information pack lands. Twelve deliverables: the package map, the scope gaps, the interface matrix, the workforce and welfare demand, the temporary utilities load, the mobilisation constraints with every consent worked backwards from your own dates, the procurement strategy, the risk register, an indicative cost structure and the first ninety days of actions with owners. The appendix is a reconciliation ledger — which of your own documents contradict which, with both sources quoted.

It is yours to keep whatever you do next. There is no retainer behind it and nothing to sign beyond the engagement itself.

If it is useful, the honest test is a live one: give it a bid you are pricing now, or a project about to mobilise, and judge it on whether the first two pages tell you something your team had not already found.

Ten minutes on a call, or send me the project and I will tell you whether it is worth doing.

Kind regards,

Why this is the persuasive one. It never claims to be better than their team. It claims to do a thing their team demonstrably has no time to do, and it names the thing precisely enough that the reader recognises their own last bid in it. The reconciliation ledger is the sentence that sells: it is the one deliverable a main contractor cannot produce internally at bid stage, and every commercial manager who has been surprised on site knows exactly what it would have been worth.

Two things to keep out of it
  • No price. The fee follows the pack and the project. Naming one in a cold email invites a comparison against a number they have in their head for a document, and this is not a document.
  • No client names and no case study. You have not delivered one under this banner yet. Say what the work is; never imply a track record you cannot evidence. The credibility here is the job you did, and that is real.

Email 3 — the single follow-up

Seven working days later, once, to people who did not reply. Then stop and leave them alone. A second chase on a cold list buys nothing and costs the domain.

Reply in the same thread — no new subject
Hello [First name],

Bringing this back once, in case it arrived in a bad week.

The offer is narrow: one fixed-fee report, ten working days, on a live bid or a project about to mobilise, telling you where your own information pack disagrees with itself before it costs you anything on site.

If it is not for you, a one-line no is genuinely useful and I will close the file.

Kind regards,

Why "a one-line no is useful" belongs in it. It gives a busy reader the cheapest possible action, and the reply rate to a request for a no is higher than the reply rate to a request for a meeting. Every no you collect also cleans the list, which is worth more than the send.

What good looks like on 500 cold addresses
  • Most will never be read by the right person. That is the arithmetic of a switchboard list, not a failure of the writing.
  • Ten to twenty redirections to a named person is a good return, and those names are the asset — they outlast this campaign.
  • One or two conversations that reach a live bid is what pays for the whole exercise, because the first paid diagnostic is also the first reference.
