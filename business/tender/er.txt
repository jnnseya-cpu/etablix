Contents 
 
1  Introduction and status of this document 
1.1  Purpose 
These Employer's Requirements define the accommodation and amenity buildings required for the workforce village serving Project NORTHREACH — Workforce Accommodation Village. They are issued as part of the tender documents for P2 — Modular Accommodation and form part of the subcontract on award. 
1.2  Document hierarchy 
Where documents conflict, the following order prevails, the first named taking precedence: 
1.   The subcontract conditions and any amendments 
2.   These Employer's Requirements 
3.   The fire strategy issued at Appendix 2 
4.   The drawings listed in the Drawings and Documents Register at section 11 
5.   The Contractor's Proposals as accepted under 13.4, to the extent they do not conflict with the above 
A tenderer who identifies a conflict before the clarification deadline shall raise it. A conflict raised after award is resolved by the order above and at the tenderer's cost. 
1.3  Design responsibility 
This is a contractor-designed package. Section 13 states, element by element, what the Employer has fixed and what the Contractor designs, and every line of every room data sheet carries a code saying which of the two it is. A tenderer should read section 13 before reading anything else in this document, because it determines what the rest of it means.

4  Technical specification, by element 
4.1  Interface with substructure 
Modules are set on bases constructed under P1 Civil Works. The Contractor shall issue setting-out drawings and a base tolerance specification not later than eight weeks before the first delivery. Level tolerance across any single base: ±5 mm. Plan position tolerance: ±10 mm. Relative level between adjacent bases supporting one module: ±3 mm. The Contractor shall inspect and accept the bases in writing before delivery; delivery to unaccepted bases is at the Contractor's risk. The Contractor shall also state, at tender, the ground bearing pressure imposed by its craneage under the outrigger condition at 4.21, so that P1 can design the hardstanding to it rather than to an assumption. 
4.2  Module frame and structure 
Steel or timber framed volumetric modules, designed to BS EN 1990 and the relevant material Eurocode, for the loadings at Appendix 8. Design working life 25 years minimum notwithstanding the intended 38-month deployment, for the relocation reason stated at 13.7 — the design life is required because the modules will be dismantled, transported and re-erected at least twice, not because they will stand here for 25 years. All lifting points designed, certified and clearly marked; a lifting plan issued with each delivery. Serviceability, deflection and the temporary condition during installation are at 4.27. 
4.3  External walls 
Composite panel or framed construction achieving the U-value at 6. External finish: factory-applied coated steel or fibre cement, colour from the approved range at Appendix 5, and meeting the durability and colour fastness requirements at 4.22. Impact resistance to withstand normal site traffic adjacent to haul routes. All penetrations sealed and weathered; no site-applied sealant relied upon as the primary weather line. 
4.4  Roof 
Pitched or flat to falls not less than 1:80 after deflection. Single-ply membrane or profiled sheet, with a 20-year manufacturer's material warranty assigned to the Employer at handover. Rainwater goods sized to BS EN 12056-3 for the design rainfall at Appendix 8, discharging to the drainage provided under P1 at the termination points scheduled at 5.2. Weathertightness testing, roof access and fall protection are at 4.22. 
4.5  Windows and external doors 
As the room data sheets. All external doors: weather-sealed, with a threshold not exceeding 15 mm, measured after P1's finished external levels are laid — the threshold is achievable only if those levels are right, which is why it is scheduled at IF-15 and surveyed jointly before P1 surfaces around each block. Escape doors to open in the direction of escape and be openable without a key from the inside at all times, in a single action, in accordance with the fire strategy at Appendix 2. Any electrically held escape door shall be FAIL-SAFE — see 4.18, which distinguishes escape doors from bedroom doors and states a different failure mode for each. 
4.6  Internal walls and partitions 
Full height to the underside of the floor above, achieving the acoustic and fire performance scheduled at 6. No service penetration shall reduce the rated performance; all penetrations firestopped by a third-party certified installer and recorded in the firestopping register required at 10.4. Where a partition forms part of the acoustic separation, the Contractor shall demonstrate that the flanking path through the module floor, ceiling and inter-module joint does not defeat it; a laboratory figure for the partition alone is not evidence of compliance. 
4.7  Corridors and circulation 
Minimum clear corridor width 1200 mm, and 1500 mm where required by the fire strategy or by BS 8300-2 for accessible routes. Note the consequence at the room data sheets: a 1200 mm corridor sets the door clear opening width, and a Contractor proposing a narrower corridor changes a requirement it does not own. Wall protection to 1200 mm AFFL throughout, corner protection at every change of direction. Floor finish continuous, slip resistance PTV ≥ 36 wet and dry. 
4.8  Stairs 
Where blocks are two storey: stairs to the fire strategy, minimum 1200 mm clear width between handrails, handrails both sides at 900–1000 mm, tactile and visual contrast to nosings with a luminance contrast of not less than 30 LRV points. External stairs to have a slip resistance maintained when wet and either a means of preventing ice accretion or a stated winter maintenance regime, which shall be handed to P5 at IF-08 and not left as an operational discovery. 
4.9  Internal finishes 
As the room data sheets for rooms and ensuites. Circulation and amenity areas: wall finishes wipe-clean to 1200 mm minimum; floors slip resistant and welded. No carpet in any circulation or amenity space. All finishes to be maintainable with standard commercial cleaning products, and a schedule of approved products issued in the O&M manual — that schedule is the reference for every cleanability requirement in the room data sheets. 
4.10  Mechanical services — heating 
The requirement is the duty stated in the room data sheets, not an emitter. The Contractor shall select, size, position and control the heating to meet 21 °C dry resultant at the external design condition at Appendix 8, and the 90-minute heat-up requirement, and shall submit the calculation under 13.5. Central plant, where offered, to be located, acoustically treated and access-provisioned to 4.14. The tender shall state the resulting connected load per block, which is checked against 4.24. 
4.11  Mechanical services — water 
Hot and cold water distribution from the central plant provided under this package. Design to CIBSE Guide G and the Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999. The supply characteristics at the point of connection, the metering and the isolation strategy are at 4.25. Dead legs minimised and recorded; every dead leg exceeding 3 pipe diameters to be scheduled in the O&M. Hot water stored at 60 °C minimum and distributed at 50 °C minimum at every outlet within one minute, with outlet temperature limited to 41 °C by TMV3 valves at every basin and shower. 
4.12  Mechanical services — legionella 
The Contractor shall prepare and hand over a written scheme of control to HSG274 Part 2 and ACOP L8. THE DUTY PASSES AT FIRST FILL, NOT AT FIRST OCCUPATION. Where the system is filled and then stands at low or nil occupancy before handover, the Contractor shall operate and record a flushing regime, and shall hand over the log with the O&M manual. 4.18  Access control, and the failure mode on fire alarm 
This clause states two different failure modes for two different door types. Confusing them is the single most serious error available in this package. 
BEDROOM DOORS. Escape from a bedroom shall be mechanical, in a single action, from the inside, at all times, without a key, without a tool and without electrical power. The electronic lock therefore controls entry only, and never egress. On that basis the lock shall be FAIL-SECURE: it shall remain locked against entry on loss of power, on battery depletion and ON FIRE ALARM ACTIVATION. Fire and rescue service access to a locked bedroom shall be by mechanical override key held in a secure key cabinet at the fire alarm panel and recorded in the premises information box, and this arrangement shall be agreed in writing with the fire and rescue service before commissioning. Battery-operated locks shall give a low-battery indication locally and to the village management system at not less than 20% remaining capacity, and shall retain the mechanical override and the mechanical egress function when the battery is wholly depleted. 
ESCAPE ROUTE DOORS. Any door on an escape route held closed or locked by electrical means — cross-corridor doors, final exit doors, any door with a magnetic lock — shall be FAIL-SAFE: it shall release on fire alarm activation, on loss of power and on operation of a green break-glass unit adjacent to the door, in accordance with BS 7273-4 Actuation Type A. These doors shall be listed individually in the cause and effect matrix at 4.15. 
GENERAL. Audit trail retained for 90 days minimum. Master, floor-master and housekeeping key hierarchies agreed with the Employer and with P5 before commissioning, per IF-09. Mechanical override keys handed over under a documented key control procedure. The Contractor shall submit, with its tender, a door schedule stating for every door in the package which of the two regimes above applies to it.

4.26  Evacuation strategy and the construction site alarm interface (Rev B) 
These are the Employer's decisions. They are stated here because Rev A did not state them, and a Contractor cannot design a fire alarm system without them. 
EVACUATION. Simultaneous evacuation of the whole of an accommodation block on the operation of any detector or call point in that block. Phased evacuation shall not be used. Travel distances and the two-storey arrangement do not require it, and phased evacuation of sleeping occupants who have been woken is not a strategy that survives being tested at three in the morning. 
EACH BLOCK IS A SEPARATE EVACUATION ENTITY. An alarm in Block A shall not evacuate Block B. Evacuating 225 people simultaneously, at night, onto a site adjoining live construction, creates a greater hazard than the one being managed. Blocks shall be separately zoned and separately sounded, and the fire strategy at Appendix 2 shall confirm the separation distance that permits it. 
VOICE ALARM. Not required. Sounders and, in RT-02 and the amenity buildings, visual alarm devices to BS EN 54-23. The reason is recorded so it is not reopened at value engineering: the workforce is multilingual, a spoken instruction in English cannot be assumed to be understood by every occupant, and a fire alarm tone is universal. Where the Contractor believes a voice alarm is nonetheless required by the fire strategy, it shall raise it as a tender query and not price it silently. 
INTERFACE WITH THE CONSTRUCTION SITE. The village fire alarm system shall NOT be interlinked with the construction site alarm system, in either direction. A construction site evacuation shall not sound in sleeping accommodation, and a village alarm shall not stop work on site. Both shall be separately monitored to the same alarm receiving centre to BS EN 50518, with separate zone identification, and the ARC shall be instructed which response applies to which. This shall be confirmed in writing with the Principal Contractor before commissioning, and is scheduled at IF-13. Zoning across the kitchen building, where a detector over a cooking range must not evacuate sleeping accommodation, is scheduled separately at IF-16. 
UNWANTED ALARMS. Target not exceeding one unwanted alarm per 100 detectors per year, reported monthly to P5. Detector selection shall be made to achieve it: no optical smoke detector shall be installed within an ensuite, and the room detector shall be positioned not less than 1.5 m from the ensuite door opening, because steam from a shower is the most common cause of a night-time unwanted alarm in accommodation of this type. In a village where an unwanted alarm wakes a night-shift worker who then works the following day, this is a safety requirement and not a convenience. 
ASSEMBLY. Assembly points on the village side of the segregation line at 10.3, never on the construction site, illuminated to 5 lux with the route to them illuminated to the same level, and identified on the fire action notice in every block. 
4.27  Structural serviceability and the temporary condition (Rev B) 
DEFLECTION. Floors: not exceeding span/360 under imposed load and span/250 under total load, with a further limit of 15 mm absolute in any bedroom. Footfall-induced vibration: response factor not exceeding 8 to SCI P354, assessed for a corridor and for a bedroom floor — a modular floor cassette that meets the deflection limit can still be unacceptable to a sleeping occupant on the floor below. External wall panels: not exceeding span/360 under the design wind load, and not exceeding the glazing manufacturer's limit at every window head. 
DIFFERENTIAL MOVEMENT. The Contractor shall state the differential movement it has designed for between adjacent modules and between the module and the base, and shall demonstrate that the inter-module joint at 4.22, the internal linings and the services connections accommodate it without loss of fire, acoustic or weather performance. 
TEMPORARY CONDITION. The Contractor shall demonstrate the stability of a module in every condition between leaving the factory and being fully connected: on the transport vehicle, during the lift, when set but not yet connected to its neighbours, and when a block is part-erected and left overnight or over a weekend. The overnight and weekend cases shall be designed for the wind speed with the appropriate return period for the exposure period, not the full design wind, and the Contractor shall state the wind speed at which a part-erected block must be tied down or completed. Racking of the module during lifting shall be controlled by design rather than by care. 
SUBMISSION. All of the above under 13.5, before manufacture.

13  Design responsibility 
13.1  This is a contractor-designed package 
THIS IS THE CLAUSE THAT REV A DID NOT HAVE, AND ITS ABSENCE WAS THE MOST SERIOUS FAULT IN THAT REVISION. Without it a tenderer cannot know what it is being asked to warrant, and every competent tenderer would have returned a qualified offer. Qualified offers cannot be compared with one another, which would have defeated the evaluation model issued with this tender. 
This package is let on a contractor-designed basis. The Employer has fixed the things listed as ED in the matrix at 13.3 and has stated a required performance for everything else. The Contractor is responsible for the design of everything not marked ED, for its integration with the elements that are, and for the design's compliance with statute. The Employer's design is not warranted by the Employer beyond its own statement: where the Contractor considers that an element marked ED prevents it from meeting a performance requirement, it shall say so as a tender query before tender return, or as a departure under 13.4, and shall not price around it silently. 
13.2  The basis codes on the room data sheets 
Every line of every room data sheet at section 3 carries a code in its final column. 
E — EMPLOYER-PRESCRIBED. The Employer has specified the article, the dimension or the arrangement. The Contractor shall provide what is described. If what is described does not achieve a performance stated elsewhere in this document, that is the Employer's problem and the Contractor's duty is to identify the conflict, not to resolve it silently. The Contractor is still responsible for the workmanship, the installation and compliance with statute. 
C — CONTRACTOR-DESIGNED. The Employer has stated a duty, a rate, a temperature, an illuminance or a classification. The Contractor selects the product, sizes it, positions it, installs it and warrants that it achieves the stated duty. A Contractor who meets the letter of a C line with an article that does not achieve the duty has not complied. 
Rev A mixed the two without deciding between them. It specified, for example, a 1.0 kW panel heater. The effect of specifying the heater rather than the duty was to make the Employer responsible for the room being warm, which is the opposite of what this package was let to achieve. Rev B states the duty — 21 °C dry resultant at the external design condition, and a 90-minute heat-up — and leaves the emitter to the Contractor. Twenty-two lines were converted in this way.

16  Sustainability, carbon and social value 
16.1  Status of this section 
The tender is evaluated on sustainability within the quality criteria and on social value at 5%. Rev A asked for neither, which meant the evaluation had nothing to measure and every tenderer would have been scored on an assertion. This section states what is asked for so that what is scored is a commitment. 
16.2  Embodied carbon 
The Contractor shall declare, with its tender, the embodied carbon of the modules and their fit-out: modules A1 to A5 to the RICS Whole Life Carbon Assessment for the Built Environment, 2nd edition, expressed in kgCO₂e in total and per m² GIA, with the calculation, the data sources and the assumptions disclosed. Environmental product declarations to BS EN 15804 shall be provided for the ten products of greatest mass in the build-up. 
The Contractor shall also declare modules B and C so far as they can be estimated, and shall state separately the effect of relocation under 13.7 — the avoided A1 to A3 impact of a second and third deployment, and the C and D consequences of designing for disassembly. This is where the carbon case for a relocatable village is actually made, and a declaration that stops at A5 misses it. 
No cap is imposed. A cap without a credible baseline for volumetric modular accommodation of this type would be an arbitrary number, and an arbitrary number is either met by an arbitrary calculation or priced as a risk. What is required is a disclosed, comparable declaration, and it is scored. 
16.3  Operational energy 
The Contractor shall declare the connected load per block arising from its design under 4.10 and 4.24, and the predicted annual energy consumption per bed using the same model as the TM59 assessment at section 6, stating the occupancy pattern used. The metering at 4.24 and 4.25 shall be capable of testing that declaration in operation, and P5 will do so. A declaration that cannot be checked against measured data is not a declaration. 
16.4  Waste and circularity 
The waste requirements at 10.5 apply. In addition the Contractor shall submit a design-for-disassembly statement: every connection between modules and between module and base shall be demountable without destroying the component, or the Contractor shall state which components are consumed and schedule them under 13.7. Packaging shall be taken back or recycled and shall not be an on-site disposal problem for the Employer. Protection materials applied in the factory shall be removable without solvent damage to the finish beneath. 
16.5  Social value 
The Contractor shall offer commitments against the Employer's framework at Appendix 12, covering as a minimum: apprenticeship and training weeks delivered on this package; employment of people resident within the travel-to-work area stated in that framework, including people who have been unemployed for more than six months; spend with small and medium-sized enterprises and with voluntary, community and social enterprises; and one commitment directed at the people who will live in this village. 
Commitments offered are contractual. They shall be reported quarterly against the same units in which they were offered, and a shortfall shall be made good or explained. A commitment that is not measured is marketing, and it will be scored as such. 
 
17  Change control 
17.1  What is, and is not, an instruction 
A change to this document is effected only by a written instruction issued by the Employer's Representative, who is named in the contract. Nobody else can instruct a change. In particular, a communication from the Employer's fire engineer, from P5 or its staff, from the Principal Contractor, from another package contractor, from a designer, from a visiting officer of the Employer or from anyone on site is not an instruction, whatever its tone. The Contractor shall not act on one, and shall refer it to the Employer's Representative within two working days. 
A Contractor who carries out additional work on a verbal request has no entitlement to be paid for it. This is stated plainly because it is the most common way in which a contractor of this size loses money on a project of this type. 
17.2  How a change is valued 
In this order: first, the rates and prices in the activity schedule at Appendix 1 where the changed work is of the same character and executed under the same conditions; second, those rates and prices pro-rata where the character is the same but the conditions or quantity differ; third, a fair valuation built from the all-in rates the Contractor states in its tender. 
The Contractor shall therefore state in its tender: all-in hourly rates for each trade and grade it will employ on site and in the factory; the percentage addition for overheads; the percentage addition for profit; the percentage addition for sub-contracted work; and the rates for the principal items of plant including craneage per shift and per hour. These are competed at tender, which is the only point at which competition exists. They are fixed for the contract period and apply to every change. 
17.3  The effect of a change on time and on design 
No change carries an extension of time unless the Contractor gives notice under 18.3. A change instructed after the design freeze for the affected element carries the cost of the abortive design, which shall be quoted before the instruction is issued rather than claimed after. The Contractor shall provide a quotation for every proposed change stating the cost, the effect on the programme and the effect on any other requirement in this document, within ten working days of being asked; where it cannot, it shall say so and state when it can, within the same ten days. 
17.4  Changes proposed by the Contractor 
The Contractor may propose a change at any time. A proposal shall state the effect on every requirement in this document, and shall be honest about which requirements it reduces. 
Where a proposal reduces cost without reducing any stated performance, the saving is shared equally between the parties. Where it reduces a stated performance, it is not a saving and it is not value engineering: it is a departure under 13.4 and shall be presented as one, with the performance reduction stated in the terms in which the requirement was written. The Employer will consider it on that basis and may accept it. What the Employer will not accept is a reduction presented as an efficiency. 
 
18  Sectional completion, delay and liquidated damages 
18.1  Sectional completion 
This package completes in sections against the bed demand curve at Appendix 6, not on a single date. The sectional completion dates and the number of beds required at each are in that appendix and are contractual. Each section is complete when the beds in it are commissioned, the handover deliverables at section 9 relating to them have been received, and the Employer has certified it. A section is not complete because the modules are set. 
18.2  Liquidated damages, and the calculation behind them 
Liquidated damages of £145 per bed per calendar day apply to each section, from the sectional completion date until that section is certified complete, subject to an aggregate cap of 7.5% of the contract sum. 
The figure is stated with its build-up because a rate that is not a genuine pre-estimate of the Employer's loss is a penalty and will not be enforced, and both parties are better served by a number that survives being looked at. If a bed is not available, the worker it was for is accommodated commercially and travels further, and the loss is built up as follows.

Liquidated damages are the Employer's exclusive financial remedy for late completion of a section. They are not the remedy for a failure to meet a performance requirement, which is dealt with under section 19. 
18.3  Extension of time 
The Contractor is entitled to an extension of time where completion of a section is delayed by: a change instructed under section 17; an act of prevention by the Employer or by another package contractor for whom the Employer is responsible; the Employer's failure to give access, information or a decision by a date in the accepted programme; a delay to an interface at section 5 that is not the Contractor's; exceptionally adverse weather measured against the ten-year average for the site as recorded by the nearest Met Office station; a change in statutory requirement after the tender return date; or the abnormal load route at Appendix 7 proving unavailable, that risk being the Employer's and stated as such at 11.1. 
Notice shall be given within ten working days of the Contractor becoming aware of the event, with the entitlement demonstrated on the accepted baseline programme by time impact analysis. A claim that identifies a cause and asserts a period without showing the critical path is not a demonstration. 
Where a delay for which the Employer is responsible is concurrent with a delay for which the Contractor is responsible, the Contractor is entitled to time but not to money for the period of concurrency. This is stated in advance because it is the point on which such disputes turn and it is cheaper to agree it now. 
18.4  Float 
Float in the accepted programme is a project resource. It belongs to the project and not to either party, and it is available to whichever party first has need of it. Neither party may consume it merely to protect a position. 
The Contractor shall show total float on every activity in the accepted baseline programme and in every update. It shall not conceal float by inflating durations, by inserting artificial logic or by setting constraint dates that are not real; the Employer will review the programme for these and will reject one that contains them. 
Terminal float — the period between the Contractor's planned completion of a section and the contractual date for it — is the Contractor's, and the Contractor is not obliged to give it up. But it is float: a delay does not entitle the Contractor to an extension of time until it has consumed the float on the affected path and moved the sectional completion date. That follows from the definition of float and is stated here so that it is not argued about later. 
18.5  Early warning 
The Contractor and the Employer shall each notify the other as soon as either becomes aware of any matter that could increase the cost, delay a sectional completion date or impair the performance of the village in use. A joint risk register shall be maintained and reviewed monthly, recording each risk, its owner, the action agreed and the date by which it is to be taken. A matter that could have been notified and was not, and which the other party could have mitigated had it known, shall be taken into account when the consequences are assessed. 
18.6  Acceleration 
Where a section is forecast late for a cause that is the Contractor's, the Contractor shall take, at its own cost, whatever steps are necessary to recover, and shall submit a recovery programme within ten working days of the forecast. Where the Employer wishes to complete a section earlier than the contractual date, or to recover a delay for which it is itself responsible, it may ask the Contractor to quote for acceleration; the quotation shall be given before any acceleration is instructed, and shall state the measures, their cost and the time recovered. Acceleration shall not be instructed retrospectively and shall not be assumed from an instruction to work harder. 
 
19  Defects, obsolescence and support after completion 
19.1  Defects liability period 
Twelve months from the date of certified completion of each section, running separately for each section. Where a defect is rectified, the period for that element in that room begins again from the date of rectification. Where the same defect occurs in more than five per cent of the rooms in a section, it is a systemic defect: the Contractor shall investigate the cause, report it, and rectify every instance of it including those not yet reported, and the period for that element runs again across the whole section. 
19.2  Response and rectification 
Priority 1 — loss of heating, hot water, cold water or power to a block; a fire alarm fault; a failure of the access control system affecting more than one room; a bedroom door that cannot be secured; an escaping water leak. Attend within four hours of notification, make safe or provide a temporary measure within eight hours, complete the permanent rectification within five working days. 
Priority 2 — loss of heating, hot water or power to a single room; a failed extract; a single failed lock; a failed emergency luminaire; a defect that makes a room unfit to occupy. Attend within twenty-four hours, rectify within five working days. 
Priority 3 — finishes, joinery, cosmetic damage and anything not affecting the use of the room. Rectify within twenty working days. Priority 3 items only may be batched. 
ACCESS. Rooms are occupied. Access to an occupied room is arranged through P5 with not less than twenty-four hours' notice to the occupant, except for a Priority 1 defect where access is immediate. The Contractor shall allow in its pricing for working around occupancy, for out-of-hours attendance where an occupant works nights, and for the fact that a village of 225 single-occupancy rooms on shift rotation has no convenient time. A delay caused by the Contractor's failure to arrange access is not an excuse for a missed rectification time. 
Where the Contractor fails to meet a response or rectification time, the Employer may have the work done by others and recover the cost, after giving forty-eight hours' notice of its intention to do so. 
19.3  Retention 
Retention of three per cent of each payment, reduced to one and a half per cent on certification of the last sectional completion, and released fourteen days after the last defects certificate. 
Three per cent is stated rather than the more usual five because the payment profile of a modular package is front-loaded into manufacture, and five per cent of a manufacturing-heavy contract sum is a working capital cost the Contractor will price back to the Employer at a higher rate than the Employer's own cost of capital. The Employer would rather hold less and rely on the rectification remedy at 19.2. A retention bond from a surety acceptable to the Employer may be substituted for the whole of the retention at any time, at the Contractor's cost. 
19.4  Obsolescence and support for proprietary systems 
This applies to the access control system, the fire alarm system, the village management system, the metering, the wireless network and any other system whose function depends on a manufacturer's software or firmware. 
SUPPORT PERIOD. For each such system the Contractor shall state at tender: the manufacturer's committed support period from the date of handover; the committed spares availability period; the notice the manufacturer gives of end-of-life; and whether the protocol is open or proprietary. Support and spares shall be available for not less than the thirty-eight month deployment plus twenty-four months. Where a manufacturer will not commit to that, the Contractor shall say so at tender rather than after. 
OPEN PROTOCOL. Metering shall report on an open protocol. The fire alarm panel shall be either an open protocol panel or one whose protocol is licensed to the Employer, so that the Employer is not tied to a single maintainer for the life of the village. The Contractor shall state at tender any respect in which the Employer would be so tied. 
SUBSCRIPTIONS. No system shall be offered in which a safety or security function — detection, alarm transmission, door locking, emergency lighting monitoring — ceases or degrades if a subscription, licence or cloud service is not renewed. Where a subscription is required for any other function, its cost for the deployment period plus twenty-four months shall be stated at tender and included in the price. 
CREDENTIALS AND CYBER SECURITY. Every default password shall be changed at commissioning. No shared account shall remain in use. A complete account and credential list, including any manufacturer's or maintainer's remote access, shall be handed to P5 under IF-09, and any remote access shall be disclosed at tender. Firmware update responsibility during the defects period is the Contractor's, and its intended policy shall be stated. 
RELOCATION. Every licence and configuration shall survive relocation under 13.7 to a different site and a different maintainer, or the Contractor shall state at tender that it does not. 
19.5  Spares and consumables 
Twelve months' consumables from the last sectional completion, and a critical spares holding covering every asset rated criticality 1 under 15.3, held on site or available within twenty-four hours from a stated location. Both are listed and priced separately at item J of the activity schedule so that the Employer can see what it is buying and can decline it knowingly. The spares list shall state, for each item, the asset it serves, the lead time if not held, and the effect on a room if it is not available.
