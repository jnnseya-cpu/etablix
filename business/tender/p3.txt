1  Introduction, and the one risk in this package 
1.1  The one risk in this package, and where it sits 
This package fits out a building it does not build, to serve a demand it does not control, for an operator it is not. 
The equipment is the easy part. Every serious risk in this package is at its boundary: the penetrations through a module that is manufactured in a factory four hundred miles away; the floor loading under a cold room; the drainage falls beneath a floor that is laid before anybody stands a kettle on it; the extract route through a roof somebody else warrants; the electrical supply; the ceiling height; and the width of the door the combi oven has to come through. 
EVERY ONE OF THOSE IS FIXED BEFORE P2 MANUFACTURES. After manufacture a penetration is a variation to a completed module built four hundred miles away, and it is priced accordingly. section 7 states what must be frozen, by whom, and by when, and it is the most important section in this document. 
The Employer draws the tenderer's attention to it in these terms because the classic failure of a kitchen fit-out package is that it is priced against a shell it did not draw, by a contractor who assumed the shell would suit it, and the discovery is made on site. 
1.2  Output specification, and where this document departs from it 
The default is an OUTPUT specification: the Employer states the duty and the Contractor selects, sizes and warrants the equipment that meets it. A kitchen specified by product schedule is a kitchen designed by the Employer, and the Employer then owns the question of whether it can produce the covers. 
Every line of the equipment schedule at section 5 carries a code. 
E — EMPLOYER-PRESCRIBED. The Employer has specified the article, the dimension or the arrangement. The Contractor provides what is described and the Employer carries the risk that what is described achieves the duty. 
C — CONTRACTOR-DESIGNED. The Employer has stated a duty — a number of covers in a number of minutes, a temperature, a throughput, a rate. The Contractor selects and sizes the equipment and warrants that it achieves the duty. 
A KITCHEN CARRIES MORE PRESCRIPTION THAN A SERVICE CONTRACT DOES, and the Employer states why rather than claiming otherwise: 25 of the 38 lines below are Employer-prescribed. They fall into three groups and no others — a statutory floor (63 °C hot holding, the suppression system, the extract failure mode); a food safety flow that is not a matter of preference (raw and ready-to-eat separation, dirty-to-clean routing); and an interface dimension that another package has to build to and cannot discover later. Everything else is a duty and the Contractor designs to it. 
1.3  What is NOT in this package 
The building. The shell, its structure, envelope, floor, roof, external doors, windows and the services up to the termination points are constructed under P2 Modular Accommodation. What P2 provides and in what state is at section 8. 
The operation. Food, staff, menus, HACCP, cleaning and the running of the kitchen are under P5 FM and Operation. This package hands over a commissioned kitchen and trains the people who will use it — see section 10. 
External drainage beyond the building, and the incoming utility supplies, are P1 Civil Works. 
Where a requirement of this document cannot be met because something provided under another package is inadequate, the Contractor's duty is to raise it before manufacture, not to work around it. A kitchen that works around its shell is a kitchen that is harder to clean, harder to maintain and shorter-lived than the one that was paid for.

6  Specification, element by element 
6.1  Design responsibility, and the Contractor's Proposals 
This is a contractor-designed fit-out. The Employer has fixed the shell, the area allocation at section 4, the demand at section 3, and the lines coded E at section 5. Everything else is the Contractor's design. 
The Contractor is responsible for: the kitchen layout within the allocated areas; the selection and sizing of every item coded C; the mechanical, electrical, drainage and ventilation design of the fit-out; the integration of all of it with the shell; and compliance with statute. 
WITH ITS TENDER the Contractor shall submit Contractor's Proposals: a layout at 1:50 showing the flow at 6.2, an equipment schedule against every line of section 5, the extract calculation, the electrical load calculation, the drainage design, and the schedule of loadings and penetrations required from the shell at section 7. 
EVERY DEPARTURE from this document shall be listed in a Schedule of Departures with the clause, what is offered, the reason, the effect on every other requirement, and the price effect as a plus or a minus. A departure not listed is not accepted, whether or not it is visible in the drawings. 
Review, comment or acceptance by the Employer does not transfer design responsibility. It applies to an accepted submission as fully as to a commented one. 
6.2  The flow, which is the layout requirement that outranks the others 
Food moves one way through this kitchen: DELIVERY → STORE → PREPARATION → COOKING → SERVICE. Waste and dirty equipment move the other way and the two routes shall not cross. 
The following are requirements, not preferences, and a layout that breaches any of them will be rejected however efficient it is: 
Raw meat and fish preparation is physically separate from ready-to-eat preparation, with its own sink, its own refrigeration and no shared route. 
The dirty goods-in and waste route does not pass through the production kitchen, the servery or the dining area. 
The wash-up has a one-way flow: dirty in, clean out, no crossing. Clean crockery does not return through the dirty side. 
No route from any WC or changing area to the production kitchen without a hand wash basin on it. 
Nothing that has been on the floor is set down on a preparation surface — which means every section has landing space, and the layout shows it. 
P5 will operate a HACCP system in this kitchen for 38 months. HACCP is a management system, but it can only control what the layout permits: a kitchen whose flows cross cannot be managed into compliance and the operator will spend the whole deployment working around it. 
6.3  Stores, and why they are sized for four days 
This site is not served daily. The stores are sized at section 4 for 4 days of chilled stock and 7 days of dry and frozen, at the peak demand. 
That is an Employer decision made on delivery access rather than on kitchen efficiency: the abnormal load route at P2 Appendix 7 is incomplete, winter access to a site of this kind is not guaranteed, and a kitchen that runs on daily delivery stops when the delivery does. A tenderer proposing smaller stores shall price it as a departure and shall state the delivery frequency it has assumed. 
Stock rotation shall be possible without moving stock out of the store: shelving arranged so that the oldest is reachable. A cold room where FIFO requires unloading is a cold room where FIFO does not happen. 
6.4  Finishes, and fixing into a modular building 
FINISHES. Floors: slip resistance PTV ≥ 36 wet, impervious, coved 150 mm to all walls, no open joint at any junction, laid to the falls at 6.11. Walls: impervious, wipe-clean, full height in the production kitchen and wash-up, with no ledge that collects. Ceilings: cleanable, sealed, with no accessible void in a food area. All to withstand daily cleaning with the products in the Operator's approved schedule for 38 months. 
FIXINGS. Every item of equipment is fixed into a MODULAR floor, wall or ceiling built off-site. The Contractor shall submit its fixing schedule against the shell's structure before the freeze date at section 7, stating for every fixed item its weight, its point loads and the fixing it requires. A heavy item fixed to a partition that was not designed for it is a defect in both packages and is discovered when it is loaded. 
No fixing shall penetrate a fire-rated or vapour-sealed element without a detail agreed with P2 before manufacture. 
6.5  The night meal, and cook-chill 
SP5 at section 3 is a hot cooked meal for approximately 40 people at midnight, and it is the service period that a conventional kitchen design does not contain. 
It shall not be produced by a chef standing in a kitchen at midnight if the Contractor's design can avoid it, and it shall not be a vending machine or a heated cabinet. The intended method is cook-chill: produced during SP4's production run, blast chilled at section 5 EQ-17, and regenerated at midnight by one person. 
THE DUTY: regenerate 40 covers to 75 °C core within 20 minutes, by one person, without opening the main kitchen. The Contractor shall size the blast chill and regeneration capacity for it, and shall state in its Proposals the method it intends and the labour it assumes. P5 prices its establishment from that assumption, so an unstated one becomes an argument between two packages. 
6.6  Equipment access — getting it in, and getting it out again 
Every item shall be capable of being brought into its final position, and removed for replacement, through the completed building without dismantling permanent construction. 
The Contractor shall state, for the 3 largest items, the dimensions of the item and the clear opening it requires — not less than 50 mm clearance on the governing dimension and 100 mm on the route — and shall check them against the shell before the freeze date at section 7. A combi oven that does not fit through the door is discovered on the day it is delivered, and by then the door was manufactured four hundred miles away. 
The same applies at the end of the term. The village relocates under section 18, and equipment that could only be installed before the building was finished cannot be removed after it. 
6.7  Commissioning, and the test meal 
COMMISSIONING. Every item commissioned by the manufacturer or its approved agent, with the certificate issued to the Contractor and handed on. Temperatures, flow rates, extract volumes, make-up air volumes and the kitchen-to-dining pressure differential at EQ-28 all measured and recorded individually. A commissioning record stating that the installation is satisfactory without individual results will be rejected. 
THE TEST MEAL. Before handover the Contractor shall produce, in the completed kitchen and with the Operator's staff, a full service of not fewer than 124 covers within the SP4 window, served through the servery to the queueing limit at 6.8, followed by a full wash-up cycle. 
It is the only test that establishes whether this kitchen can do what it was bought to do. Every previous test is of a component. This one is of the kitchen, and it is witnessed by the Employer and by P5. 
A failure requires the cause to be identified and the test repeated in full. A partial retest of the item that failed does not discharge this clause, because the failures worth finding are the ones that only appear under load. 
6.8  The servery and the queue 
Two independent lines at section 5 EQ-18, each capable of 200 covers per hour, plus a separate packed-meal point at EQ-19. 
QUEUEING SHALL NOT EXCEED 10 MINUTES from joining to being served, at the 90th percentile, at any service period. It is measured at the test meal at 6.7 and thereafter by the Operator under its own performance regime. 
The queue shall not form through a fire escape route or across the dining area, and the layout shall show where it forms at 124 covers. A servery that works at 40 covers and blocks the room at 124 has been designed against the wrong number. 
6.9  Dining 
100 seats at section 4, against a peak concurrent figure of 50. The Employer has provided double deliberately: residents on a rotation eat together, and a room sized exactly to its arithmetic is a room in which a crew of six cannot sit down. 
Seating and tables cleanable, movable for cleaning, and durable for 38 months of continuous use. Dining furniture is IN THIS PACKAGE and not in P4 Furniture — see section 8, where the boundary is stated because it is the one that is routinely priced twice or not at all. 
A quiet area or a screen shall be provided such that a resident can eat alone without sitting in the middle of the room. It costs nothing at design stage and it matters to somebody eating their four hundredth meal here. 
6.10  Electrical 
A DEDICATED SUPPLY. Connected load not exceeding 320 kW and maximum demand after diversity not exceeding 200 kVA, taken from the point provided under P1 and P2 rather than from an accommodation block's supply. 
The Contractor shall submit its load calculation with its tender, with diversity stated and justified. Where its equipment selection exceeds the figures above, the reinforcement is its cost and not a variation — the figures are stated at tender for exactly that reason. 
Installation to BS 7671. RCBO protection to every final circuit. Equipment on the emergency isolation at EQ-38. Separate final circuits for refrigeration so that a cooking-circuit fault does not defrost four days of stock. 
REFRIGERATION SHALL BE ON THE STANDBY SUPPLY where one is available under P2 Appendix 8, and the Contractor shall state the load it requires supported. 
6.11  Drainage, and the grease that closes kitchens 
FLOOR DRAINAGE. Gullies to every wet area, falls between 1:60 and 1:80, gratings removable by hand, and rodding access reachable without lifting equipment. No gully sited where a person stands to work. 
GREASE. A separator sized to BS EN 1825-2 for the calculated peak flow, minimum nominal size NS 4, taking every discharge that carries fat — which includes the pot wash and the combi drains, not only the sinks. 
IT SHALL BE SITED SO THAT IT CAN BE EMPTIED WITHOUT ENTERING THE KITCHEN. A separator that can only be reached through the production area is emptied during a service period, or it is not emptied. This is the commonest cause of a kitchen closing unexpectedly and it is designed in or out at layout stage. 
The Contractor shall state the emptying frequency its sizing assumes, and shall hand that figure to P5 at section 10, because P5 pays for it. 
CONNECTION. One connection to the P1 drainage at the invert on the coordination drawing. The invert is a freeze item at section 7: a falls-to-gully design cannot be adjusted after the floor is manufactured. 
6.12  Extract, fire, and the failure mode 
THIS IS THE CLAUSE THAT THE P2 GAP REGISTER WOULD CALL FATAL IF IT WERE MISSING, and its equivalent was missing from Revision A of that document. 
FUEL. This kitchen is ELECTRIC. Gas is not used. That decision removes the gas interlock, the gas detection, the emergency isolation valve and the ventilation-proving requirements of BS 6173 from a building where people sleep 40 metres away, and the Employer has taken it deliberately. A tenderer proposing gas shall price it as a departure and shall price the interlock, the detection, the proving and the fire strategy consequence with it. 
SUPPRESSION. Wet chemical suppression to every cooking appliance under a canopy, to LPS 1223 or equivalent, covering the appliance, the canopy plenum and the first section of duct. On discharge it shall isolate power to every appliance it protects. Serviced at the manufacturer's interval, and the service passes to P5 at section 10. 
THE FAILURE MODE ON FIRE ALARM. The kitchen extract SHALL SHUT DOWN on fire alarm activation, and cooking power shall isolate with it, unless the fire strategy at P2 Appendix 2 requires otherwise IN WRITING. 
The reason is stated so it is not reopened: a running extract feeds a fire and moves smoke through a duct that runs above a building where people are asleep. Where a fire engineer requires the extract to continue for smoke control, that is a decision for the fire strategy and it shall be recorded there, not assumed here. 
NO FIRE DAMPER SHALL BE INSTALLED IN A GREASE EXTRACT DUCT. A damper in a grease duct clogs, fails to close when it matters, and cannot be inspected. Fire separation of the duct shall be achieved by construction — a fire-rated enclosure or a rated duct — providing not less than 60 minutes integrity and insulation where it passes through or over any compartment other than the kitchen, tested to BS EN 1366-1 and BS EN 1366-8 as applicable. The Contractor shall state which method it has used and to what standard. 
The interface with the P2 fire alarm cause and effect matrix is at section 8 IF-05 and shall be demonstrated at commissioning, not described. 
6.13  Ductwork, and being able to clean it 
Ductwork to DW/172. Access panels at every change of direction and at intervals not exceeding 3 m, sized to permit cleaning, and REACHABLE WITHOUT DISMANTLING EQUIPMENT OR ERECTING A SCAFFOLD. 
A grease duct that cannot be cleaned is a fire that has not happened yet, and the insurer will say so. The Contractor shall submit a duct access drawing showing every panel and the means of reaching it, and shall demonstrate access at commissioning. 
The duct shall be laid to falls back to the canopy so that condensed grease drains to a collection point rather than pooling in a horizontal run. 
The termination shall discharge clear of every fresh-air intake, every openable window and every accommodation block, at the separation the P2 fire strategy and the planning consent require. The Contractor shall confirm the position against both before the freeze date. 
6.14  Refrigeration, and the refrigerant somebody else has to service 
Refrigerant with a global warming potential not exceeding 150 for all new equipment, and compliant with the F-gas Regulations for the life of the installation. 
It is prescribed because the consequence lands on somebody else. P5 services this equipment for 38 months and pays for the refrigerant when it is topped up; a high-GWP refrigerant subject to a phase-down is a servicing cost and a supply risk transferred to the operator by a decision taken here to save capital. 
Where a natural refrigerant is used the Contractor shall state the safety requirements it brings — ventilation, detection, charge limits — and shall show them on its layout. 
Leak detection to every walk-in system, alarmed locally and to the Operator. Records kept from commissioning and handed over.

7  The shell freeze 
7.1  What a freeze date is, and why it is absolute here 
This kitchen is fitted into a building manufactured in a factory. Once a module is manufactured, an interface dimension cannot be changed: it can only be cut, patched or worked around, on a completed module, at a cost that bears no relation to what the change would have cost a week earlier. 
THE FREEZE DATE IS EIGHT WEEKS BEFORE P2 COMMENCES MANUFACTURE OF THE KITCHEN AND DINING BUILDING. Every item in the register at 7.2 is fixed by then, in writing, signed by this Contractor and by P2. 
Eight weeks is not a comfort margin. It is the period P2 needs to incorporate a penetration schedule into a manufacturing drawing, order the components and have them at the line — and it is stated as a date rather than as a principle because a principle slips and a date does not. 
7.2  The freeze register 
Every item below is fixed at the freeze date. The Contractor produces the information; P2 confirms it can build to it; the Employer records the agreement. An item not fixed by the date is escalated the same day, not the following month. 
7.3  The loading the P2 appendix does not currently carry 
The Employer states this against itself rather than letting a tenderer find it. 
Appendix 8 of the P2 Employer's Requirements gives an imposed floor load of 3.0 kN/m² for 'amenity and dining'. IT STATES NO LOADING FOR A KITCHEN PRODUCTION FLOOR, and a kitchen production floor is not an amenity floor: a walk-in cold room, a bank of combi ovens on a stand and a full stock room impose more, and they impose it as concentrated point loads rather than as a uniform area load. 
THIS PACKAGE REQUIRES, and P2 shall confirm it can provide: 
5.0 kN/m² uniformly distributed across the production kitchen and wash-up. 
7.5 kN/m² beneath the walk-in chill and freezer rooms, and beneath any equipment the Contractor's schedule identifies as exceeding it. 
Point loads as stated on the Contractor's fixing schedule at 6.4, item by item. 
3.0 kN/m² across dining, as the appendix already provides. 
The Contractor shall submit its loading schedule not later than TWO WEEKS BEFORE the freeze date, so that there is time for P2 to confirm or to object. P2 Appendix 8 is to be updated to carry these figures before manufacture; until it is, this clause is the requirement and it prevails. 
A tenderer that reads Appendix 8, prices a 3.0 kN/m² floor and discovers a cold room on it has been failed by the Employer's own documents. Which is why it is written here. 
7.4  After the freeze date 
A change to any item in the register after the freeze date is a variation, valued under section 14, and it carries THE FULL COST OF THE CHANGE TO P2 — abortive manufacturing drawings, re-ordered components, a delayed manufacturing slot, or a modification to a completed module — as well as the cost to this package. 
Where the change arises from this Contractor's own development of its design, that cost is this Contractor's. Where it arises from an Employer instruction, it is the Employer's, and the Employer would rather know the number before it instructs. 
The Contractor shall therefore treat the eight weeks before the freeze date as the period in which its design is actually settled, and shall not rely on developing it during manufacture. A fit-out contractor is accustomed to a shell it can drill. This one is not.

11  Programme and delay 
11.1  The date that governs this package 
The kitchen shell is handed to this package at the Section 1 sectional completion, at the end of month 3 of the P2 programme, and the first meal is served on the day the first 65 residents arrive. 
THAT DATE IS THE FIXED POINT. Sixty-five people arriving to a village with no kitchen are sixty-five people the Employer accommodates and feeds commercially, at the rate at 11.3, until there is one. 
Working backwards from it: the test meal at 6.7 not less than 2 weeks before; commissioning complete not less than 3 weeks before; handover to P5 and training not less than 4 weeks before, per IF-08. The Contractor's programme shall show each of them and shall show the float it holds. 
11.2  And the date that governs everything else 
The freeze date at section 7 — eight weeks before P2 commences manufacture — falls MONTHS before this Contractor is on site. It is the earlier of the two dates and it is the one a fit-out contractor is least accustomed to. 
The Contractor's design is settled at the freeze date, not at mobilisation. Its tender programme shall show the design period leading to it and shall name the person responsible for the information P2 needs. 
11.3  Delay 
Liquidated damages of £145 per bed per calendar day apply where the first meal service is not available on the date the first section is occupied, calculated on the beds occupied and unfed, and capped in aggregate at 7.5% of the contract sum. 
The rate is the same as the P2 sectional rate and is built up the same way — substitute accommodation, subsistence, travel and administration — because the loss is the same loss: a resident who cannot be fed here is accommodated elsewhere. The build-up is at clause 18.2 of the P2 requirements and it is not restated so that there is one version of it. 
Where the first meal service is unavailable for a cause within P2 — a shell handed over late or not to the freeze register — the damages do not fall on this Contractor, and the Contractor's duty is to have given the early warning that proves it. 
 
12  Health, safety and working next to an occupied village 
12.1  CDM and the site 
The Employer has appointed a Principal Designer and Principal Contractor for the project. This Contractor is a contractor under CDM 2015 and complies with the construction phase plan. Nothing in this document appoints it to either statutory role. 
The Employer's site rules apply, including induction, competence cards, drug and alcohol testing, PPE and permits. Installation takes place alongside a live construction site and, for the later sections, alongside an OCCUPIED VILLAGE. 
12.2  Working next to people who are asleep 
From the Section 1 sectional completion onward there are residents 40 metres away, and a proportion of them are asleep at every hour. 
The Contractor shall plan noisy work — core drilling, fixing, plant commissioning, extract balancing — by zone and shift under the same discipline P5 applies at its own clause on quiet hours, and shall agree its noisy-work windows with the Employer weekly rather than starting at eight and finding out. 
Commissioning the extract at full volume at two in the afternoon wakes the night shift. It is not a courtesy point: they work a 12-hour shift that evening. 
12.3  Hot works, and the specific risk here 
Hot works permit required, issued by the Principal Contractor, with a fire watch during and for not less than 60 minutes after. 
The specific risk in this package is hot works in a building with a grease extract system partly installed and a suppression system not yet commissioned. The Contractor shall not carry out hot works within a canopy or duct once grease-bearing surfaces exist, and shall state in its method statement the point in its sequence after which that prohibition applies. 
12.4  Manual handling and lifting into a finished building 
Equipment is lifted into a completed building through a door. The Contractor shall produce a lifting and handling plan for every item exceeding 100 kg, covering the route, the equipment used, the floor protection and the floor loading during the move — which is a point load on a manufactured floor and is not the same as the loading in service.

15  Defects, response and support 
15.1  Defects liability 
12 months from the date of certified handover of the kitchen. Where a defect is rectified, the period for that item begins again from rectification. Where the same defect occurs in more than one item of the same type, it is systemic: the Contractor investigates the cause and rectifies every instance including those not yet reported. 
Equipment warranties run from commissioning and are assigned to the Employer, with their start dates recorded in the asset register. 
15.2  Response, in an operating kitchen 
PRIORITY 1 — anything that stops a service period: the primary cooking capacity, the servery, the dish wash, a walk-in cold room, the extract, the suppression system, the hot water, or a drainage blockage. ATTEND WITHIN 4 HOURS. Make safe or provide a working alternative within 8. Rectify within 5 working days. 
PRIORITY 2 — a single item out of service where the period can still run: rectify within 5 working days. 
PRIORITY 3 — cosmetic and non-operational: 20 working days, and may be batched. 
THE TIMES ARE DELIBERATELY THE SAME AS THE P2 DEFECTS REGIME and are longer than P5's reactive times, because P5's maintenance contractor is on site and this Contractor is travelling. Where an item falls to both, P5 attends first to its own times and this Contractor rectifies to these — the Operator sees the faster of the two. 
ACCESS. This kitchen serves 5 periods a day. The Contractor shall work around service, or out of hours, and shall price for it. A rectification that requires the kitchen for a day requires the Employer's agreement and a plan for feeding 225 people that day. 
15.3  Obsolescence and support 
For every item the Contractor shall state at tender: the manufacturer's committed spares availability period from handover; the notice given of end-of-life; and whether the control system is open or proprietary. 
Spares and support shall be available for not less than the 38-month deployment plus 24 months. Where a manufacturer will not commit, the Contractor shall say so at tender rather than after. 
No item shall be offered whose function depends on a subscription, licence or cloud service that, if not renewed, disables it or its monitoring. Where a subscription is required for anything else, its cost for the deployment plus 24 months is stated and included.

Equipment shall be selected on whole-life cost and not on capital cost alone. Where a higher-capital item has a lower predicted consumption, the Contractor shall offer it and show the comparison over 38 months — the Employer will take the better trade, but only if it is shown the arithmetic.

Prescriptive and performance specification mixed
