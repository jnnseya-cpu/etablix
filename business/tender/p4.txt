1  Introduction, and what this package really is 
1.1  The smallest package on this project, and the one most likely to embarrass it 
This is the cheapest of the five packages and it is the last to arrive. It is also the only one that every resident touches, every day, for up to thirty-eight months. 
THE DEFINING FACT. This package has almost no work of its own. Its entire risk lives at its boundary with P2 — where fitted joinery stops and loose furniture starts. P2 builds a wardrobe, a desk and a shelf into the module in a factory four hundred miles away. This package brings the bed, the chair and everything else. If those two lists do not agree, the failure is discovered when a bed is carried into a room that has no space for it, and by then the module has been manufactured. section 4 exists for that and it is the most important section in this document. 
THE SECOND FACT. Furniture in sleeping accommodation is not furniture. It is fire load, in a room where somebody is asleep. 6.3 states the standards and they are not negotiable by price. 
THE THIRD FACT. Twelve of these rooms are accessible rooms. An accessible room is designed as an empty box and it is THIS package that decides whether it is still accessible once there is a bed in it. 6.6. 
1.2  Output specification, and where this document departs from it 
The default is an OUTPUT specification. The Employer states the duty — a dimension, a durability class, a fire standard, a clearance — and the Contractor selects, supplies, assembles and warrants the product that meets it. 
Every line of the furniture schedule at section 5 carries a code. 
E — EMPLOYER-PRESCRIBED. The Employer has specified the thing. The Contractor provides it as described and the Employer carries the risk that what is described achieves the duty. 
C — CONTRACTOR-DESIGNED. The Employer has stated a duty. The Contractor selects the product, sizes it, and warrants that it performs. 
The prescribed lines here are of three kinds and no others: a DIMENSION that has to agree with fitted joinery P2 has already built, a LIFE-SAFETY OR ACCESSIBILITY requirement, and a HYGIENE requirement that follows from the same bed being used by a succession of people. Everything else is a duty, because the Employer has no business selecting a chair and then owning the fact that it is uncomfortable. 
NO PRODUCT IS NAMED IN THIS DOCUMENT. Where a figure looks like a product it is a dimension or a class, and any product meeting it is acceptable. 
1.3  What is NOT in this package 
FITTED JOINERY IS P2. The wardrobe, the desk worktop and the shelving over it are built into the module. They are specified at the P2 room data sheets and they are not reissued here — 4.2 states how the two lists are reconciled. 
DINING FURNITURE IS P3. The tables and chairs in the dining room are in the kitchen and catering package, because they are sized from the covers and the servery throughput rather than from the room. The boundary is at the dining room door and it is scheduled at IF-06. 
BED LINEN, TOWELS AND THEIR LAUNDERING ARE P5. This package supplies the durable items — the mattress, the protector, the pillow and the duvet. The sheets, pillowcases, duvet covers and towels are consumable stock supplied and laundered by the Operator under its linen service. 6.4 and IF-09. 
LAUNDRY AND DRYING MACHINES ARE P5, sized and sited by the Operator against its own waiting-time requirement. This package furnishes the laundry room around them — IF-10. 
ANY FIXING INTO THE MODULE STRUCTURE IS P2's TO AGREE. See 6.7. This package does not put a screw into a wall, a ceiling or a fire-rated element without a detail P2 has accepted.

4  The boundary with P2, and the Common Schedule 
4.1  Why this is the first section a tenderer should read 
This package has almost no independent work. Every serious risk in it is at the line where P2's fitted joinery stops and this package's loose furniture starts. 
THE FAILURE THIS SECTION PREVENTS. P2 manufactures a bedroom in a factory, complete with a fitted wardrobe, a fitted desk worktop and fitted shelving. Months later this package arrives with a bed, a chair, a bedside unit and a bin. If the two lists were never reconciled, one of three things happens, and all three are discovered with 114 modules already built: 
AN ITEM IS SUPPLIED TWICE. Two parties both priced a bedside unit, and one of them is a credit nobody wants to give. 
AN ITEM IS SUPPLIED BY NOBODY. Each party read the other's document and assumed. The first resident moves into a room with no chair. 
AN ITEM DOES NOT FIT. The wardrobe door opens into the space the bed occupies, or the desk chair will not go under the fitted worktop, and neither is anybody's fault under either contract. 
The third is the expensive one, because the module is already built and the only remedy left is different furniture. 
4.2  The Common Schedule 
THE EMPLOYER SHALL ISSUE A SINGLE COMMON SCHEDULE, listing every item in a bedroom and allocating each to P2 or to P4, and it shall be issued and signed by both parties BEFORE EITHER PACKAGE IS LET. It is scheduled at P2 IF-05 and at IF-01. 
The schedule below is the Employer's starting position and is the basis on which this package is priced. It is not a summary of another document: where it and the P2 room data sheets differ, the difference is resolved before either party orders anything, and this clause prevails until they are reconciled. 
EVERY LINE STATES FOUR THINGS: the item, what P2 provides fitted, what this package provides loose, and what goes wrong if the line is missed. The fourth column is there because a boundary schedule that lists only the split gets skimmed, and the point of the exercise is to make somebody think about each line. 
EACH PARTY CONFIRMS IN WRITING, against the schedule, before it orders: that it has priced every line allocated to it, and that it has priced none of the lines allocated to the other. A tender that does not include that confirmation is incomplete. 
4.3  Dimensional coordination, which is the half that is not about who supplies what 
Agreeing WHO supplies the bed does not prevent the bed from being the wrong size for the room P2 built. 
BEFORE THIS CONTRACTOR ORDERS ANYTHING it shall obtain from P2, and shall confirm its layout against: the internal room dimensions as manufactured; the position, depth and door swing of the fitted wardrobe; the position, height, depth and knee clearance of the fitted desk worktop; the height and projection of the fitted shelving; the position of every socket, data outlet, switch, radiator and reading light; and the door swing and clear opening. IF-02 and IF-05. 
The bed at F-01 is prescribed at 900 × 2000 mm for exactly this reason. It is the one dimension P2 has already designed the room around and it is not the Contractor's to change. 
THE CHAIR UNDER THE DESK is the coordination check that is most often missed. P2 states a fitted worktop at 730 mm above finished floor level with a stated knee clearance. The task chair at F-07 shall fit under it, at its lowest setting, with its arms if it has them, and the Contractor shall demonstrate it in the sample room at 6.13 rather than assert it from a dimension sheet. 
4.4  Change after the freeze, and who pays for it 
Once P2 has commenced manufacture, the Common Schedule is fixed. A change to it after that point is a change to two packages and it is instructed by the Employer under section 13, never agreed between contractors. 
WHERE THE CHANGE ARISES FROM AN ERROR IN THE SCHEDULE ITSELF — an item allocated to nobody, or allocated to both — the Employer carries it, because the Employer issued the schedule. 
WHERE IT ARISES FROM A PARTY FAILING TO CONFIRM under 4.2, or from a party ordering against a dimension it did not verify under 4.3, it is that party's. 
That allocation is stated now, in advance, because after the event both positions are arguable and neither is provable.

6  Specification, element by element 
6.1  Design responsibility, and the Contractor's Proposals 
This is a contractor-selected package. The Employer has stated the duties: the dimensions that have to agree with P2's fitted joinery, the fire standards at 6.3, the accessible clearances at 6.6, the hygiene requirements at 6.5 and the durability classes at 6.2. The products that meet them are the Contractor's selection and the Contractor warrants that they do. 
WITH ITS TENDER the Contractor shall submit Contractor's Proposals containing, as a minimum: a schedule against every line of section 5 naming the product offered, its dimensions, its mass and its test evidence; a furniture layout at 1:50 for RT-01, RT-02 and RT-03; the RT-02 layouts demonstrating the clearances at 6.6; the access statement at 6.8 for the three largest items; the cleaning products compatibility statement at 6.11; and its delivery programme against the four sectional dates at section 10. 
EVERY DEPARTURE from this document shall be listed in a Schedule of Departures with the clause, what is offered instead, the reason, the effect on every other requirement, and the price effect as a plus or a minus. A departure not listed is not accepted, whether or not it is visible in a photograph. 
TEST EVIDENCE IS SUBMITTED, NOT PROMISED. A catalogue statement that an item is 'contract grade' or 'fire retardant' is not evidence. The test certificate, the standard, the level and the date are. 
Review, comment or acceptance by the Employer does not transfer responsibility for the selection. 
6.2  Contract furniture, and why domestic furniture bought in quantity is not it 
Everything in this package is used by a succession of strangers, at every hour, for thirty-eight months, and is cleaned to a commercial regime throughout. Domestic furniture bought in bulk fails inside a year and it fails in a way that is visible to every resident. 
MINIMUM PERFORMANCE, by item type, with the test report submitted for each: 
Seating, non-domestic — BS EN 16139 severity level L2 (severe), with BS EN 1728 for the seating strength and durability. 
Office task chairs — BS EN 1335-1, -2 and -3, type B. 
Tables and desks — BS EN 15372 level 5 (severe contract use). Office desking to BS EN 527-1, -2 and -3. 
Storage furniture — BS EN 16121 and BS EN 16122, level 5 where the item is in a resident area. 
Beds and mattresses — BS EN 1725 for the strength, stability and durability of the frame. 
Outdoor furniture — BS EN 581-1 and -2, contract use. 
Where an item is not covered by any of those standards the Contractor shall state the standard it has tested to and why it applies. 'No applicable standard' is not an answer; it is a reason to ask what the item is made of. 
WARRANTY. Not less than 5 years on every frame and structural component and 2 years on every moving component and upholstery, running from the sectional handover at which the item is installed and not from the date of order. 
6.3  Fire performance, which is the reason this document exists 
FURNITURE IN SLEEPING ACCOMMODATION IS NOT FURNITURE. It is the largest single item of combustible material in a room where somebody is asleep, and it is the difference between a fire that is contained and one that is not. 
MATTRESSES, DIVANS AND BED BASES — BS 7177, MEDIUM HAZARD as a minimum, permanently and durably labelled with the standard and the hazard level on the item itself. 
UPHOLSTERED SEATING, INCLUDING EVERY CHAIR, SOFA AND UPHOLSTERED HEADBOARD — BS 7176, MEDIUM HAZARD as a minimum, permanently and durably labelled. 
Those are the levels for hotel and hostel sleeping accommodation and they are the floor, not the target. Domestic furniture complying only with the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988 does NOT satisfy this clause, and a supplier offering it has misread the building. 
CURTAINS, BLINDS AND ANY FABRIC HUNG IN AN ESCAPE ROUTE — BS 5867-2 type B, where any is provided under this package. 
THE LABEL IS PART OF THE SPECIFICATION. An unlabelled item is a non-compliant item, whatever its paperwork says, because in three years the paperwork will be in a file and the label will be on the chair. Labels shall survive the cleaning regime at 6.11 for the whole deployment. 
FIRE LOAD IN THE AMENITY BUILDING. The quantity of upholstered seating at F-21 is a fire load in a room with a stated fire strategy. The Contractor shall issue its seating schedule to the Employer's fire engineer for confirmation against that strategy before it orders, per IF-12. Adding seating to a lounge after the strategy was written is how a compartment stops working. 
COMPLIANCE WITH STATUTE. The Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 applies to these premises and the responsible person's duty is not discharged by this clause. Nothing here reduces it. 
6.4  Linen, and exactly where this package stops 
The division is between DURABLE and CONSUMABLE, and it is stated here because it is the boundary that gets discovered on the first day of occupation when 65 beds have no sheets. 
THIS PACKAGE SUPPLIES, once, as durable stock: the mattress, the encasement at 6.5 plus one spare per bed, the pillows and their protectors, and the duvet. 
P5 SUPPLIES AND LAUNDERS, as consumable stock throughout the deployment: sheets, pillowcases, duvet covers, towels and bath mats, under its linen service and to its own cycle. 
THE CONTRACTOR SHALL STATE the mattress, pillow and duvet dimensions in its Contractor's Proposals, and the Employer shall issue them to P5 before P5 procures a single item of linen. A 200 mm deep mattress and a fitted sheet cut for 250 mm is 225 beds of linen that does not stay on. IF-09. 
REPLACEMENT of a durable item during the deployment is dealt with at 6.12, not by P5 buying a different mattress. 
6.5  The mattress, and the thing nobody writes into a furniture specification 
A bed in workforce accommodation is not slept in by one person. Over thirty-eight months it is used by a succession of people, some of whom arrive tired from another site, and the two things that damage it are fluids and infestation. Neither is in a furniture catalogue and both are certain. 
THE ENCASEMENT. Every mattress shall be fully enclosed in a zipped, waterproof, vapour-permeable, wipe-cleanable encasement, launderable at not less than 60 °C, with one fitted and one spare per bed delivered at handover. A fitted sheet laid over a mattress is not a protector: it protects the top surface of a mattress from the one direction that matters least. 
The encasement is changed and laundered between residents by P5, and the mattress beneath it is inspected at the same time. That is possible only because it unzips, which is why the requirement is a zipped encasement and not a stretch cover. 
THE FRAME, and why it is prescribed at F-01. Metal or solid-section, with no open tube end, no upholstered base and no fabric-covered headboard, and not less than 150 mm of clear open space beneath the whole footprint. 
Every one of those is an infestation requirement. Bed bugs live in the seams, the voids and the open ends of a bed frame, they arrive in somebody's bag through no fault of anybody's, and in shared accommodation they are a matter of when. A frame that can be visually inspected in two minutes and treated in ten is the difference between one room out of service for a day and a block out of service for a fortnight. A hollow tube with an open end cannot be inspected at all. 
The Contractor shall state, in its Proposals, how the bed it offers is inspected and treated, and P5 shall confirm that the answer is workable before the order is placed. Neither party should meet this problem for the first time in month nine. 
CONDEMNING A MATTRESS. The Contractor shall state the criteria at which a mattress is withdrawn — fluid penetration through the encasement, structural failure, or an infestation treatment that cannot be verified as successful — and the replacement route at 6.12 shall cover them. 
6.6  The accessible room, and the furniture that decides whether it still is one 
THIS IS THE CLAUSE THAT CATCHES THE FAULT NOBODY LOOKS FOR. An accessible room is designed, approved and signed off as an empty box. It becomes accessible or not on the day this package puts a bed in it, and by then everybody who checked it has gone. 
IN EACH OF THE 12 RT-02 ROOMS, THE FOLLOWING SHALL BE ACHIEVED WITH ALL FURNITURE IN ITS FINAL POSITION and shall be demonstrated room by room on the layout at F-14, not asserted once for a room type: 
A clear turning space of not less than 1500 mm diameter, clear of every item of furniture, the door swing and the wheelchair user's own footplates. 
A clear transfer space of not less than 1000 mm to at least one side of the bed, at full length. 
Top of the mattress between 450 and 500 mm above finished floor level, and the Contractor shall state the compressed height and not the label height. 
Not less than 150 mm clear height beneath the entire bed frame footprint, unobstructed, for a mobile hoist. 
Every item of storage, every surface and every control that a resident uses within the reach ranges of BS 8300-2, from a seated position. 
The chair at F-13 with arms, stable under a transfer load applied at its front edge, and not on castors. 
No item positioned so that it reduces the clear width of the door approach or the route to the ensuite below 900 mm. 
VERIFICATION. Each of the 12 rooms is measured with the furniture in place and the record issued, before that room is accepted at 6.13. A drawing showing compliance is not the verification; the measurement in the built room is. 
AND IF IT CANNOT BE ACHIEVED, SAY SO IN THE TENDER. If the room as built will not take a compliant furniture layout, that is a finding about the Employer's room and the Employer would rather have it now, while P2 can still change something, than after twelve modules have been manufactured. 
6.7  Fixings, and the rule that this package does not put a screw in a wall 
Every wall, floor and ceiling in this village was manufactured in a factory four hundred miles away, and some of them are fire-rated, vapour-sealed or acoustically rated elements whose performance was tested before this package existed. 
NO ITEM SUPPLIED UNDER THIS PACKAGE SHALL BE FIXED TO THE MODULE STRUCTURE, TO ANY PARTITION, TO A CEILING OR TO A FIRE-RATED OR VAPOUR-SEALED ELEMENT except in accordance with a fixing detail P2 has accepted in writing. 
The Contractor shall submit a fixing schedule listing every item it intends to fix, its mass, its point loads, the position, and the fixing it proposes, and shall submit it in time for P2 to confirm or object before manufacture where the fixing requires a pattress or a reinforced zone — IF-04. 
A television bracket screwed into a partition that was not designed for it is a defect in two packages and it is discovered when the television comes off the wall. 
WHERE A FIXING CANNOT BE AGREED the Contractor shall offer a free-standing alternative rather than fix anyway. Every item in the schedule at section 5 is capable of being provided free-standing except where the Employer has prescribed otherwise, and that is deliberate. 
6.8  Getting it into the room, and out again in thirty-eight months 
Furniture is installed AFTER the modules are set and BEFORE handover, per IF-03. There is no stage at which a wall is open. 
EVERY ITEM SHALL THEREFORE be capable of being brought to its final position, and removed for replacement, through the completed building — either assembled, or in components assembled in the room without specialist tools. 
The Contractor shall obtain from P2 the clear opening widths and heights of every door, corridor, stair and turn on the route to each room and to each amenity space, and shall check its three largest items against them, allowing not less than 50 mm clearance on the governing dimension and 100 mm along the route. For tender purposes it shall assume a bedroom door clear opening of 800 mm and shall confirm the actual figure before it orders — IF-05. 
A 900 mm bed does not pass through an 800 mm door assembled. That is not a problem; it is a reason the frame at F-01 is required to assemble and dismantle in the room. It becomes a problem only if nobody checks until the lorry arrives. 
THE STAIR. Blocks are two storey and there is no lift. Every item destined for a first-floor room shall pass the stair and its turn, in the components in which it will be carried, and the Contractor shall state that it has checked it. 
AT THE END OF THE TERM the same applies in reverse. Under section 17 this furniture is removed from occupied rooms in a live village; an item that could only be installed before the corridor was finished cannot be taken out after it. 
6.9  Weight, and the person who has to move it 
P5 moves this furniture for thirty-eight months: room turnarounds, deep cleans, redecoration, replacements and the pest control inspections at 6.5. 
NO SINGLE ITEM SHALL EXCEED 23 kg in the components in which it is routinely moved. Where an assembled item exceeds it, the item shall either separate into components below it or shall carry a permanent, legible two-person handling mark stating its mass. 
Every item in a bedroom shall be movable clear of the wall and the bed shall be movable clear of every wall, by one person, for cleaning and inspection. A bed that takes two people to move is a bed that is not moved, and what is under it is not cleaned. 
The Contractor shall state the mass of every scheduled item in its Contractor's Proposals. That figure is used by P5 to write its method statements and by the Employer to check this clause, and an item delivered heavier than stated is a departure. 
6.10  Stability, and the failures that injure somebody 
Every item of storage furniture shall be stable to BS EN 16122 with the heaviest drawer or door fully extended and loaded to its rated capacity, without anchoring to a wall. Where an item cannot achieve that it shall not be supplied, because the anchoring the standard would otherwise require conflicts with 6.7. 
Every chair shall be stable to BS EN 1728 under a load applied at its front edge, at its side, and to its back at full recline where it reclines. 
Every table shall be stable under an off-centre load at the point where somebody will lean on it, which is the corner nearest the door. 
No glass in any resident area except where it is safety glass to BS EN 12600 class 2 or better, and no glass table tops at all. 
TWO STANDARDS FOR THE SAME THING, AND THE EMPLOYER STATES IT AGAINST ITSELF. The P2 room data sheets specify window glazing as toughened or laminated to BS 6206 Class A. BS 6206 WAS WITHDRAWN and is superseded by BS EN 12600, in which the classes run 1, 2 and 3 by drop height rather than A, B and C. Class A under the old standard corresponds to Class 1 under the current one, and Class B to Class 2. 
This package specifies BS EN 12600 because it is the standard in force. The two documents are to be read as one requirement and P2 Appendix 8 and the room data sheets are to be corrected to the current standard. A tenderer offering glass certified only to a withdrawn standard has not met this clause, and a tenderer that noticed the conflict and priced around it silently has not either — it is listed as a departure under 6.1. 
SHELF AND RACK CAPACITIES shall be marked on the bay, permanently, per F-34. An unmarked capacity is an overloaded shelf within six months and there is nobody left who knows what it was rated at. 
6.11  Cleaning, and surviving the regime it will actually get 
P5 cleans these rooms to a stated standard, daily in circulation and at every turnaround in a bedroom, with a defined chemical schedule, for thirty-eight months. 
Every surface supplied under this package shall withstand daily cleaning with the products in the Operator's approved schedule, without discolouring, crazing, delaminating, softening or losing its label, for the whole deployment. 
THE CONTRACTOR SHALL OBTAIN P5's CHEMICAL SCHEDULE and shall submit a written compatibility statement against it, product by product, with its Contractor's Proposals. Where P5 is not yet appointed the Employer will issue an indicative schedule; the Contractor shall confirm against the final one before it orders. 
No unsealed timber, no untreated fabric, no crevice that cannot be reached, and no upholstery seam in a horizontal surface where liquid will sit. 
UPHOLSTERY COVERS in resident areas shall be removable and washable at 60 °C, or shall be an impervious wipe-cleanable material. A lounge chair with a fixed woven cover is a chair that is replaced in year two. 
6.12  Attrition, model continuity, and what happens in month nineteen 
Furniture in a village of 225 people breaks. This clause is written so that a broken chair is replaced in a day rather than becoming the fourteenth different chair in the lounge. 
ATTRITION STOCK. Not less than 3% of every bedroom line and 5% of every seating line, delivered at the FIRST sectional handover and held in the amenity store at R-10, with an inventory issued to and acknowledged by P5 under IF-11. Stock delivered at the end of the contract has arrived after the breakages. 
MODEL CONTINUITY. The Contractor shall guarantee availability of each supplied model, or a demonstrably compatible successor, for the deployment period of 38 months plus 12 months. Where a model is discontinued it shall give not less than 6 months' written notice and shall offer a compatible alternative, and 'compatible' means it matches on dimension, on fire classification, on finish and on the fixings it uses. 
THE REPLACEMENT ROUTE. The Contractor shall state its lead time for a replacement item ordered by P5 during the deployment. It shall not exceed 4 weeks for any bedroom line. Where it does, the attrition percentage for that line shall be increased and priced accordingly. 
NONE OF THIS IS A LUXURY. A village where the lounge has four kinds of chair and two rooms have no desk chair at all is a village that looks neglected, and it looks that way to the people who live in it every day. 
6.13  The sample room, and how a room is accepted 
THE SAMPLE ROOMS. Before any bulk delivery the Contractor shall fully furnish one room of each type — RT-01, RT-02 and RT-03 — on site, in a completed module, and the Employer and P5 shall inspect and approve them jointly. 
The RT-02 sample is measured against every clearance at 6.6, in the room, with the furniture in place. 
Approval of the sample rooms is a condition precedent to the first bulk delivery. A photograph of a showroom is not a sample room: the point of the exercise is the furniture in the actual room, at the actual dimensions, and it is the only test that finds the item that does not fit. 
ROOM ACCEPTANCE. A room is accepted when it has passed a room acceptance check against a checklist agreed with P5 before the first delivery, covering: every scheduled item present; every item assembled, stable and undamaged; the encasement fitted and the spare present; the fire labels present and legible on every labelled item; nothing left in the room including packaging; and for RT-02, the clearances measured and recorded. 
THE CHECK IS ROOM BY ROOM AND IT IS SIGNED. A sample-based acceptance is not acceptable for 225 rooms, because the failures are individual: one missing bin, one damaged chair, one room where the bed was put on the wrong side. Each of those is invisible in a sample and obvious to the person who moves in. 
HANDOVER OF A SECTION is complete when every room in it has passed and the inventory has been issued to and acknowledged by P5 under IF-08, not when the delivery is complete. 
6.14  Delivery, sequence, and the four dates that matter 
This package delivers against the sectional occupation dates, not against a single completion: 65 beds at month 3, cumulative 150 at month 6, 205 at month 9, 225 at month 12. 
FURNITURE FOR A SECTION SHALL BE INSTALLED AND ACCEPTED BEFORE THAT SECTION IS OCCUPIED. There is no arrangement in which residents move into an unfurnished room and the furniture follows. 
THE INSTALLATION WINDOW is after module setting and building services commissioning, and before handover — IF-03. It is short and it is the last thing in the sequence, which means every delay upstream lands on this package. The Contractor shall state, in its Proposals, the minimum installation window it requires per section and the resource it will apply. 
WHERE THE SEQUENCE SLIPS, P2 provides secure storage under its own requirements and the Contractor shall not deliver furniture into an unsecured or incomplete building. 6.16 states why. 
A delivery accepted into a building that is not ready is a delivery that is stored in a corridor, damaged, and disowned by both parties. 
6.15  Packaging, and 225 rooms of cardboard 
Furnishing 225 bedrooms and 600 m² of amenity space generates a volume of packaging that is a genuine logistics and waste problem on a site with a laydown area of 0.42 ha, and it is a problem this package creates. 
ALL PACKAGING SHALL BE REMOVED FROM SITE BY THE CONTRACTOR, on the day it is generated. No packaging shall be left in a room, a corridor, a stairwell or an escape route at the end of any working day, and none shall be placed in the village waste provision, which is sized for residents and not for this. 
The Contractor shall state, in its Proposals, the packaging it expects to generate by volume and how it will be segregated, removed and recycled, and shall report actuals monthly under section 15. 
The Employer will evaluate a returnable, reusable or blanket-wrap delivery method favourably, because it removes the problem rather than managing it. 
NO PACKAGING IN AN ESCAPE ROUTE, at any time, for any duration. From month 3 there are people asleep in the building next door and the escape route belongs to them. 
6.16  Security, because this is the most stealable package on the project 
Furniture is valuable, portable, unmarked and, unlike a module or a drainage run, it fits in the back of a van. It arrives at the very end of the programme, when site security attention has usually moved on. 
No delivery shall be made to a building that is not lockable and secured. Where the programme requires earlier delivery, the Contractor shall provide secure storage at its own cost and shall not store furniture in an unsecured shell. 
Deliveries shall be sealed and the seal number recorded, checked on arrival, and a broken seal reported within 2 hours and before any item is installed. 
An inventory shall be maintained from the first delivery to the last room acceptance, reconciled weekly against the delivery records, and any discrepancy reported. A shortage found at handover cannot be attributed to anybody; a shortage found in the week it happened can. 
From first delivery until sectional handover the Contractor holds responsibility for the furniture in the building, under the key control regime P2 and P5 operate, and hands it over with the inventory rather than with the last delivery note. 
6.17  Comfort, and the only test that matters 
Everything above this clause can be satisfied by a compliant, durable, fire-rated, cleanable set of furniture that nobody wants to sit on. 
The Employer will evaluate the tender partly on the Contractor's Proposals being sat in. The sample rooms at 6.13 shall be available to a panel including residents' representatives or, before occupation, the Employer's and P5's staff, and their view is recorded. 
AFTER OCCUPATION the furniture is measured by the residents' satisfaction survey P5 operates. Where a scheduled item is the subject of a sustained adverse response — the same item raised by more than 10% of respondents across two consecutive surveys — the Contractor shall attend, investigate and propose a remedy. Where the item is failing to meet a requirement of this document, the remedy is at the Contractor's cost. 
That is not a soft clause. A desk chair that is unusable after two hours is a resident who works sitting on their bed for thirty-eight months, and it is one of the few things in this village that a person notices every single day.

10.1  The four dates, and being last in the sequence 
This package has no completion date of its own that matters. It has four, and they are the sectional occupation dates: 65 beds at month 3, cumulative 150 at month 6, 205 at month 9, and 225 at month 12. 
For each section, this package is complete when every room in that section has passed the room acceptance check at 6.13 and the inventory has been issued to P5. 
BEING LAST IS THE PROGRAMME RISK AND IT IS NOT THIS PACKAGE'S FAULT. Furniture goes in after the modules are set and after services commissioning, and before handover. Every delay in every package upstream arrives here as a compressed window. 
The Contractor shall state, with its tender, the minimum installation window it requires per section and the resource it will apply, and the Employer will hold the programme to it. A window that is compressed below the stated minimum is a change under section 13 and not an expectation.

Three deliverables in this package sit months ahead of any delivery, and they are the ones that matter:

THE SAMPLE ROOMS at 6.13, approved before any bulk delivery.

Monthly progress report stating: orders placed by line and their lead times; the status of the Common Schedule and the dimensional confirmation; the sample room status; rooms accepted to date against the number required for the next sectional date; every open interface at section 7 and its status; and any matter that could affect a sectional date, whether or not it is this Contractor's responsibility. 
LEAD TIMES ARE REPORTED HONESTLY AND EARLY. Contract furniture is manufactured to order and a twelve-week lead time discovered in week ten is a section that is not furnished. The Employer would far rather hear about it in month one. 
10.4  Delay, and what a late section costs 
Delay damages apply to each sectional occupation date separately, at £145 PER BED PER DAY for every bed in that section that cannot be occupied on its date. It is the same rate as the P2 sectional damages because it is the same loss: the Employer houses those people somewhere else, at the cost of a bed elsewhere and a longer journey to work. 
It is stated as a genuine pre-estimate of that loss and it is not a penalty, and it is stated in this document rather than in a schedule so that a tenderer prices the risk of a late section knowingly. 
A ROOM WITHOUT A BED IS AN UNOCCUPIABLE ROOM. It does not matter that the module cost two hundred times what the bed cost. 
WHERE THE DELAY IS NOT THIS CONTRACTOR'S, an extension of time is granted and damages do not run. The causes are: a change instructed under section 13; an installation window compressed below the stated minimum at 10.1; late issue or late reconciliation of the Common Schedule; a building not ready or not secure to receive delivery under 6.16; and any act of prevention by the Employer or another package. Notification within 10 working days, supported by records kept at the time. 
 
11  Health, safety and working in an occupied village 
11.1  The duties this section does not restate 
The Contractor shall comply with the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974, the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 as they apply to it, the Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992 and every other applicable statutory requirement. Nothing in this document reduces those duties. 
The clauses below are the requirements this package has that a competent furniture contractor's standard arrangements do not automatically cover, because this is not a delivery into an empty office block. 
11.2  Working in a village where people are already asleep 
From month 3 this package installs furniture in a building next to buildings where people are living, and by month 12 it is installing on a site that is almost entirely occupied. 
The site rules at Appendix 3 apply in full, including the noise rule near occupied blocks. Flat-pack assembly is a noisy activity carried out for weeks; the Contractor shall programme it inside permitted hours and shall not assume that an internal activity is exempt because it is indoors. 
DELIVERY VEHICLES follow the routes P1 states at IF-07 and stop where P1 says they stop. No vehicle reverses in a resident pedestrian area without a marshal. 
NOTHING IS STORED IN AN ESCAPE ROUTE, a corridor or a stairwell, at any time, including during a working day. 6.15. 
11.3  Manual handling, which is the real risk in this package 
This package carries several thousand items up stairs, along corridors and into rooms, over a short window, repetitively. That is the injury profile of a furniture installation and it is entirely predictable. 
The 23 kg limit at 6.9 applies to the installation as well as to the operation. The Contractor shall submit a manual handling assessment for the installation covering the stair, the turn, the corridor length and the repetition, and shall state the equipment it will use. 
A method statement that says 'two-person lift where required' without stating which items require it is not an assessment. The masses are known: they are in the Contractor's own Proposals under 6.9. 
11.4  People entering rooms in an occupied village 
In an occupied section, an installer entering a room is entering somebody's home. Entry to any occupied room is under P5's entry procedure, logged, and never unaccompanied by arrangement with P5. 
Every person is inducted, identifiable and recorded before starting work. The conduct standards at section 16 apply to every person this Contractor brings to site, at every tier and including agency labour, and a breach is a removal from site. 
No photography of residents or of the interior of occupied rooms, per the site rules.

THE GRANULARITY IS THE DECISION THAT MATTERS AND THIS DOCUMENT MAKES IT. Every bed, mattress, task chair and item of amenity seating is recorded individually, by room, because those are the items P5 replaces one at a time and it has to know which one it is replacing. Bins, laundry bags and mirrors are recorded by type and quantity per room, because nobody tracks a bin by serial number and pretending otherwise produces a register nobody maintains. 
A register at the wrong granularity is worse than no register, because it is abandoned in month four and then relied on in month thirty. 
12.2  When 
Progressively, section by section, issued within 2 weeks of each sectional acceptance and a precondition of the payment for that section under section 18. Not held to the end and compiled from delivery notes. 
12.3  What P5 actually needs 
P5 replaces, cleans, moves and inspects this furniture for thirty-eight months. It needs, per item type: the identification, the supplier, the replacement lead time, the cleaning method, the fire classification and where the label is, and the warranty position. 
The care instructions at section 9 shall be one page per item type, written for a housekeeper on a shift, not a manufacturer's brochure. A forty-page booklet is not read and the chair is cleaned with the wrong product on day one. 
 
13  Change control 
13.1  The only route by which anything changes 
No change to these Requirements, to the Common Schedule, to a prescribed dimension or to a fire classification takes effect except by written instruction from the Employer's authorised representative. 
No instruction from another package's contractor, from a site meeting, or from P5's operational team has any effect. A request from the Operator is not an instruction, however reasonable it is and however senior the person making it. 
13.2  Valuation 
Every proposed change is valued and its programme effect stated BEFORE it is instructed, using the rates in the priced schedule at section 18, or where no rate applies by a build-up the Contractor submits and the Employer accepts. Quotation within 10 working days of a request. 
A CHANGE AFTER ORDER IS DIFFERENT FROM A CHANGE BEFORE ORDER, and the Contractor shall state, for each line, the date beyond which a change carries a cancellation or restocking cost. Contract furniture is manufactured to order and that date is real. 
13.3  Change originating in another package 
Where P2 changes a fitted item, a dimension or a fixing position, or P3 changes the dining boundary, it is a change to this package and it is instructed by the Employer under 13.1. It is not agreed between contractors. 
Where this Contractor becomes aware that a requirement of another package cannot be met because of something in this one, it shall notify the Employer immediately rather than leave it for that package to find. The allocation of cost for a Common Schedule error is at 4.4. 
 
14  Defects, response and the systemic rule 
14.1  Period 
The defects liability period is 12 months from the acceptance of each section, running separately for each. 
THE WARRANTIES AT 6.2 RUN LONGER and are not extinguished by it: 5 years on frames and structural components, 2 years on moving components and upholstery, from the sectional handover at which the item was installed. 
A SYSTEMIC DEFECT — the same failure in more than 5% of any supplied line — is a defect in the whole line, not in the items that have failed so far. The remedy is replacement of the line, and the Contractor shall not wait for each item to fail before acting on it. That rule exists because 225 of anything fails on a curve, and the Contractor sees the curve before the Employer does. 
14.2  Response, in an occupied village 
PRIORITY 1 — anything that makes a room unusable or unsafe: a bed that has failed structurally, a chair that has collapsed, a storage unit that has become unstable, or damage that presents a sharp or trapping hazard. Attend or replace from attrition stock within 24 hours; the room is not left unusable overnight. 
PRIORITY 2 — an item that is unusable but does not make the room unusable, or a failure in an amenity space that reduces its capacity. Replace within 5 working days. 
PRIORITY 3 — everything else, including cosmetic damage. 20 working days or an agreed programme. 
These are achievable BECAUSE of the attrition stock at 6.12. Priority 1 is met from stock held on site, not from a manufacturing lead time, and that is the whole reason the stock is delivered at the first handover rather than the last. 
14.3  Fair wear, damage and who pays 
Damage caused by a resident is P5's to recover through its own arrangements and is not a defect. Failure of an item in normal use within its warranty period is the Contractor's. 
THE LINE BETWEEN THEM IS DRAWN BY THE SYSTEMIC RULE AT 14.1, not by argument item by item. One broken chair leg in 225 rooms is wear. Twelve broken chair legs in the same line is a chair that is not fit for the use it was bought for, whatever the residents did to it, because the use was stated in this document. 
The Contractor shall attend and inspect on request rather than assess a failure from a photograph. 
 
15  Materials, packaging and end of life 
15.1  The product itself 
The Contractor shall state, for every scheduled line: the principal materials by mass; the recycled content; the proportion by mass that is separable and recyclable at end of life; and the certification of any timber, which shall be FSC or PEFC certified without exception. 
UPHOLSTERY AND FOAM shall be free of added halogenated flame retardants where the required BS 7176 or BS 7177 classification can be achieved without them, and the Contractor shall state where it has and has not achieved this. The fire classification is not negotiable; how it is reached is a legitimate question and this clause asks it. 
The Employer will evaluate a tender partly on these answers, and a tenderer that returns 'not known' on every line is telling the Employer something about its supply chain. 
15.2  Packaging and delivery 
Packaging volume, its removal and its recycling rate reported monthly per 6.15, and a returnable or blanket-wrap delivery method evaluated favourably. 
Delivery consolidation: the Contractor shall state the number of vehicle movements it expects to make and shall consolidate rather than deliver line by line. A site with a 0.42 ha laydown does not have room for thirty part-loads. 
15.3  End of life, which is the point of this package 
This is the most reusable package in the village. A base is broken out and a module is dismantled, but a bed, a chair and a table are removed intact and used again somewhere else, and that is the single largest sustainability decision available here. 
The Contractor shall state, with its tender, its take-back position at the end of the deployment: what it will take back, on what terms, and the residual value it would place on it. That figure is evaluated and it is carried into section 17. 
A tenderer that will not take back what it supplied is telling the Employer what it thinks the furniture will be worth in three years. 
 
16  Labour standards and the place of manufacture 
16.1  Standards that apply to everybody this Contractor brings 
The requirements apply to every person working on this package, including every subcontractor at every tier and every agency worker. 
No person shall pay any fee to obtain or keep work on this project. No person's identity document shall be retained by anybody. Every person shall have a written contract in a language they understand, shall be paid not less than the applicable statutory minimum, directly into an account in their own name, in full and on time. Working hours and rest periods to the Working Time Regulations 1998. 
The Modern Slavery Act 2015 applies and compliance is evidenced, not asserted. 
16.2  The place of manufacture, which is where the real risk in this package is 
This is the package most likely to be manufactured outside the United Kingdom, and furniture manufacturing is a sector with a documented history of forced labour, unsafe conditions and undeclared subcontracting. 
THE CONTRACTOR SHALL DISCLOSE, WITH ITS TENDER, THE PLACE OF MANUFACTURE OF EVERY SCHEDULED LINE — the country and the site, not the country alone, and not the address of a trading company. Where a line is subcontracted onward, that is disclosed too. 
The Employer reserves the right to audit any place of manufacture, at any tier, without notice, itself or through a third party, and to interview workers privately. A refusal is a breach. 
Undisclosed subcontracting of a manufacturing operation is a material breach in its own right, whatever the conditions at the undisclosed site turn out to be, because the undisclosed site is the one nobody audits. 
COTTON AND TEXTILES. Where a line contains cotton or textile components the Contractor shall state the origin of the fibre and shall evidence it. This is a live and specific risk in soft furnishings and it is not discharged by a supplier's self-declaration. 
16.3  Social value 
The social value framework at P2 Appendix 12 applies unamended, reported quarterly. 
This package has a smaller footprint than the others and one real opportunity: the installation labour is local, short-term and repetitive, and it is exactly the kind of work that goes to agencies by default. The Employer will evaluate a proposal to employ and train that labour directly. 
 
17  Removal and reuse 
17.1  What happens to it in thirty-eight months 
At the end of the deployment the village is removed and, if the Employer elects, relocated. This package's contents are the part with a genuine second life. 
THE CONTRACTOR SHALL STATE, WITH ITS TENDER, three things, and they are evaluated: 
Which lines it expects to be reusable at the end of the deployment, and in what condition. 
Which lines are consumed by the deployment and will not be — the mattresses being the obvious case, for the reasons at 6.5. 
Its take-back terms and the residual value it would place on the reusable lines at month 38. 
An honest answer here is worth more than an optimistic one. A tenderer claiming every chair will be as good as new after thirty-eight months and 225 residents is either not telling the truth or has not done this before. 
17.2  Removal 
Removal is from occupied and recently vacated rooms in a live village, under the same regime as installation: the access constraints at 6.8, the manual handling limits at 6.9, and P5's entry procedure. 
Items for reuse are removed, cleaned, inspected, inventoried and packed to a stated standard. Items for disposal are segregated by stream and reported under section 15. Mattresses are disposed of under the withdrawal criteria at 6.5 and their disposal route is stated. 
Nothing is left in a room, a store or a container for somebody else to deal with. The inventory issued at handover is the list that is reconciled at removal, item by item, which is the other reason the granularity at 12.1 matters. 
 
18  Pricing 
18.1  How this is priced 
The tender is priced against the schedule below in its issued structure. A tenderer who alters it produces a return that cannot be compared with the others and it will be marked accordingly. 
RATES ARE PER ROOM OR PER ITEM, DELIVERED, ASSEMBLED, IN POSITION AND ACCEPTED. They include delivery, storage, assembly, installation, packaging removal, the room acceptance check, the inventory, overheads and profit. A rate that covers supply only, with installation elsewhere, is not comparable with the others and will not be normalised for the tenderer. 
A rate left blank is a rate of nil and the item is deemed included elsewhere; a tenderer intending to exclude something lists it as a departure under 6.1 rather than leaving a cell empty. 
QUANTITIES ARE FIRM: 225 rooms — 199 RT-01, 12 RT-02, 14 RT-03 — and the areas at section 3. They are the Employer's and they are not remeasured. 
18.2  What is priced separately, and why 
Four items are pulled out of the room rate because folding them in makes them invisible, and an invisible obligation is one that is performed badly or not at all. 
THE SAMPLE ROOMS at 6.13 — three fully furnished rooms, on site, before any bulk delivery. It is a real cost incurred months early and it is the single most valuable quality control event in this package. 
THE ATTRITION STOCK at 6.12 — because it is delivered at the first handover and must be visible as a quantity, not absorbed into a rate where it quietly shrinks. 
THE RT-02 LAYOUT AND VERIFICATION at 6.6 — twelve rooms measured individually with the furniture in place, and the record issued. It is an hour a room and it is the difference between twelve compliant rooms and twelve that are not. 
THE PACKAGING REMOVAL at 6.15 — because it is a daily logistics operation for the length of the installation, not a line in the preliminaries. 
18.3  Payment 
Payment is by application at each sectional acceptance, valued against this schedule, and conditional on: every room in that section having passed the room acceptance check at 6.13; the inventory issued and acknowledged by P5; and the asset data for that section issued within 2 weeks per 12.2. 
NO PAYMENT IS MADE FOR FURNITURE DELIVERED BUT NOT INSTALLED AND ACCEPTED. Material on site is not progress in this package: a room full of boxes is a room that cannot be occupied. 
Retention is released against sectional acceptance and at the end of the defects period for each section.
