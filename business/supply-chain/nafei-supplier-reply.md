Being on our register means that when a client's temporary estate is being

So: send us no bank details, no payment terms and no framework for signature.
We will not ask for them, and if anyone appearing to write on our behalf does,
it is not us.

**3. We are narrowing your register category, and the reason is scope, not
quality.** You were recorded as "Modular-building suppliers; Camp and
accommodation suppliers". Only the second half is within what ETABLIX
specifies.

Our scope is the temporary site environment around the permanent works —
workforce accommodation, welfare, canteen and drying facilities, site offices,
security and waste — from first mobilisation to final reinstatement. Permanent
modular buildings, which is most of what you describe (townhouses, apartments,
hotels, schools), sit in the main contract and are specified by the client's
designers and bought by their main contractor. We are not in that chain and
would do you no good pretending otherwise.

Campsite and workforce accommodation is the line where you are relevant to us,
and that is what your entry will say.

**4. Certification is the precondition for being specified, not a step after
an order.** You wrote that you can support the certification process for the
destination market once cooperation is confirmed. I understand why it is
offered in that order, but it does not work in the United Kingdom, and this is
the single thing most likely to stop you being specified.

A specification is written, priced and tendered **before** any order exists.
A UK client's building control body and their insurer will ask for the evidence
at that point. If the evidence can only be produced after an order, the product
cannot go into the specification, so the order never arises.

What we would need to see, with the evidence attached rather than offered:

- **UKCA marking and a Declaration of Performance** under the Construction
  Products Regulations 2013, against the designated standard that applies to
  the unit.
- **BS EN 1090-1 factory production control**, certified by a body recognised
  in the UK, with the declared execution class for the structural frame.
- **Reaction-to-fire classification to BS EN 13501-1** for the external
  envelope, internal linings and insulation, with test reports from an
  accredited laboratory. For accommodation that people sleep in this is the
  one that gets refused, and a report against a different national standard
  will not be accepted in its place.
- **Fire detection to BS 5839** for sleeping accommodation, with the
  configuration stated.
- **Electrical installation evidence to BS 7671**, and where the units sit on
  a construction site distribution system, compatibility with **BS 7375**.
- **Thermal performance** against Approved Document L for any unit occupied
  through a British winter, with U-values declared rather than described.

And one more, because you describe yourself as cooperating with manufacturing
bases rather than owning one: **name the factory.** The factory production
control certificate belongs to the manufacturer, not to the trading company,
and a client's building control body will ask whose it is. A specification that
names a trader and cannot name the plant is a specification that gets queried
at the first technical review.

## Why the letter says what it says

**Nothing is promised and no price appears.** The one thing a supplier in this
position wants is a forecast, and we do not have one to give — the demand
belongs to clients' projects.

**The payment correction is the whole point of writing.** They expected to
discuss payment details with us. Had that conversation happened it would have
breached two of the six standing prohibitions — ETABLIX holding the supplier
contract and holding the client's money — and the professional indemnity policy
insures a consultant and would not have answered it. Saying it in writing, now,
also closes the fraud route: a supplier told plainly that we never ask for bank
details will recognise anyone who does.

**The category narrowing is honest rather than generous.** Keeping them
registered for permanent modular buildings would cost nothing and mean nothing,
and the first time a client asked we would have to explain that we do not
specify that work.

**The certification sequence is the senior point.** "We can certify once
cooperation is confirmed" is a reasonable offer in a market where the buyer
orders first. In the UK the evidence is a precondition of the specification,
and reversing it is why imported modular accommodation most often fails to get
specified. Naming the standards rather than saying "UK certification" is what
tells them we have asked these questions before.

**The factory is named or the entry is worth little.** They are a trading
company cooperating with manufacturing bases. Factory production control
attaches to the plant. This is the question a client's building control body
asks first and a trader's register entry most often cannot answer.
