Nobody argues about a skip. A skip is forty pounds and a phone call, and on a project worth tens of millions it does not register. That is exactly why it is a problem.

The operating lines on a site establishment share three properties: each one is individually too small to challenge, each one recurs every week for the whole programme, and none of them has an owner. Multiply a small number by seventy-eight weeks and it stops being small. Do that across nine lines at once and you have the largest uncontrolled cost on the compound.

The lines

  • Waste. Collection, disposal, and the difference between a clean stream and a contaminated one.
  • Cleaning. Welfare, offices, drying rooms. Daily on an occupied compound, and the first thing an inspector looks at.
  • Consumables. Everything the cleaning uses and everything the welfare consumes.
  • Servicing. Emptying and refilling anything not on mains drainage or mains water.
  • Fuel and power. Generator fuel, heating, and the standing charge on a temporary supply.
  • Water and connectivity. Both metered, both easy to oversize at the start and never revisit.
  • Security. Including any manned cover, which is the one line that is not small.
  • Hire. Every cabin, every fence run, every item of plant that is still on site.
  • Transport and logistics. Deliveries, plant movements, and the routes between them.

Idle hire, which is the one that compounds

Hire starts on delivery and ends on collection. Collection requires somebody to notice a unit is finished with, raise the off-hire, and then confirm the collection actually happened — three separate acts, the last of which is the one most often skipped.

On a live site the person who would notice is the person building the permanent works. A cabin at the far end of a compound is invisible to them, and it stays invisible until an invoice grows large enough for a commercial manager to query it. On a long programme that can be months. We have never yet surveyed an established compound and found nothing on hire that nobody was using.

The fix is unglamorous: a register of what is on site against what was ordered, a named owner for off-hire, and a weekly check on anything that accrues daily. Not sophisticated. Simply work that nobody has been given.

Waste is a duty, not a service

The {{source:wasteDutyOfCare|waste duty of care code of practice}} applies to anyone who produces, carries, keeps or disposes of waste. It is not discharged by handing a skip to somebody with a van: you have to check they are authorised, describe the waste accurately, and keep the transfer documentation.

The practical failure on a construction site is rarely the paperwork. It is a subcontractor filling a skip that was ordered for a different stream, which turns a clean load into a mixed one and changes both the cost and the record. That happens because the skip is nearest, and it keeps happening until somebody owns the arrangement rather than the order.

Why these lines have no owner

Because they fall between two jobs. The quantity surveyor prices packages, and these are not packages. The site manager runs the works, and these are not the works. {{source:cdm2015|CDM 2015}} puts duties on the people managing construction work, and a duty to provide and maintain welfare throughout the construction phase plainly covers keeping it clean and serviced — but a duty is not a cost code, and the person discharging it is rarely the person who sees the invoice.

So the arrangement is made correctly at the start by somebody competent, and then nobody watches it for eighteen months.

What we do about it

This is one of the places where the {{link:models|management integrator model}} earns its fee without touching a contract. Every supplier stays contracted to the client and paid by the client. What changes is that each of the lines above has a baseline, a frequency, an expected quantity and a name against it before mobilisation, and somebody reconciles the actual against the expected every week rather than when an invoice looks wrong.

The savings are not dramatic in any one month. They are the difference between a running cost that was decided once and a running cost that was managed for the whole programme, and on a long scheme that difference is the single easiest money on the site.
