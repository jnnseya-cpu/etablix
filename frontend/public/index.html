01The problem we solve

The project rarely fails because every supplier is incompetent.
It fails because nobody owns the complete system.

On any project with a peak workforce above ~100 people, temporary services are typically bought as 10–25 separate packages: cabins, fencing, generators, fuel, water, sewage, ICT, security, cleaning, catering, waste, traffic management, transport, accommodation. Each supplier is individually competent. Nobody owns the interfaces between them — the generator sized without the welfare load, the village opening two weeks after the workforce arrives, three suppliers invoicing for the same standby labour, no common programme, no common cost code, no single performance report.

The consequences are familiar to every Project Director: mobilisation delay, uncontrolled prelims escalation, disputes over scope gaps, and a site team consumed by supplier administration instead of the permanent works. Tier-one clients have already concluded the answer — appoint one accountable contractor — but the supply side has not organised to meet it. There are cabin firms, FM firms and camp caterers; there are very few dedicated lifecycle integrators, and none with an AI-native control platform.

What this looks like on a real site.
